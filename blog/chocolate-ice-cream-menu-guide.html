Chocolate-named choices

A Chocolate Lover's Guide to Sweet Escape's Frozen Desserts

Published September 13, 2026 · Updated September 29, 2026 · Sweet Escape, Memphis

When you are in the mood for chocolate, a dessert menu can give you more choices than you expected. At Sweet Escape in Memphis, chocolate appears as a scoop name, as part of other ice cream names, in frozen yogurt, in gelato, and in specialty desserts. The useful question is which version of a chocolate dessert you want—not whether every item containing the word is the same kind of treat.

This guide separates those decisions. Start with a scoop if you want to explore ice cream, a self-serve cup if you want frozen yogurt, or a specialty if the dessert's other components are part of the attraction. Then read the exact product description rather than using a chocolate name as a shortcut for ingredients or availability.

Chocolate and Udderly Chocolate are separate scoop entries

The scoop catalog documents both Chocolate and Udderly Chocolate as Ice Cream. They have separate nutrition and allergen records. That alone is a reason to keep their full names when browsing: a card for one is not the label for the other.

Chocolate's published record declares milk and includes an equipment-related note naming peanuts, tree nuts, wheat, soy, and eggs. Udderly Chocolate's record declares egg, milk, and soy, with no additional allergen notes provided in that source. “No additional notes provided” is an information limitation, not a claim that cross-contact cannot occur.

The records do not support an invented comparison of cocoa percentages, chocolate intensity, or how many kinds of chocolate each product contains. A playful name is not a recipe. If those details matter to your choice, ask about the current product rather than expecting the name to answer them.

Use the published numbers with their serving information

The Chocolate record lists a 2/3-cup, 95-gram serving with 210 calories, 12 grams of total fat, 21 grams of carbohydrates, and 18 grams of total sugars. Its source is dated April 2019. Udderly Chocolate's owner-provided nutrition record lists 230 calories, 13 grams of total fat, 26 grams of carbohydrates, and 24 grams of total sugars per 2/3 cup; it does not supply a serving weight.

Those figures are reference information, not a nutrition contest. They also do not describe a custom dessert after additions. Keep the source context and serving line with the numbers, and verify current labels if they affect your decision. Do not assume a shop scoop weighs the reference amount or that a missing gram weight can be borrowed from another card.

Other chocolate-named scoops widen the search

The catalog also includes Chocolate Chip, Chocolate Covered Strawberry, Chocolate Malt, Chocolate Peanut Butter, Chocolate Hazelnut, and White Chocolate Raspberry. These names give you several directions to investigate. They do not establish that all of those products are in the current lineup, nor do they provide complete ingredient statements by themselves.

The allergen records illustrate why individual lookup matters. Chocolate Malt declares milk, soy, wheat, and eggs. Chocolate Peanut Butter declares egg, milk, and peanut. Chocolate Hazelnut declares egg, hazelnuts, and milk. A person comparing these entries needs the exact card and its cautions, not a general statement about “chocolate ice cream.”

If you have a particular second flavor in mind, include it in your search. “Chocolate Peanut Butter” is a more focused question than “something chocolate.” Conversely, if you only want to begin with chocolate itself, start with the Chocolate card and avoid turning the whole catalog into a required reading list.

Triple Chocolate frozen yogurt follows a different format

The yogurt catalog lists Triple Chocolate as a Yogurt entry. Its published ingredients include milk, nonfat milk, cocoa processed with alkali, whey, and cream, among the other listed components. Its allergen table declares milk and accompanies that declaration with the manufacturing-facility caution.

The name does not give us a verified count of chocolate types or an exact cocoa percentage, so we do not supply one. The useful information is the documented cocoa-containing yogurt base and the self-serve format available at Sweet Escape. You start with the frozen yogurt and can then visit the topping station.

For a chocolate-focused cup, decide whether the base is the main thing you want or whether you want to explore additions. Specific topping choices should be checked in store. A cup with toppings is a different finished dessert from the base described by its manufacturer label.

Dreamy Dark Chocolate and Chocolate yogurt are not duplicates

Two other yogurt-catalog entries are Dreamy Dark Chocolate and Chocolate. Dreamy Dark Chocolate is categorized as Nonfat and lists cocoa processed with alkali in its ingredient statement. Chocolate appears in the No Sugar Added category and has a different statement that includes maltodextrin, sorbitol, and sucralose.

Both declare milk in their published allergen tables. The category labels therefore should not be read as milk-free claims. Both also retain a facility note about processing peanuts, tree nuts, eggs, soy, milk, and wheat products. These cautions remain relevant even when the flavor name sounds straightforward.

If No Sugar Added is what prompted your search, keep that category attached to the exact product. It is not interchangeable with sugar free, and it does not establish suitability for a medical or dietary need. Our guide to the no-sugar-added entries explains the documented choices in more detail.

White Chocolate Mousse has its own ingredient story

White Chocolate Mousse yogurt is not simply another name for Triple Chocolate. Its published statement includes a white chocolate base containing white chocolate, with components such as milk, cocoa butter, soy lecithin, and vanilla. The product's allergen table marks milk and soy “Yes.”

That ingredient detail is more useful than guessing from the word “mousse.” The catalog describes a frozen yogurt flavor; it is not a promise of a separate plated mousse dessert. Similarly, the presence of “white chocolate” does not let you transfer the label from a different white-chocolate-named scoop.

When two names appeal to you, compare their complete descriptions and category information. You can choose a direction without declaring one universally richer, sweeter, or better. Those comparisons would need evidence beyond the product names.

Chocolate appears in the gelato collection, too

The gelato catalog includes Belgian Dark Chocolate, Coffee Chocolate Chip, and Piemonte Hazelnut Chocolate Crunch. These offer different documented combinations. Coffee Chocolate Chip is described with coffee gelato and dark chocolate chips; Piemonte Hazelnut Chocolate Crunch is described with chocolate and hazelnut gelato, caramel pearls, and a chocolate-hazelnut ribbon.

Read each record's cautions. Belgian Dark Chocolate lists milk and egg, plus possible wheat, tree nuts, peanuts, and soy. Coffee Chocolate Chip lists milk and notes that complete published allergen information was unavailable and chocolate may introduce soy. Piemonte Hazelnut Chocolate Crunch lists milk and hazelnut, with an additional soy caution.

A separate Vegan Chocolate entry appears in the Vegan category. Its description identifies non-dairy gelato, while its note cautions that chocolate may introduce soy and asks visitors to verify the current container label. The category is a research aid, not a complete cross-contact or allergy assurance.

When the chocolate element is part of a specialty

Sometimes what you want is not a chocolate-flavored base but a dessert that includes another chocolate component. Sweet Escape's specialties menu describes Brownie Base with a warm brownie and one ice cream scoop. Dubai Dream is described with two scoops, Dubai chocolate filling, pistachio cream, and crisp kataifi.

Those formats deserve their own consideration. The written descriptions tell you which components define the dessert; they do not establish every possible substitution. If you want a particular scoop in a specialty, ask about current choices. A nutrition record for the scoop alone does not account for the brownie, filling, or other additions.

Our warm-base comparison is a better next page if the brownie format is the main attraction. It keeps that decision separate from choosing among chocolate frozen-dessert flavors.

Make the availability check specific

When this guide was refreshed on September 29, the live stock file showed a September 25 timestamp and listed Chocolate and Udderly Chocolate among scoops, plus Triple Chocolate and White Chocolate Mousse among yogurts. That dated list is not a permanent menu promise.

Use the live menu for the latest reported lineup, checking the category as well as the name. If the item you want is absent, do not infer that a similar name is an identical replacement. Choose another documented option to investigate or call before making a special trip.

Choose the chocolate dessert you actually mean

A scoop, a yogurt cup, gelato, and a brownie-based specialty can all fit a chocolate-focused visit, but they are different choices. Pick the format that interests you, read the specific product information, and bring any unresolved ingredient questions to the shop.
