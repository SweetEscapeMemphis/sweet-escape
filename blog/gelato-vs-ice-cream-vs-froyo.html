Gelato vs. Ice Cream vs. Frozen Yogurt: What's the Difference?

Published August 28, 2026 · Updated September 29, 2026 · Sweet Escape, Memphis

Ice cream, gelato, and frozen yogurt can all answer the same question: what sounds good for dessert? They do not answer it in exactly the same way. At Sweet Escape in Memphis, they have separate menus, individual product information, and different ways to become your next treat.

The most useful comparison is not a contest with one winner. It is a way to choose the experience you want. Hand-dipped ice cream keeps a scoop at the center of the decision. Self-serve frozen yogurt gives you a swirl-and-toppings format. Gelato and sorbetto introduce a separate collection of products to explore. Here is how to compare them without relying on broad claims that may not fit a specific flavor.

The quick distinction at Sweet Escape

Start with the service format. Our ice cream menu is hand-dipped; our frozen yogurt specialty is self-serve, followed by a topping station. Gelato and sorbetto have their own catalog, including separate product categories. Those distinctions tell you where to begin browsing even before you choose a flavor.

A specialty dessert adds another layer to the decision. Cookie Dough Base and Brownie Base each include one ice cream scoop, while Ice Cream Nachos are described as two scoops with waffle chips. Choosing a category of frozen dessert is therefore different from choosing an assembled treat. The specialties menu explains those formats without requiring you to infer them from a photograph.

What makes gelato a different starting point?

Villa Dolce, the supplier documented in our gelato catalog, describes gelato as Italian-style ice cream. Its product-attributes page identifies low air incorporation as a feature of its gelato. That manufacturer context helps explain why gelato is discussed as a distinct style rather than merely a flavor name.

It does not establish an exact comparison between every gelato and every ice cream we list. Individual recipes and serving amounts still matter. Nor does the style tell you that one choice will automatically have fewer calories, less sugar, or a particular ingredient profile. Our gelato catalog is the better place to move from the general idea to a specific product.

For example, its Birthday Cake entry describes a vanilla cake gelato with sprinkles and cake pieces. Coffee Chocolate Chip is described with coffee and dark chocolate chips. These descriptions identify what distinguishes the documented flavors; their presence in the catalog does not establish today's availability. Choose the entry that interests you, then check the live menu.

Where hand-dipped ice cream fits

The scoop catalog covers a wide set of named products, including familiar directions such as Chocolate, French Vanilla, Cookie Dough, and Oregon Strawberry. It also includes related categories, so the category on the individual card matters. A shared catalog location does not turn sherbet into ice cream.

Hand-dipped ice cream is a useful starting point when you want to focus on a particular scoop flavor or choose an ice cream component for a listed specialty. Think of those as two different decisions: which flavor interests you, and whether you want it on its own or as part of a dessert whose published format includes ice cream.

Do not assume that a familiar flavor name tells you the complete recipe. The scoop records include product-specific allergen fields and nutrition serving information. French Vanilla and Vanilla Bean, for example, have separate records. That distinction is worth keeping even if both fall within the flavor family you came looking for.

What changes when you choose frozen yogurt?

At Sweet Escape, the defining visitor-facing feature is the self-serve format. The specialty description invites you to swirl frozen yogurt and then finish it at the topping station. Instead of selecting only a named scoop, you are deciding how you want to build the cup.

The yogurt catalog provides a closer look at the individual bases. French Vanilla yogurt lists milk, nonfat milk, cream, and yogurt cultures among its ingredients. Cookie Monster lists a cookie base; Cookies 'N Cream lists cookie pieces. These are concrete recipe differences, not just alternative names for one underlying product.

Frozen yogurt also should not be treated as a universal nutrition claim. The catalog includes different categories and individual ingredient statements. Picking yogurt rather than ice cream does not, by itself, establish the nutrition of a finished cup. Serving amount and additions still affect what you are comparing.

If making those finishing choices is the part you enjoy, that is a practical reason to explore froyo. You do not need to turn the choice into a health ranking or assume every cup should contain several toppings. Start with the base you want and decide how much of the dessert you want the extras to define.

Where sorbet and sorbetto belong in the comparison

You will encounter fruit-oriented names in more than one menu section. Watermelon Sorbet appears in the yogurt catalog as a Sorbet entry. Alphonso Mango appears in the gelato catalog as Sorbetto. Their placement tells you which product record to open; it does not make either one a yogurt simply because you reached it through a particular page.

Watermelon Sorbet's published statement includes water, liquid sugar, watermelon flavor syrup, and other ingredients, along with a facility caution. Alphonso Mango's record notes that a complete major-allergen statement was not published online and asks visitors to verify the current container label. These examples show why checking the exact entry is more useful than applying one assumption to every fruit-named dessert.

If sorbetto is the category you want to explore further, the sorbetto guide keeps that question separate from the broader decision between ice cream, gelato, and froyo.

Compare like with like when a name appears twice

Birthday Cake is a good example of a name that crosses categories. The yogurt entry is categorized as Nonfat and describes vanilla cake flavor. The gelato entry describes a product with sprinkles and cake pieces. You would miss that difference if you stopped reading after the two-word name.

Try a three-part comparison: full product name, category, then ingredients or description. Keep those parts together when you bookmark a choice or ask about it. “Birthday Cake gelato” is a more precise starting point than “the birthday cake one,” especially when you are researching before a visit.

The same method works for chocolate, vanilla, fruit, and cookie themes. It lets you follow a flavor you like across menus while recognizing that you are comparing separate products. A similar theme can lead you toward another option, but it does not promise identical taste, ingredients, nutrition, or availability.

A better way to narrow your options

Ask which feature matters most to you today. If it is the hands-on choice of a base and toppings, begin with frozen yogurt. If it is a particular scoop or a cookie-based specialty, begin with the ice cream and specialties pages. If it is exploring the gelato collection, start there without feeling you need to compare every other menu first.

Next, choose one product to investigate and one alternative. Keep the comparison focused on an actual decision rather than collecting a dozen possibilities. Our two-choice shortlist guide is designed for that stage. It is especially useful when a favorite name has led you into more than one category.

Check product details without turning them into promises

Nutrition comparisons need matching serving information. A number for one manufacturer's serving is not automatically comparable to a number for a different amount. Likewise, information about a base alone does not describe the complete dessert after cookies, a brownie, waffle chips, or toppings are added.

For ingredient or allergy questions, read the declared allergens and any facility or possible-allergen cautions. A blank field, a “No” in a status table, or a category such as vegan is not a guarantee about cross-contact. Verify the current product labels and discuss relevant handling questions before ordering; the website alone cannot establish suitability.
