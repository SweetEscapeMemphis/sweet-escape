Cherry Amaretto vs. Amaretto Cherry: Frozen Yogurt and Ice Cream in Memphis

Published October 1, 2026 · Sweet Escape, Memphis

Two Sweet Escape catalog names put cherry and amaretto side by side: Cherry Amaretto and Amaretto Cherry. They sound like variations on a single flavor, but the website lists them as different products in different categories. One is a nonfat frozen yogurt whose product description specifically mentions sweet cherry and warm almond notes. The other is an ice cream entry with its own nutrition and allergen record. That distinction is the useful starting point if you are browsing the Memphis menu and deciding which details matter to you.

This guide compares only what Sweet Escape’s catalog and linked product information actually publish. It does not assume the two recipes are alike, assign extra taste notes to the ice cream, or treat a product name as a full ingredient statement. Availability changes separately from catalog descriptions, so check the posted in-stock menu before choosing a visit-day favorite.

What Sweet Escape publishes for Cherry Amaretto frozen yogurt

The yogurt page identifies Cherry Amaretto as a nonfat frozen yogurt. Its description says the flavor pairs sweet cherry with the warm almond notes of amaretto. That gives you a little more to go on than the name alone: the published note is about the flavor association, not a promise that the product contains pieces of cherry or almonds. The ingredient list names a Cherry Amaretto flavor component, sugar, water, natural and artificial flavor, citric acid, and color ingredients, alongside milk-based ingredients and active yogurt cultures.

For ingredients, the page is the best place to read the full statement in context. It lists nonfat milk, liquid sugar, corn syrup, milk, whey, and several ingredients present at less than two percent. It also lists the cultures S. Thermophilus, L. Bulgaricus, L. Acidophilus, B. Bifidus, and L. Casei. These details describe the published yogurt formula record; they are not a general description of every frozen yogurt, nor do they establish a health benefit.

The page’s allergen information declares milk. Its accompanying facility note says the product is manufactured in a facility that also processes peanuts, tree nuts, eggs, soy, milk, and wheat products. Those statements are not the same thing: the declared allergen line and the facility note answer different questions. If an allergy matters, read the current package information and contact the shop to verify the current container label before ordering.

The yogurt page links to the manufacturer’s Nonfat Yogurts information, where Cherry Amaretto appears among that producer’s nonfat yogurt flavors. The supplier’s page is a useful primary source for the category and its published description. Sweet Escape’s own page remains the practical reference for how the item is presented in the local catalog, its product cautions, and the shop’s posted menu information.

What the Amaretto Cherry ice cream record says

The scoop catalog lists Amaretto Cherry as an ice cream flavor. Its current product detail page is intentionally more limited in prose: it identifies the category and links to the published nutrition facts. It does not offer the same cherry-and-almond flavor sentence as the yogurt page. A careful comparison should preserve that difference instead of assuming that the ice cream tastes exactly like the yogurt or contains the same flavoring ingredients.

The nutrition record gives a serving size of two-thirds of a cup, or 95 grams. For that listed serving, it reports 240 calories, 13 grams of total fat, 9 grams of saturated fat, 27 grams of carbohydrates, 22 grams of total sugars, and 3 grams of protein. It also reports 19 grams of added sugars, 40 milligrams of sodium, and 45 milligrams of cholesterol. The record’s source date is June 12, 2019, so these are the figures shown in the published record, not a guarantee that a current container label has not changed.

For allergens, the scoop record says the product contains milk and soy. Its shared-equipment note lists peanuts, tree nuts, wheat, and eggs. The item page links to the corresponding entry in Sweet Escape’s nutrition facts PDF. If a dietary need depends on an exact ingredient or current manufacturing information, treat the online record as a starting point and ask the shop to check the package currently being served.

How to choose without guessing

Start with the format you want to explore: the yogurt entry or the ice cream entry. If the description “sweet cherry” with “warm almond notes” is important to your decision, that wording belongs to the Cherry Amaretto yogurt record. If you are comparing the hand-dipped scoop, use its own product page and nutrition label; do not transfer the yogurt’s description to it. The names make them easy to confuse, but the category labels and product links keep the two choices distinct.

Next, decide which published details you need. Someone comparing ingredients should open the yogurt page’s ingredient section and the scoop page’s nutrition-facts link, recognizing that they do not provide identical levels of detail. Someone comparing allergen information should read both cautions independently. A nutrition comparison is possible only where the same type of data is available for both items and the serving basis is clear. The scoop record has a serving size and nutrition values; the yogurt page links to its own label. Avoid comparing the scoop’s figures to an assumed yogurt serving or treating “nonfat” as shorthand for a broader nutritional conclusion.

Then check availability. Catalog pages describe documented products; they do not promise either flavor will be available on a particular day. Use the posted stock list for the latest availability information. Questions visitors often have

Are Cherry Amaretto and Amaretto Cherry the same flavor?

They are separate catalog records: Cherry Amaretto is frozen yogurt and Amaretto Cherry is ice cream. Their names are similar, but their published descriptions and product information are not identical.

Does Cherry Amaretto contain almonds?

The yogurt ingredient statement mentions almond notes in the description, but the listed ingredients do not name almonds; its allergen information declares milk and carries the facility note shown on the product page. Do not use that summary to make an allergy decision. Check the current label and ask the shop to verify the container and handling details.

Can I compare their nutrition numbers directly?

Only if you use the actual current label for each product and compare equivalent serving sizes. The scoop page publishes figures for a 95-gram serving; the yogurt page links to a separate manufacturer label. Don’t assume the serving bases match.

Are these flavors available today?

The catalog confirms that the products are documented, not that they are on the counter today. Check the posted stock list, and call before making a special trip if either flavor is a must-have.
