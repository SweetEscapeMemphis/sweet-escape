Reading the vegan label

How to Find Sweet Escape's Vegan Gelato Choices in Memphis

Published September 9, 2026 · Updated September 30, 2026 · Sweet Escape, Memphis

Searching for vegan gelato in Memphis can mean more than one thing. You may want to browse flavors marked Vegan, find out which choices are listed today, or confirm whether a product meets a particular ingredient or handling requirement. Sweet Escape’s website helps with each step, but the answers live in different places. The catalog describes documented products, the live menu reports current availability, and the shop can check current container labels when an ingredient or dietary concern matters.

This guide shows how to use those sources together. It focuses on two entries that Sweet Escape’s published catalog files under Vegan—Vegan Vanilla and Vegan Chocolate—without implying that either is currently in stock or that a category label replaces an ingredient check. The goal is to make your next menu decision clear, specific, and based on the product information that actually applies.

What the Vegan label tells you on this menu

Sweet Escape’s gelato directory includes category filters for Gelato, Sorbetto, and Vegan. In that directory, “Vegan” is a navigation category attached to particular product records. It gives you a focused place to start browsing. It is not the same as a complete ingredient statement, an allergen certification, or a promise about handling in the shop.

The source linked in Sweet Escape’s product data, Villa Dolce’s flavor directory, also presents Vegan as a product category and has individual pages for Vegan Vanilla and Vegan Chocolate. Sweet Escape’s own cards repeat selected product descriptions and add label-check cautions. Use the shop’s card as your local menu record, then ask Sweet Escape directly when you need current packaging or handling information.

Meet the two documented catalog entries

Vegan Vanilla is described by Villa Dolce as a non-dairy, vegan gelato featuring imported Madagascar vanilla bean. Sweet Escape’s catalog repeats that description and includes a note that a complete major-allergen statement is not published online for this entry. If you are interested in vanilla, open the exact Vegan Vanilla product card rather than relying on a general vanilla category or an image.

Vegan Chocolate is described by Villa Dolce as a dairy-free gelato featuring imported dark chocolate. Sweet Escape’s product note adds that chocolate may introduce soy and asks guests to verify the current container label. That additional caution illustrates why two items sharing a menu category still need to be checked one at a time. A label for Vegan Vanilla does not answer questions about Vegan Chocolate, and the reverse is also true.

These descriptions help you identify the entries and understand how the supplier characterizes them. They do not state that either flavor is available on a particular day. Nor should they be expanded into unlisted ingredient, manufacturing, or medical claims. For the full current information, use the exact catalog card and confirm essential details with the shop.

Catalog discovery and today's menu are different tasks

The gelato and sorbetto catalog is a directory of documented product information. Its Vegan filter makes it easier to compare the entries filed under that category. The live in-stock menu answers the separate question of what the shop currently reports. Open the live menu’s Gelato tab, check its update time, and search for the exact name you want.

A catalog listing is not a live availability signal. A flavor can have a useful product card even when the live menu does not currently report it. And a list you viewed earlier can be out of date by the time you travel. If Vegan Vanilla or Vegan Chocolate is essential to your plan, check the current page close to your visit and call the shop to confirm. Do not use this article or a dated screenshot as a same-day stock report.

It helps to decide what you need to know before you browse. If you are just exploring, the catalog filter is a convenient first stop. If you are planning to order a specific flavor, the live list comes next. If you have an allergy or another strict requirement, the exact card and current container label are the relevant sources. Separating those jobs makes it easier to find the right answer without mistaking one kind of information for another.

Why “vegan” does not answer every allergy question

“Vegan” and “allergen-safe” are not interchangeable claims. The term on a product page describes how the manufacturer or shop categorizes that product; it does not by itself confirm every ingredient, possible trace, shared equipment, or in-store handling detail. Sweet Escape’s records specifically note that a complete major-allergen statement is not published online for Vegan Vanilla and that soy may be introduced by the chocolate component in Vegan Chocolate. Those cautions are important parts of the records—not footnotes to ignore.

Likewise, a blank allergen array or an empty field should not be interpreted as a guarantee that no allergen is present. The information available online may not cover every ingredient or every operational detail. Labels can change, and ingredients in a component such as chocolate may have details that are not obvious from the flavor name. Read the full card and note any explicit instruction to verify the current container label.

If a food allergy or dietary need matters, ask the shop to review the label for the exact product being served. Explain the specific ingredient or handling question; “is it vegan?” may not address a concern about soy, other allergens, or contact with shared equipment. Sweet Escape notes that in-store cross-contact is possible, so an online description cannot certify that a product is free from an allergen. If the information remains incomplete or uncertain, do not treat the category filter as a substitute for a verified answer.

For additional context, the nutrition and allergen guide explains how Sweet Escape’s product records present declared allergens and other cautions. It is a reading aid, not a medical assessment or a safety certification. The current label and a direct conversation with the shop are the right next steps when an ingredient or handling detail affects your decision.

Compare the two flavors by their published descriptions

If both entries interest you, compare only what their records actually say. Vegan Vanilla highlights Madagascar vanilla bean. Vegan Chocolate highlights imported dark chocolate, and its current catalog note specifically says chocolate may introduce soy. That is enough to help you identify which card to open and which follow-up question to ask. It is not a basis for making broader claims about which product is safer, healthier, lower in sugar, or suitable for a particular person.

Use the same careful approach when comparing a Vegan entry with a Sorbetto or another Gelato item. Different category names do not provide a complete side-by-side ingredient list. Open each exact card and compare the descriptions and disclosed cautions. If one entry omits a detail you need, note that omission and ask the shop rather than borrowing information from a similar flavor or from the manufacturer’s general category page.

A straightforward way to check before visiting

  1. Open the gelato catalog and select the Vegan filter to find the documented Vegan Vanilla and Vegan Chocolate entries.
  2. Read the exact flavor card, including the ingredient and label-check cautions.
  3. Open the live menu, select Gelato, and check the update time for current reported availability.
  4. If a flavor is essential to your visit, contact the shop to confirm it is currently listed.

This is deliberately a short process. It keeps catalog browsing from turning into a promise about stock, and it keeps a vegan category from being mistaken for an all-purpose allergy statement. If you do not have a specific flavor in mind yet, browse first and make a short list of possibilities. Then check the live menu and ask only the questions that affect your decision.
