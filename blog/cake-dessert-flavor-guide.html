Cake and Cheesecake Flavors: A Guide to Sweet Escape's Catalogs

Published September 15, 2026 · Updated September 29, 2026 · Sweet Escape, Memphis

Sometimes you want the idea of cake in a frozen dessert rather than a slice on a plate. Sweet Escape's catalogs include cake-, batter-, and cheesecake-named choices across ice cream, frozen yogurt, and gelato. The names are inviting, but they describe separate products with different records—not a single recipe offered in several forms.

This Memphis menu guide helps you follow the dessert-inspired names without confusing a flavor with a finished cake or a specialty. It is an evergreen catalog guide refreshed in September, not a promise that all the examples are currently available. Start with the kind of frozen dessert you want, then look at what the exact entry says.

A cake flavor is not an ice cream cake order

Anniversary Cake, Birthday Cake, and Cake Batter are names in the product catalogs. Their presence does not establish that Sweet Escape offers custom cakes, birthday packages, decorations, or a cake-ordering service. A flavor can have a celebratory name without describing an event service.

That distinction makes your search more useful. If you want a cake-inspired frozen dessert, the flavor cards are the right place to start. If you are arranging a celebration with specific requirements, contact the shop about those requirements instead of inferring them from a flavor name. Our group dessert-stop guide separates menu browsing from logistics that need confirmation.

You also do not need a special occasion to explore a cake-named flavor. Treat the name as an invitation to read the product description, not a rule about when you should order it.

Anniversary Cake and other scoop-catalog names

The scoop catalog lists Anniversary Cake as Ice Cream. Its published allergen record declares milk, soy, eggs, and wheat, with peanuts and tree nuts in the additional equipment-related note. Those details belong to that exact entry and should not be carried over to another cake-named product.

Carrot Cake is a separate Ice Cream entry. It declares milk, wheat, and soy, while its additional note names peanuts, tree nuts, and eggs. Yellow Cake Batter has another declaration again: milk, peanuts, soy, wheat, and eggs, with tree nuts in the additional note.

The available scoop records do not provide full ingredient narratives for these items. We therefore do not invent a frosting type, a piece size, a nut mixture, or a manufacturing method from the names. The current product label is the place to verify details that the online record does not contain.

Cheesecake-named scoops are also distinct

Several scoop entries use cheesecake in their names: Lemon Cheesecake Bar, O-O-Cheesecake, Raspberry Chip Cheesecake, and Strawberry Cheesecake. The fruit or other qualifier is worth retaining when you look them up. “Cheesecake ice cream” alone is too broad to identify the correct product information.

Lemon Cheesecake Bar declares milk, soy, wheat, and eggs. O-O-Cheesecake declares milk, wheat, and soy. Raspberry Chip Cheesecake declares milk and soy. Strawberry Cheesecake declares milk, eggs, and wheat. Each record also has an equipment-related caution that should be read in full rather than reduced to the short declaration here.

These differences are not a safety ranking. They show why two dessert-inspired names cannot share an assumed allergen statement. A product with fewer declared allergens still requires attention to the current label and relevant handling questions.

Birthday Cake frozen yogurt starts with a different record

In the yogurt catalog, Birthday Cake is categorized as Nonfat and described as a vanilla cake flavor. Its published ingredient statement includes nonfat milk, liquid sugar, corn syrup, whey, milk, and other components, including natural and artificial flavors and yogurt cultures.

The allergen table declares milk. The Nonfat label should therefore not be confused with a milk-free statement. The record also includes a manufacturing-facility caution covering peanuts, tree nuts, eggs, soy, milk, and wheat products. That note remains part of the information even when a particular status-table row says “No.”

At Sweet Escape, frozen yogurt belongs to the self-serve cup format, with a topping station available to finish the dessert. The Birthday Cake product statement describes the base itself. It does not describe every possible topping combination or establish a complete nutrition total for a finished cup.

Cake Batter yogurt is not Birthday Cake yogurt

Cake Batter appears in the Yogurt category as its own entry. Its description points toward a buttery cake-batter flavor, while the ingredient statement lists milk, nonfat milk, whey, cream, a named cake flavor component, and other ingredients. The record declares milk and retains the same published facility caution.

The important distinction is that Birthday Cake and Cake Batter are separately documented products. Similar themes do not mean the descriptions, nutrition labels, or complete ingredient statements are interchangeable. If both names interest you, open both cards instead of choosing whichever search result appears first.

Nor does the word batter establish that the finished yogurt contains a portion of raw cake batter. Use the published ingredient statement for what is actually documented. A flavor name can evoke another dessert without supplying a literal recipe for it.

Cheesecake yogurt has a named base component

Cheesecake yogurt is categorized as Nonfat. Its ingredient statement includes a cheesecake base with components such as butter and buttermilk powder, alongside the yogurt's other ingredients. The record declares milk. This gives you a concrete detail to investigate beyond the short cheesecake name.

It does not establish that crust pieces are included. In contrast, a different catalog entry may explicitly describe a crust component. The distinction is simple but useful: a cheesecake flavor and a product described with cheesecake-style additions do not necessarily offer the same structure.

If you want fruit to be part of your search as well, our fruit-flavor guide follows the fruit-named options across categories. Keep those product records separate from Cheesecake yogurt rather than assuming a fruit-named cheesecake scoop is the same base with an added fruit.

Birthday Cake gelato explicitly describes pieces

The gelato catalog has a Birthday Cake entry described as vanilla cake gelato with sprinkles and cake pieces. That description distinguishes it from the yogurt entry, whose name is similar but whose record is different. Keeping “gelato” in the full name helps you stay with the intended product.

The gelato record lists milk and cautions that cake and sprinkle components may introduce egg, wheat, or soy. It asks visitors to verify the current container label. Do not replace that caution with the yogurt's allergen table or assume the two Birthday Cake products share a formula.

This is a useful place to ask what matters to you about a cake-inspired dessert. If the documented sprinkles and pieces are the attraction, read the gelato card. If you want a self-serve base, investigate the yogurt card. The format and description give you a more meaningful comparison than the shared name.

NY Strawberry Cheesecake gelato adds another combination

NY Strawberry Cheesecake is described as cheesecake gelato with strawberry swirls and graham-crust pieces. Its published record lists milk and notes that crust components may introduce wheat or soy. The full current label should be verified when those details matter.

The written description lets you identify three parts of the documented combination: cheesecake gelato, strawberry swirls, and crust pieces. It does not establish exact proportions, a complete ingredient list, or the ability to remove a component. Ask about the product rather than assuming it can be assembled differently because its description names several elements.

Likewise, this gelato is not the same record as Strawberry Cheesecake ice cream. Both can be relevant to a cheesecake-themed search while remaining separate products with separate information.

Distinguish frozen flavors from baked-base specialties

Sweet Escape also documents Cookie Dough Base and Brownie Base on the specialties menu. These are composed desserts: a baked chocolate-chip cookie dough base or a warm brownie base, each with one ice cream scoop. They are not cake-flavored yogurt or gelato entries.

If the warm baked component is what you want, use the cookie dough and brownie comparison to choose that format first. Then ask about available ice cream choices. A yogurt or gelato catalog listing does not confirm it can be substituted into a specialty described with an ice cream scoop.
