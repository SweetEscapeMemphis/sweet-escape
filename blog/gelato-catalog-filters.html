Find the exact gelato record

How to Use Sweet Escape’s Gelato Catalog

Published September 19, 2026 · Updated September 30, 2026 · Sweet Escape, Memphis

Sweet Escape’s gelato menu includes search, category buttons, and a known-allergen selector. Search looks at a flavor’s name, category, and description; category buttons group gelato, sorbetto, and vegan entries; the allergen selector checks recorded known-allergen values. None alone proves current stock or safety for a particular person.

This guide explains how to use the gelato and sorbetto catalog, what the cards contain, and what to do when a label or stock question remains. It uses specific menu examples to show why reading the exact record matters. The goal is practical browsing based on published details—not a promise about current formulas, availability, or dietary suitability.

Start with the question you need answered

Are you trying to find a named flavor, browse a product group, check a disclosed allergen, or confirm availability today? Those questions lead to different parts of the site. The catalog is a directory of product information. The live in-stock menu reports what the shop currently lists. A dedicated flavor page gives an item-level view. Knowing which source answers which question avoids reading a search result as a full label.

Know a name? Search. Exploring a type? Pick a category. An allergen matters? Use the selector to locate records, then open the full details. If a flavor is essential to your visit, check the stock page and update time separately.

Search uses the name, category, and description

The gelato search checks text from three fields: the flavor name, category, and description. A short product term can therefore find a card by its name or by a descriptive phrase. Searching “mango,” for instance, can find a record whose name or description uses that word. Search is a simple text match, not a question-answer feature, so one or two specific words work better than a full sentence.

The field does not search every ingredient statement, nutrition record, allergen note, or supplier document. A search for a word that appears only in an expandable allergen detail may not reveal every card that contains it. When your question is about a specific ingredient, use the product’s full card and source links. Search is a finder; the complete record is where the relevant fields live.

Search, category, and allergen conditions combine. If no cards remain, clear the search and return category and allergen to All; then add one condition at a time to see which choice narrowed the list.

Browse Gelato, Sorbetto, and Vegan separately

The category buttons are All, Gelato, Sorbetto, and Vegan. Choosing one narrows the current catalog records to that assigned category. All returns the full gelato directory. The category is a helpful browsing label, not a replacement for the exact product name or its description. Read the card after selecting a category, especially when the distinction affects what you want to compare.

Alphonso Mango is one example of a record categorized as Sorbetto. Its description names Alphonso mango from India, and its note says a complete major-allergen statement was not published online and asks guests to check the current container label. Limoncello is also filed under Sorbetto and has a similar incomplete-statement caution. These are examples of how a category and its product note work together—not a statement about what is in stock today.

The Vegan category is also a product classification. Sweet Escape’s Vegan Chocolate card uses the manufacturer’s description of imported dark chocolate and says chocolate may introduce soy; it asks guests to verify the current label. “Vegan” does not eliminate every possible ingredient or handling concern. For allergy or dietary questions, read the exact card and speak with the shop rather than treating a category as a certification.

A flavor in the Gelato category may have its own declared allergens. Pure Sicilian Pistachio, for example, lists milk and tree nut (pistachio) in the catalog. That is product-specific information and should not be carried over to another gelato. The separate Vegan gelato guide explains the limits of using a broad category label when ingredient or handling details matter.

What the known-allergen selector does

The selector currently offers Milk, Egg, Wheat, Soy, and Tree nut. Its filter checks the card’s known-allergen and may-contain values. That makes it useful for finding records where one of those terms appears in the stored allergen data. It is not a complete ingredient search and it does not determine the full risk of a product or serving.

Not every card has a complete major-allergen statement online. When a card lacks a declared allergen array, the summary displays “Check current label,” and the details note that a complete statement was not published online. Other entries can include both known allergens and a separate “May contain” line or a note about a component such as chocolate. Read all those fields; do not stop at the summary chip or a filter result.

The control lists five known allergens, but a product question may concern another ingredient, an incomplete record, or a handling issue. Search does not scan those separate fields. Open the product details, follow the available nutrition and allergen sources, and ask staff to check the current container label. Sweet Escape’s safety note specifically directs guests to request that check for allergies or dietary needs. The nutrition and allergen guide provides a wider explanation of the site’s product records and cautions.

Read the item card and source links

Each card provides a flavor name, category, description, and a link to its dedicated item page. Depending on the record, it may also show a nutrition summary, known allergens, a “May contain” field, a caution note, and manufacturer or nutrition-source links. Some nutrition numbers appear only where a product label is publicly available. If a field is missing, do not fill it in from a different flavor or an assumption about the category.

The expandable sections let visitors scan product names first and open details when needed. Expand both nutrition and allergen information before comparing products. If a manufacturer label or supplier catalog is linked, the external source can provide context for that specific item. The current package remains important because a page or label file may not reflect every subsequent packaging change.

A product card is not a reservation. It is also not a guarantee about how a serving is handled at the counter. If a food allergy matters, Sweet Escape asks guests to verify the current container label. Ask separately about the practical handling detail relevant to your concern; an online ingredient list cannot certify cross-contact conditions.

A zero-result view is a filter outcome

When several filters are active, the list can narrow to no matching catalog entries. That means no product record met the exact combination of search text, category, and allergen condition. It does not mean there are no gelato flavors in the catalog or none available at the shop. Return to All, clear the search, and reset the allergen selector before trying again.

The catalog count updates to reflect the filtered records. This is useful feedback while you browse, but it is not the stock total. The live stock page has separate counts for scoops, yogurt, and gelato. Compare the right kind of number on the right page, and check the timestamp when availability matters.

Move from discovery to today’s menu

Once you find an item to explore, check the live stock page and select Gelato. Search the exact product name and read the displayed availability update time. If the flavor is not shown or if your plan depends on it, contact Sweet Escape for confirmation. Catalog membership documents a product; the stock page reports the current list.

For a broader first-time comparison between frozen dessert formats, see the gelato, ice cream, and frozen yogurt guide. It can help you decide which collection to browse, while the individual gelato card gives product-level information. The Visit page has the shop’s phone number and directions if you need to check a detail before traveling.

A careful browsing routine

  1. Use the catalog search for a short flavor name or descriptive term.
  2. Select Gelato, Sorbetto, or Vegan to narrow the records by their assigned category.
  3. Use the known-allergen filter as a locator, then read all fields on the exact card.
  4. Open supplier and nutrition sources when available; verify current packaging for important details.
  5. Check the separate stock page for current reported availability.

If the online record leaves a question unanswered, keep the full flavor name handy when you contact the shop. Mention whether you need a stock check, current label information, or a question about serving handling. Those are different requests, and naming the exact product makes it easier to direct the question to the right information.

Use the catalog as a guide, not a verdict

Sweet Escape’s gelato catalog connects search, category browsing, known-allergen filters, product descriptions, and source links. Each feature is helpful within its scope. Search finds words in limited text fields; category labels group records; the allergen selector looks for stored values; and the item card presents the details available online. None alone verifies current stock or decides whether a product meets a personal need.

Start with the gelato directory and open the exact record. Check the live in-stock list separately, and ask Sweet Escape to verify the current label when ingredients or handling matter. This keeps a category or search result in its proper context.
