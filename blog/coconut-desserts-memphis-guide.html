A Memphis guide to coconut-forward desserts

Coconut Desserts in Memphis: Pineapple, Flakes, or Gelato?

Published October 12, 2026 · Sweet Escape, Memphis

When a dessert menu has several coconut names, the useful question is not simply “Which one is coconut?” It is what the product page says comes with the coconut. At Sweet Escape in Memphis, three catalog entries give different answers: Pina Coolada pairs coconut ice cream with pineapple chunks, Loco 4 Coco pairs coconut ice cream with coconut flakes, and Caribbean Coconut is a gelato listing whose description mentions toasted coconut texture.

This guide compares those published details so you can decide which page to explore first. It does not promise that any flavor is in the case today; the live in-stock menu is the place to check current posted availability.

The quick comparison

  • Pina Coolada: ice cream described as coconut ice cream with pineapple chunks.
  • Loco 4 Coco: ice cream described as coconut ice cream with coconut flakes.
  • Caribbean Coconut: gelato described as creamy coconut gelato with a mellow tropical finish and toasted coconut texture.

The first two listings share a category and supplier product-line label, but the named additions differ. The third belongs to another catalog category and uses a broader descriptive phrase. These are practical distinctions in the published records—not a ranking, a guarantee about mouthfeel, or a claim that the recipes are otherwise alike.

Pina Coolada: the listing that names pineapple

Sweet Escape’s Pina Coolada page uses the short description “Coconut ice cream with pineapple chunks.” That is the clearest reason to start there if pineapple is the component you are looking for. The page categorizes it as ice cream and identifies its supplier product line as Super-Premium. The product page also links to the manufacturer’s nutrition label, which is the right place to consult for that product’s published nutrition information.

The name resembles “piña colada,” the familiar Puerto Rican cocktail. The naming connection can be useful context, but a flavor name is not an ingredient statement. Discover Puerto Rico’s history overview notes competing origin accounts for the cocktail; Sweet Escape’s page, meanwhile, documents coconut ice cream and pineapple chunks. It does not settle every ingredient or alcohol question. If alcohol content matters to you, ask the shop to verify the current container label rather than relying on the name or a general cocktail recipe.

Loco 4 Coco: coconut named twice, with flakes

The Loco 4 Coco description is “Coconut ice cream with coconut flakes.” Compared with Pina Coolada, its published distinguishing addition is coconut flakes rather than pineapple chunks. Its item page also identifies the category as ice cream and the product line as Super-Premium, and links to a manufacturer nutrition label.

That makes the choice straightforward at the description level: look at Pina Coolada when the pineapple component is what caught your eye; look at Loco 4 Coco when the listing’s coconut flakes are the specific detail you want to explore. The records do not say that either option has more coconut, a stronger taste, or a particular sweetness level, so this guide avoids making those comparisons.

Caribbean Coconut: the gelato entry

Caribbean Coconut is listed in Sweet Escape’s gelato catalog, not the scoop-flavor category. Its product page describes it as “Creamy coconut gelato with a mellow tropical finish and toasted coconut texture.” That wording offers more descriptive detail than the short ice-cream listings, including the toasted-coconut texture note. It does not provide a complete numeric nutrition label in the published record.

If you are deciding between product types as well as coconut additions, Caribbean Coconut is the entry to read alongside the two ice-cream pages. Sweet Escape’s gelato and sorbetto catalog and scoop catalog separate those categories. A category label tells you how the item is listed; it should not be used to infer an undocumented recipe, serving style, or availability.

How to choose without guessing

Try this quick menu-browsing sequence:

  1. Choose the detail you care about. Pineapple chunks point to Pina Coolada; coconut flakes point to Loco 4 Coco; the gelato description with toasted coconut texture points to Caribbean Coconut.
  2. Open the individual product page. Check the category, exact description, and any linked nutrition information rather than relying on a search snippet or a similar-sounding name.
  3. Check the live menu. A product can remain documented in the catalog when it is not listed in the latest stock update. Confirm the current menu before making a trip for one particular item.
  4. Verify the current label when ingredients matter. Published notes are a useful starting point, but labels and recipes can change. Ask the shop to confirm the current container label if you have an allergy or ingredient question.

This approach also works for a group with different preferences: each person can pick a product page based on its stated component, then check the same live menu before heading out. No one has to assume that a tropical-sounding name guarantees pineapple, toasted coconut, or a particular format.

Allergen and nutrition notes worth checking

The Pina Coolada and Loco 4 Coco item pages both list milk and coconut as allergens. Both also publish the same shared-equipment caution: the products are made on equipment that also processes peanuts, other tree nuts, wheat, soy, and eggs. That is an important part of the comparison, especially because one flavor name may sound simpler than another.

Caribbean Coconut’s page identifies milk and tree nut (coconut) as known allergens and asks guests to verify the current container label. It also states that a complete numeric nutrition label was not available in the published record. Do not fill that gap using the numbers for a different coconut product. For more context, see Sweet Escape’s nutrition and allergen guide, then check the specific item page and ask the shop about the current label whenever an allergy or dietary concern matters. None of these catalog notes should be treated as a guarantee about cross-contact safety.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between Pina Coolada and Loco 4 Coco?

Both are listed as coconut ice cream. Pina Coolada’s description names pineapple chunks; Loco 4 Coco’s names coconut flakes. Their published allergen and shared-equipment notes are the same.

Is Caribbean Coconut an ice cream flavor?

Sweet Escape categorizes Caribbean Coconut as gelato and describes toasted coconut texture. Its item page says a complete numeric nutrition label is not available in the published record.

Does the Pina Coolada name mean the dessert contains alcohol?

The product description names coconut ice cream and pineapple chunks, but the name alone does not answer an alcohol question. Verify the current container label with the shop if that detail matters.

Can I count on one of these being available today?

No catalog entry guarantees same-day availability. Check the live in-stock menu before visiting.

A simple next step

For a pineapple component, start with Pina Coolada; for coconut flakes, read Loco 4 Coco; for a gelato description that mentions toasted coconut texture, open Caribbean Coconut. Then check the live menu and the product-specific label notes. That small bit of browsing helps you arrive with a clear first choice—and accurate information about what the page actually says.
