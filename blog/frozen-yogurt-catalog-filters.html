A guide to yogurt filters

How to Search Sweet Escape’s Frozen Yogurt Menu

Published September 18, 2026 · Updated September 30, 2026 · Sweet Escape, Memphis

Sweet Escape’s frozen yogurt catalog includes search, category buttons, and a declared-allergen selector to help visitors narrow the list. These tools make it easier to locate a flavor card, but they do not all search the same information. Understanding what each control does—and when to open the full record—helps you browse confidently without treating a filter result as a nutrition claim, an ingredient guarantee, or a live availability report.

This walkthrough follows the controls on the frozen yogurt menu. It explains the category choices, the Seasonal filter, the allergen options, and the expandable information on each card. It also separates catalog discovery from today’s in-stock list, so you know which page to use for each question.

First decide what you are trying to find

If you have a name in mind, search can be the shortest path. If you want to browse a general group, choose a category. If you are looking at declared allergen records, use that selector only as a directory aid and then inspect the exact product card. If your main question is whether a flavor is listed today, use the stock page instead of the broader catalog.

These are separate tasks because each page has different data. The yogurt catalog is built from product records and their descriptions. The live in-stock menu uses a current stock list to show the items reported for sale. Finding a name in the catalog does not tell you that it is in stock; seeing a product in stock does not provide a full ingredient statement.

Search by flavor name, category, or description

The search box checks three text fields in each record: the flavor’s name, its category, and its description. That means a search term can find a name directly or match a descriptive phrase. Try one short word first—such as a flavor name or a word you remember from its description. If results are broad or empty, try a different term rather than assuming that the product is not part of the catalog.

Search is not a natural-language question box. It does not search the full ingredient statement, nutrition PDF, facility note, or every allergen status field. Searching for a specific ingredient therefore cannot replace opening the item and reading its details. Search helps you locate likely records; the product card tells you what information is published for that product.

The search box can be combined with category and declared-allergen controls. Every active choice narrows the results: a record must match your typed text, the selected category, and the selected allergen status. If a choice disappears, clear the search field and return the other controls to All, then reapply one condition at a time. That makes it easier to see which filter changed the list.

Use the category buttons as browsing paths

The yogurt menu provides All, Nonfat, No Sugar Added, Yogurt, Sorbet, and Seasonal categories. All returns every record in the catalog. The other buttons narrow the directory using the product’s assigned category; Seasonal follows the seasonal flag in the record. These labels are useful for exploration, but they are not substitutes for the exact flavor name or the product description.

A category label tells you how a record is grouped on this menu. It does not independently answer questions about ingredients, current availability, or personal dietary suitability. In particular, “No Sugar Added” is the catalog category for those records; it should not be expanded into claims that a product is sugar-free or appropriate for a particular health need. Read the product’s own nutrition and ingredient details and verify the current package if you need that information.

The seasonal filter is also about catalog classification, not a real-time stock count. A result marked seasonal can still require a separate check on the live menu. Likewise, an item in another category may be currently available even when you are browsing only the Seasonal subset. For the broader shop menu, return to in-stock listings and check the displayed update time.

Know what the allergen selector includes

The selector lists Milk, Egg, Wheat, Soy, Tree nut, Peanut, and Sesame, plus All. In the page’s data logic, an allergen filter keeps products where the corresponding status is explicitly “Yes.” A selected option therefore narrows to records that list that allergen as declared; it is not a universal screen for every possible warning connected to the product.

Other status values matter too. Product details may display Yes, No, or Not listed for individual major allergens, and an entry may carry a separate facility note. “No declared major allergens” in a summary is not a promise about shared equipment, possible traces, or how a serving is handled. When reading an exact card, expand the ingredients and allergen details instead of stopping at the filter badge.

Sweet Escape’s published cautions say that product information may be incomplete and cross-contact in the shop is possible. If an allergy or ingredient issue matters, ask the shop to confirm the current container label and address your specific handling question. The nutrition and allergen guide describes how to distinguish declared ingredients, separate facility information, and unknown online fields without making a safety guarantee.

Read the flavor card beyond its category

Each visible flavor card includes a name, category, and short description. It also offers expandable nutrition facts and an ingredients-and-allergens section. Open both before drawing a conclusion. The collapsed appearance simply keeps the catalog easier to scan; it does not mean the hidden information is unimportant or absent.

Nutrition panels are linked to source images or PDFs for the listed product. The page’s nutrition information is provided for its stated serving basis. Compare like serving sizes and note any date or manufacturer source shown. Different products can have different ingredients and values even when they share a category or seem similar by name. Do not substitute one flavor’s label for another’s.

Ingredient lists are lengthy, so use the exact product card and the individual item page when a detail matters. Some cards include manufacturer descriptions or a facility statement. Those details provide context for the particular record, not a blanket statement about every flavor made by the same supplier. If the label is unavailable online or has changed, ask the shop to check the current container information.

Open the separate item page

The product name links to a dedicated page for the yogurt flavor. Use that page when you want to bookmark or share one exact item, view its structured product details, or check the linked current-status element. A product page is focused on one catalog entry; it does not replace the catalog’s filters or the stock page’s full menu view.

For a quick comparison, keep the full names visible as you move between cards. Similar words may appear in separate dessert formats, and a yogurt entry should not be confused with a scoop or a gelato using a related name. Sweet Escape maintains separate scoop and gelato collections with their own product records.

What an empty filtered list means

If no cards remain after you choose a category, enter a search word, or select an allergen, first reset the controls. Set the category and allergen to All and clear the search term. Then apply one filter at a time. An empty result means no record matched that particular combination; it does not establish that the shop has no frozen yogurt, nor does it prove that an item is unavailable.

The yogurt catalog’s count updates to match the filtered records, which can help show whether a filter changed the list. The stock page has its own counts and data. Do not compare numbers between these pages as if they represented the same set: one describes catalog entries; the other reports current availability across groups.

A practical way to browse

  1. Open the yogurt catalog and search one short flavor term or select a category.
  2. Add the declared-allergen selector only to locate matching “Yes” records.
  3. Open each relevant card and expand both nutrition and ingredient/allergen details.
  4. Read the exact product name and any “Not listed” or facility cautions.
  5. Check the live stock menu separately; contact the shop for a current label or handling question.

This method avoids asking one control to answer everything. Search finds likely products; category buttons organize the catalog; cards show the detailed fields; and the live page handles current stock. When your decision depends on ingredients or allergens, direct confirmation is more reliable than a filter result alone.

Make the catalog useful without overreading it

Sweet Escape’s yogurt tools can help you browse a substantial menu by name, category, seasonal status, or a declared allergen. Their most important limit is also simple: the filters work with the fields saved in each online product record. They cannot confirm every package detail, assess an individual dietary risk, or promise that a flavor will remain available.

Start at the frozen yogurt catalog, use one control at a time, and read the details for the exact product you mean. Then visit the live stock page for current listings and contact Sweet Escape if a label or handling detail needs confirmation. This gives you a clearer menu decision while keeping the catalog, current availability, and product-specific questions distinct.
