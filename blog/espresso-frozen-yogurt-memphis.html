Espresso Frozen Yogurt in Memphis: A Coffee-Inspired Sweet Escape

Published September 28, 2026 · Sweet Escape, Memphis

Some days, your dessert decision is easy: chocolate, vanilla, or the flavor you order every time. Other days, you want something that takes a different direction. If coffee is the flavor that catches your attention, Espresso frozen yogurt deserves a place on your Sweet Escape shortlist.

At our Memphis shop near Bartlett, Espresso is part of the frozen-yogurt catalog. Its description centers on roasted-coffee flavor with a smooth, creamy body, and its ingredient list includes coffee concentrate made from water and coffee. That gives this dessert a clear identity: coffee-inspired frozen yogurt, with dairy and sweetness built into the recipe.

But how does it compare with coffee gelato? Does “Espresso” tell you anything about caffeine? And should you keep your cup simple or add toppings? Here is a closer look at the flavor and the choices surrounding it.

Coffee is part of the recipe

Espresso is more than a coffee-themed name. The ingredient list identifies coffee concentrate alongside milk, cream, nonfat milk, whey, liquid sugar, and corn syrup. It also lists active yogurt cultures and ingredients used in the formulation, including stabilizers and caramel color.

For someone deciding what to order, the important distinction is straightforward: this is a sweetened, dairy-based frozen dessert with coffee in its recipe. It is not a cup of brewed coffee served cold, and it should not be approached as though it will taste exactly like your usual espresso drink.

That difference can help you choose. If you enjoy coffee as a dessert flavor, this yogurt gives you a relevant starting point. If you specifically want an unsweetened coffee drink, the ingredient list describes something else.

Our Espresso frozen yogurt page brings together the flavor description, ingredients, allergen information, and nutrition resources so you can explore the details before visiting.

What the flavor description tells you

The description emphasizes roasted coffee and a creamy body. Those are the useful expectations to bring to the order—not assumptions about a particular coffee origin, roast level, brewing technique, or number of espresso shots.

We do not have those additional details to share, and the name alone cannot supply them. Likewise, a coffee flavor does not tell you exactly how sweet or intense a finished dessert will seem to your palate.

Think about what you enjoy in other coffee-inspired desserts. Is it the coffee flavor itself? The combination of coffee and dairy? Or coffee paired with chocolate? Your answer can guide you toward Espresso yogurt or toward another coffee-themed item in our wider catalog.

You do not need a complicated tasting vocabulary. Knowing whether you want coffee to be the main idea or one part of a larger combination is enough to make the choice more personal.

Espresso yogurt versus Italian Espresso gelato

Sweet Escape’s catalog also includes Italian Espresso gelato, described as a full-bodied espresso gelato with deep roasted-coffee flavor.

The similar names make these two worth comparing, but they identify separate products. One belongs to the frozen-yogurt selection; the other belongs to the gelato selection. Their product details should be read separately rather than treating one page as a substitute for the other.

Espresso frozen yogurt fits our self-serve yogurt experience. Italian Espresso offers a coffee-focused option within the gelato catalog. That format difference may matter more to your decision than trying to rank one as universally better.

We also would not assume that one contains more caffeine, tastes stronger, or has a particular nutritional advantage without comparable product information. If coffee is your priority, start with the format you want, then check the current menu to see which matching choices are listed.

When you want coffee and chocolate together

Another catalog option is Coffee Chocolate Chip gelato. Its description pairs coffee gelato with crisp dark chocolate chips.

That is a different proposition from choosing Espresso yogurt for its coffee-focused recipe. The chocolate chips give you an explicitly described additional component, making this a useful comparison for someone who wants coffee and chocolate in the same dessert.

Ask yourself which part of that idea matters most. If you want to explore coffee flavor without choosing a product described around chocolate pieces, Espresso yogurt may be the more relevant starting point. If the chocolate-chip component is what makes the order appealing, the gelato deserves a closer look when available.

Coffee Chocolate Chip’s allergen notes also need attention: milk is listed, and its page says a complete published allergen statement was not available online. Chocolate may introduce soy, so the current container label should be verified before ordering when that information matters.

Build your cup around one clear idea

With self-serve frozen yogurt, it is easy to focus on additions before deciding what you want from the yogurt itself. For Espresso, consider reversing that order.

Start with the question: “Do I want a coffee-focused dessert, or a dessert that happens to include coffee?”

If coffee is the main attraction, a simple cup lets that choice stay central. You do not have to add several toppings to make the order feel finished. Choosing the yogurt on its own is a valid way to discover whether this is a flavor you would order again.

If you prefer a more layered dessert, choose from the toppings available during your visit with that goal in mind. Think about whether an addition contributes something you actually want, rather than adding it simply because it is there.

There is no required combination, and this guide does not promise any particular topping will be available. Our guide to building a frozen-yogurt cup offers more help with the broader process.

Does Espresso frozen yogurt contain caffeine?

The ingredient list includes coffee concentrate, but the product information available to us does not provide a verified caffeine amount.

That means we cannot tell you how many milligrams are in a serving or compare a cup of this yogurt with a shot of espresso. We also cannot describe it as decaffeinated or caffeine-free.

The practical distinction is between knowing an ingredient and knowing its measured quantity in a finished serving. Coffee appearing in the recipe answers the first question; it does not answer the second.

If caffeine content is an important part of your decision, contact the shop before ordering to ask whether current manufacturer information is available. If an amount cannot be verified, the uncertainty remains. Neither the flavor name nor how strongly a dessert tastes of coffee is a substitute for that information.

Read the dairy and allergen details

Espresso frozen yogurt contains milk. Milk, cream, nonfat milk, and whey appear in its ingredient list, so this is not a dairy-free option.

Its product information also carries the following notice: “This product is manufactured in a facility that also processes peanuts, tree nuts, eggs, soy, milk, and wheat products.”

The facility notice and the product’s declared-allergen table serve different purposes. An allergen marked “No” in that table is not a guarantee against cross-contact.

Toppings and in-store handling require their own consideration, too. Review the full product information and ask about current labels and handling if an allergy or dietary restriction affects your order. Formulas and product information can change.

For nutrition numbers, use the serving amount shown on the linked label. A manufacturer’s serving is not automatically the same as the cup you build, especially after additions. The label is a reference for the product, not a calculation of every possible finished dessert.
