Six Specialty Desserts at Sweet Escape in Memphis

Published September 12, 2026 · Updated September 30, 2026 · Sweet Escape, Memphis

Sometimes the hardest part of choosing dessert is deciding what kind of dessert you want. A scoop puts the flavor first; a composed specialty adds a base, a pairing, or a different way to enjoy it. Sweet Escape’s Memphis specialties menu documents six formats: Cookie Dough Base, Brownie Base, Dubai Dream, Ice Cream Nachos, Cookie Ice Cream Sandwiches, and self-serve frozen yogurt with toppings.

This guide compares those formats using the published descriptions, so you can narrow your choice before opening the full menu. It does not promise that every component or flavor is available on a particular day. For current listings, use the live menu; for ingredients, nutrition, or allergen information, read the individual product record and its cautions.

Start with the format, then choose a flavor

A helpful way to browse the Sweet Escape specialties menu is to separate the structure of a dessert from the flavor that goes with it. The structure is the part the menu describes consistently: a warm base beneath a scoop, two scoops paired with another component, ice cream between cookies, or a self-serve yogurt cup finished at a topping station. The flavor is a separate choice, and its current status belongs on the live menu.

That distinction keeps the decision manageable. First ask whether you want a baked base, something layered, a cookie sandwich, or a hands-on yogurt cup. Then review the relevant category for the flavor options. A familiar word in a flavor name is not a complete ingredient list, and a specialty’s description does not tell you whether every flavor is available at the moment you arrive.

For a warm base: Cookie Dough Base or Brownie Base

Both warm-base specialties follow a straightforward structure: a baked dessert underneath a chosen scoop of ice cream. Cookie Dough Base is described as freshly baked chocolate-chip cookie dough with your choice of one scoop on top. Brownie Base pairs a warm brownie base with your choice of one scoop. If your first question is “cookie or brownie?”, this is the cleanest comparison because the menu identifies the base and the scoop count for both.

Cookie Dough Base makes the cookie dough itself part of the dessert, rather than simply adding a cookie-flavored scoop. Brownie Base makes the brownie the base instead. The published descriptions do not list every preparation detail or all components’ full ingredients, so avoid reading more into the names than the shop states. If you want to compare the two in greater depth, the Cookie Dough Base vs. Brownie Base guide walks through the structures and the product-information checks to make.

When deciding between them, start with the base you are most interested in and then look at the current scoop list. The hand-dipped flavor catalog is useful for published descriptions and product details; the live in-stock menu is the place to check what the shop currently lists. Keeping those two pages straight helps avoid planning around a flavor that appears in the catalog but not in the latest availability update.

For a layered dessert: Dubai Dream

Dubai Dream has a different structure from the warm bases. Its published description specifies two chosen scoops layered with Dubai chocolate filling, pistachio cream, and crisp kataifi. Rather than a base with one scoop, the menu presents multiple named components together. That makes the component list important: someone comparing it with a simple scoop is comparing different constructions, not only different flavors.

For general browsing, the gelato catalog and scoop catalog can help you explore the product categories, while the live menu answers the changing availability question. If a particular scoop is essential to your plan, confirm the latest listing and contact the shop if the online record leaves a material question unanswered.

For dipping: Ice Cream Nachos

Ice Cream Nachos are described as two scoops of your choice served with crisp, round waffle chips for dipping. The waffle chips change how the dessert is approached: the published format pairs them with the scoops rather than placing a single scoop on a warm baked base. You can use that description to decide whether the dipping format sounds more appealing than a layered dessert or a cookie sandwich.

“Nachos” here is the name of a dessert format, not a full nutritional or ingredient disclosure. The menu tells you about the two scoops and waffle chips, but it does not by itself answer every question about brands, preparation, portions, or shared equipment. Review the posted product details that apply to the components and keep any published caution attached to the relevant item.

Because the scoops are chosen, the flavor decision remains part of the order. Check the current menu close to your visit rather than assuming a catalog example will be available. For nutrition or allergy concerns, review each selected flavor’s own card and ask for current-label verification when needed.

For cookies around the scoop: Cookie Ice Cream Sandwich

The Cookie Ice Cream Sandwich is described as your choice of ice cream pressed between two chocolate-chip cookies. It gives the dessert a hand-built sandwich form, distinct from the warm cookie dough base: the cookies frame the ice cream instead of serving as a baked base under a scoop. The cookie sandwich guide offers a closer look at that format and how it differs from the other cookie-based specialties.

For a hands-on cup: self-serve frozen yogurt

Self-serve frozen yogurt is the format on this list where the menu explicitly invites you to swirl a yogurt cup and then visit the topping station to finish it. The yogurt and toppings are separate decisions, which can make this a natural option for someone who wants to put together a combination rather than choose a composed specialty from a fixed description. The topping selection and yogurt availability can change, so the page’s format should not be mistaken for a promise about any particular topping or flavor.

Start with the frozen yogurt catalog to review published flavor details, then check the live menu for current listings. A catalog can help you learn what an item is documented as; it does not tell you what is in the self-serve machine right now. For practical steps on choosing a cup and considering toppings, see the frozen yogurt cup guide.

When an ingredient or allergen matters, review the specific yogurt and topping information rather than treating a self-serve setup as an ingredient guarantee. A cup can contain more than one item, and published cautions remain relevant to the components you select. If the online information is incomplete for your need, contact the shop for help checking current product labels and handling information.

Your preference may depend on whether you are drawn to a base, layers, dipping, cookies, or a self-serve cup. Reading the description that matches that preference is a more useful first step than trying to compare unrelated formats by name alone.
