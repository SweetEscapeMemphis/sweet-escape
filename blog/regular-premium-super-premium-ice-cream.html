Ice cream, explained

Regular vs. Premium vs. Super-Premium Ice Cream in Memphis

Published October 1, 2026 · Sweet Escape, Memphis

Ice cream labels can sound like a neat ladder: regular, premium, then super-premium. The words suggest a simple ranking, but the real picture is more specific. In the United States, federal rules set a standard for what may be called ice cream; “premium” and “super-premium” are commonly used product-line descriptions, not universal grades with one definition shared by every maker.

That distinction matters when you are comparing a tub at the grocery store with a scoop-shop flavor. Sweet Escape’s scoop catalog includes items identified by our supplier as part of its Super-Premium line. Here is what that label means in the supplier’s own terms, how it differs from its Premium line, and how to check which flavors are listed for a visit to our Memphis shop.

First, what does “regular” ice cream mean?

“Regular” is a handy everyday word, but it is not a formal federal grade alongside Premium and Super-Premium. U.S. regulations instead define the basic food category “ice cream.” The standard of identity describes its ingredients and production, including a minimum of 10 percent milkfat, with additional calculation rules for products containing bulky flavor ingredients. The rule also sets requirements for other milk solids and finished-product weight.

That legal floor answers a naming question; it does not sort every ice cream into a quality league table. A product that meets the standard can still differ from another in its recipe, flavor, inclusions, and measured nutrition. For the exact legal language, see 21 CFR 135.110.

In conversation, “regular” often means a familiar, standard-style ice cream rather than a supplier’s named premium range. Since that shorthand can mean different things to different people, it is more useful to ask what the package or product record actually says. Is a percentage given? Is a supplier product line named? Is the number about butterfat, or is it a Nutrition Facts value for a particular serving? Those are different kinds of information.

What “premium” means in this comparison

There is no single federal percentage that turns every ice cream into “premium.” The word is used by brands as a product-line label, and the most reliable interpretation comes from the maker’s own published description. For the San Bernardo products represented in Sweet Escape’s scoop catalog, the manufacturer describes its Premium Ice Cream line as 12 percent butterfat.

That gives us a concrete, sourceable comparison point rather than a vague promise. The manufacturer’s Premium Ice Cream page lists the 12 percent line and shows examples such as Banana, Birthday Cake, Coconut, Coffee, Mint Chocolate Chunk, and Pistachio. Sweet Escape’s product pages likewise identify certain flavors as belonging to the Premium line, including Banana, Birthday Cake, and Mint Chocolate Chunk.

What “super-premium” means here

San Bernardo’s Super-Premium Ice Cream line is described by the manufacturer as 14 to 16 percent butterfat. That is the supplier’s stated range for its own line—not a universal legal threshold for all super-premium ice cream. It is two to four percentage points above the 12 percent butterfat that the same supplier publishes for its Premium line.

The manufacturer’s Super-Premium Ice Cream page names the range and lists flavors in that category. The catalog records at Sweet Escape identify a number of our scoop selections as Super-Premium products from San Bernardo. Examples include Cookie Monster, Coffee, Espresso Bean Chip, Sea Salt Caramel Pretzel, Guava Have It, So Very Strawberry, and Tahitian Vanilla.

So yes: Sweet Escape’s documented scoop selection includes super-premium ice cream. The label is grounded in the supplier’s product-line information and the matching product records on our site. It is not a claim that every scoop we carry belongs to that line, or that a particular example is necessarily on the counter today.

How should you interpret the butterfat difference?

Butterfat is one measurable part of an ice cream formulation. In this supplier’s published categories, the Premium line is 12 percent butterfat and the Super-Premium line is 14 to 16 percent. That makes butterfat a useful way to understand how the maker separates these two product lines.

It is not a complete score for flavor, craftsmanship, freshness, or personal enjoyment. A percentage cannot tell you whether you will prefer chocolate to coffee, whether you like a flavor with inclusions, or what a particular serving’s full nutrition profile is. Two flavors in the same product line can have different ingredients and nutrition facts. A higher category label is not the same thing as “better for everyone.”

There is also a difference between a product-line percentage and the Nutrition Facts panel. The manufacturer’s category pages describe butterfat as a feature of a range. An individual package or flavor can have its own serving size and nutrition values, which are the relevant place to look for that specific item. Add-ins and flavor components can also affect the finished product’s nutrition and allergen information. Read the flavor’s own record rather than applying one line-wide assumption to every item.

Ingredients, nutrition, and allergy notes still matter

“Premium” and “Super-Premium” are not dietary labels. They do not tell you whether a particular flavor is appropriate for an allergy or dietary need. Ingredient lists and allergen statements must be reviewed for the exact item, and one flavor’s information should not be applied to another flavor simply because both share a product-line category.

Sweet Escape’s product pages preserve the available manufacturer documentation and the cautions attached to our records. If a page says that an allergen statement is incomplete or identifies equipment or cross-contact concerns, keep that note in view. A blank field is not a safety certification, and the product-line name cannot answer a cross-contact question.

When an allergy or dietary concern matters, read the linked product information and ask the shop to verify the current container label before ordering. Formulations, labels, and the products in stock can change. For a broader explanation of how we present these details, see our nutrition and allergen guide.

A quick comparison

  • Regular or standard: everyday shorthand, not a federal premium tier; U.S. rules define ice cream and set a baseline of at least 10 percent milkfat, subject to specific formulation rules.
  • Premium: a supplier product-line name. San Bernardo publishes 12 percent butterfat for its Premium Ice Cream line.
  • Super-Premium: a supplier product-line name. San Bernardo publishes 14 to 16 percent butterfat for its Super-Premium Ice Cream line.

The percentages make this supplier’s two named categories easy to compare. They should not be turned into a universal grading system for every brand or used as a shortcut for a flavor’s full nutrition, ingredients, or availability.
