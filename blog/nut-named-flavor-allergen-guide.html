How to Read Nut-Named Flavor and Allergen Details

Published September 15, 2026 · Updated September 30, 2026 · Sweet Escape, Memphis

A nut in a flavor name is an important clue, but it is not the whole product record. Sweet Escape’s gelato catalog includes flavors named for hazelnut, pistachio, almond, and pecan, and it also lists other flavors whose product cards contain nut-related cautions. If an allergy or ingredient concern matters, read the exact card, check its full cautions, and ask the shop to verify the current container label. Do not rely on a name, photograph, or category filter to make a safety decision.

This guide explains how to use those records carefully. It distinguishes a nut named in the product description from a “may contain” field or a separate label-check note; shows how the catalog and live menu work together; and gives you a clear set of questions for the shop. It is a guide to Sweet Escape’s published menu information—not medical advice or a guarantee about any product or serving.

Start with the exact flavor card

Sweet Escape’s gelato catalog has a dedicated card for each documented flavor. The card presents the product name and description along with allergen fields and any additional caution supplied in the record. Read the complete entry for the exact flavor you are considering. A nearby flavor, a similar name, or a product in the same category may have different listed information.

For example, Piemonte Hazelnut Chocolate Crunch is described as Belgian chocolate and Piemonte hazelnut gelato with caramel pearls and a chocolate-hazelnut ribbon. Its record lists milk and hazelnut and says chocolate components may introduce soy, with a prompt to verify the current container label. Pure Sicilian Pistachio is described as pistachio gelato and its record identifies milk and pistachio. Maple Roasted Butter Pecan lists milk and pecan. Those details come from separate product records; they should not be generalized to other flavors.

Old World Spumoni provides another useful example. The menu description names chocolate, pistachio, and Amarena cherry, while its allergen record lists milk and pistachio and notes that chocolate components may introduce soy. Cherry Chocolate Chip with Honey Roasted Almonds names almonds in the flavor itself and has its own card. Open the individual entry rather than trying to infer its full declaration from the wording in the title.

A flavor name and an allergen field answer different questions

The flavor name and description help you identify what a product is called and how Sweet Escape describes it. The allergen field and accompanying note provide more specific information recorded for that item. For an ingredient concern, that product-specific record matters more than broad words such as “nutty,” “chocolate,” or “fruit.” Even then, the online card may not include every detail you need; some records tell visitors to verify the current container label.

Some entries also show “May contain” information. That wording is not the same field as a named ingredient or the card’s declared allergen list. Read both the main list and any separate caution, and do not omit one because the other appears more relevant. Other product cards include additional notes—for example, about chocolate or other components—that may affect which current labels need checking.

The catalog’s display can help you compare records, but an empty field is not an affirmative statement that a product is free from an allergen. It can mean that the online record does not provide a complete published statement. If the card says the complete major-allergen statement is unavailable or asks staff to verify the container label, treat that instruction as part of the product information.

Some nut-related cautions are not obvious from the name

A nut may appear directly in a flavor title, as with Pure Sicilian Pistachio or Maple Roasted Butter Pecan. In other cases, the name may not point to nuts, while the online record includes a separate “May contain” note. Sweet Escape’s Belgian Dark Chocolate card, for example, lists milk and egg and separately names wheat, tree nuts, peanuts, and soy under “May contain.” Other cards also carry separate possible-presence notes.

That is why flavor names alone are not a reliable screen. A name highlights a product; it does not summarize every allergen field or caution. Read each exact card, and never transfer a note from one flavor to another.

Sweet Escape’s catalog is based on information available for particular items and may show that complete declarations are not published online for every entry. If the information is incomplete, do not fill in the gap from another product’s label or from general assumptions about a type of dessert. Ask the shop to confirm the current source information for the exact container in use.

Use the live menu only for availability

The product catalog and the live in-stock menu have different purposes. The catalog explains what flavors Sweet Escape documents and shows their product cards. The live menu reports which items are currently listed. A catalog entry does not guarantee that a flavor is in stock today; an item that is not in the live list may still have a product card with useful information.

If you are planning a visit around a particular flavor, first read its catalog card, then check the live menu’s Gelato tab and the time it was updated. If the flavor is not shown—or if the item’s current status is important to your decision—call the shop and confirm. Availability can change, so an older article, screenshot, or saved menu should not stand in for the current page.

The live-menu guide walks through the search, category tabs, timestamp, and links from a current menu result to the full catalog record. Keeping availability separate from ingredients makes the process clearer: one page can tell you what is reported in stock, while the exact product card and current container label are needed for ingredient questions.

What to do when an allergy matters

If you have a food allergy, use the product card as a starting point and ask Sweet Escape to verify the current container label for the exact flavor. Be clear about the product name and the allergen or ingredient you need checked. If the concern involves a “May contain” line, a component such as chocolate, or the shop’s handling of a scoop, say that directly so you are asking about the relevant detail rather than the flavor title alone.

Sweet Escape’s published caution notes that in-store cross-contact is possible. That means an online product description cannot promise that a serving is free from a particular allergen. Ask what current information the shop can verify about the product and handling, and make your decision based on the answer. If a needed detail cannot be confirmed, do not treat a category or an empty field as a substitute for certainty. This page cannot assess an individual’s medical risk.

The U.S. Food and Drug Administration explains that labels on most packaged foods must identify major food allergens used as ingredients, and it advises consumers with allergies to read all ingredient information. That consumer guidance is about packaged food labels; it does not establish the handling conditions for an individual scoop in a shop. For Sweet Escape’s service, the practical step is to check the current product label and ask the shop directly about the serving. The nutrition and allergen guide explains the site’s product records and their limitations in more detail.

A careful menu check in five steps

  1. Search the catalog for the exact flavor name rather than filtering only by words like “nut” or “chocolate.”
  2. Read the full description, allergen list, any “May contain” field, and every label-check note.
  3. Check the live menu separately for current availability.
  4. If an allergy or ingredient question matters, call and ask the shop to verify the current container label.
  5. Ask separately about handling or cross-contact if that is part of your concern; do not infer it from a flavor card.

This checklist keeps you from having to guess what a product name means. It also helps you ask a focused question when you call: identify the exact flavor and the detail you need verified. If you are browsing more casually, the same approach still helps you compare the shop’s own descriptions without mixing one product’s cautions into another product’s record.
