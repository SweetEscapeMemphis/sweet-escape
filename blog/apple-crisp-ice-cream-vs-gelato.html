Two apple-crisp listings

Apple Crisp Ice Cream or Bourbon Vanilla Apple Crisp Gelato?

Published October 4, 2026 · Sweet Escape, Memphis

When two desserts share the words “apple crisp,” it is natural to wonder whether they are the same flavor in different forms. At Sweet Escape, the answer starts with the full names: Apple Crisp is listed in the scoop catalog as ice cream, while Bourbon Vanilla Apple Crisp is a separate gelato entry. The names share a theme, but the product records describe different desserts and provide different kinds of information.

This guide is for anyone comparing the two while browsing the Sweet Escape menu in Memphis. It sticks to what the published records say: the category, listed components, available nutrition details, and allergen cautions. It does not assume either item is in the current lineup, invent a taste ranking, or fill in details the records do not provide. That makes the comparison useful whether you are deciding which page to read first or preparing a question for the shop.

The quick distinction: ice cream versus gelato

Apple Crisp appears as an Ice Cream item in Sweet Escape’s scoop catalog. The individual page links to a nutrition panel and gives a serving size of 2/3 cup (95 g). It declares milk, wheat, and soy, with an additional shared-equipment note naming peanuts, tree nuts, and eggs.

Bourbon Vanilla Apple Crisp is categorized as Gelato. Its description names bourbon vanilla gelato, spiced apple pieces, caramel, and crisp oat crumble. Its published allergen notes list milk and say the crisp components may introduce wheat or soy. The record does not provide a complete numeric nutrition label.

So the most reliable short answer is not that one is “more apple” or “more indulgent.” The records give us a documented scoop-catalog ice cream with a numeric nutrition panel and a gelato listing with a longer component description. Those are real differences a visitor can use without pretending to know more than the source material tells us.

What the Apple Crisp ice-cream record tells you

The Apple Crisp page provides a serving reference—2/3 cup, or 95 grams—and a nutrition panel associated with the scoop entry. The published panel lists 240 calories, 12 grams of total fat, 8 grams of saturated fat, 85 milligrams of sodium, 30 grams of carbohydrates, 22 grams of total sugars, 18 grams of added sugars, and 3 grams of protein for that serving. The panel’s source date is June 12, 2019.

A dated nutrition panel is helpful for understanding what has been published, but it should not be mistaken for a promise that a recipe or label has never changed. The product page itself advises visitors to check the current container label when an allergy matters. If you are comparing nutrition for a particular reason, use the linked Apple Crisp nutrition label as a starting point and ask the shop to verify the current information.

There is another important limit: the current public product record does not supply a complete ingredient narrative for this flavor. The name is not a substitute for an ingredient list. It does not establish the amount or form of apple, whether any particular spice is included, or the source of every component. We leave those details open rather than turning a familiar dessert name into a guessed recipe.

Its declared allergens and equipment note are specific to this Apple Crisp record. The listed contains statement is milk, wheat, and soy. The separate note names peanuts, tree nuts, and eggs in connection with shared equipment. Keep the two parts together when you review the entry; do not reduce the information to a shorter claim about what the item is “free from.”

What Bourbon Vanilla Apple Crisp gelato adds to the description

The gelato listing offers more descriptive detail about its named components: bourbon vanilla gelato with spiced apple pieces, caramel, and crisp oat crumble. That lets a reader understand the published structure of the product. The apple pieces and oat crumble, for example, are explicitly part of the description rather than assumptions based only on the words “apple crisp.”

The gelato record is also candid about what it does not show. A complete numeric nutrition label was not available in the public record reviewed for this guide. That means there is no supported basis here for comparing the calories, sugars, or serving weight of the gelato with the 95-gram Apple Crisp ice-cream serving. A missing number is not zero, and it is not evidence that two products are nutritionally equivalent.

For allergens, the gelato entry lists milk. It also cautions that crisp components may introduce wheat or soy and asks visitors to verify the current container label. That wording is not interchangeable with the Apple Crisp scoop’s declared milk, wheat, and soy statement plus its shared-equipment note about peanuts, tree nuts, and eggs. Each item needs its own current-label conversation.

If a dietary restriction affects your order, ask about the complete current label and relevant handling questions before choosing. An online catalog can help identify what to ask, but it cannot confirm that an item is suitable for a particular person or guarantee the absence of cross-contact. For a broader explanation of how Sweet Escape presents product information, see the nutrition and allergen guide.

How to choose which listing to explore first

Start with the format you are looking for. If you want to browse a scoop-catalog ice cream and a product with a documented serving-size nutrition panel, begin with Apple Crisp. If the more detailed combination of bourbon vanilla, spiced apple pieces, caramel, and oat crumble is what caught your eye, start with Bourbon Vanilla Apple Crisp gelato. That is a way to navigate the records, not a claim that either product tastes better.

Next, read the full product name and category. The scoop catalog and the gelato catalog are separate collections, and the item pages link back to their respective menu sections. Keeping the category attached to the name makes it easier to find the right record again, especially if you are sharing a link with someone who is comparing the same two options.

Then decide what information matters to your visit. Some guests want to understand the described components. Others need a serving reference or want to check allergen language. The scoop page and gelato page answer different portions of those questions. It is reasonable to use both pages side by side while noting which details are available for one product but not the other.

If your decision depends on a specific ingredient, ask rather than infer. For example, the Apple Crisp scoop page does not publish a full ingredient list in the data reviewed here. The gelato’s descriptive sentence identifies several components, but a description is not a full ingredient statement either. Product names, marketing descriptions, nutrition labels, ingredient lists, and allergen notes have different jobs.
