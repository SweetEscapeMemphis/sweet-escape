Beyond the scoop

Two Cookie Desserts to Explore at Sweet Escape

Published September 5, 2026 · Updated September 30, 2026 · Sweet Escape, Memphis

At Sweet Escape, cookies show up in more than one kind of frozen dessert. The Cookie Dough Base begins with freshly baked chocolate-chip cookie dough and one scoop of ice cream. Cookie Ice Cream Sandwiches put a chosen scoop between two chocolate-chip cookies. Both are cookie-centered specialties, but they give the cookie a different job in the dessert.

If you are browsing from Memphis and trying to decide between them, start with the format you want. Do you picture a warm base below the ice cream, or cookies around it? Once that is clear, check the current scoop list and read the product information for the specific components that matter to you. This guide focuses on those documented differences without guessing at sizes, prices, toppings, or availability.

Two specialties, two different structures

The specialties menu describes Cookie Dough Base as freshly baked chocolate-chip cookie dough with your choice of one ice cream scoop on top. The Cookie Ice Cream Sandwich listing describes your chosen ice cream pressed between two chocolate-chip cookies. The first is open-faced; the second places the ice cream between cookies.

That structural difference is the simplest way to choose. If the warm cookie-dough base is the reason the menu caught your eye, open the Cookie Dough Base page. If the sandwich format is what you mean by a cookie dessert, use the Cookie Ice Cream Sandwich page.

The descriptions do not provide every recipe or preparation detail. For example, they do not specify the cookie recipe for the sandwich or the full ingredient list for the cookie dough base. Use the published format to understand the dessert, then ask about any additional detail that affects your choice.

Cookie Dough Base: the cookie dough is the foundation

The menu's Cookie Dough Base description identifies freshly baked chocolate-chip cookie dough under one selected ice cream scoop. That makes the dough the foundation and the scoop the second part of the decision. The specialty is not simply the Cookie Dough ice cream flavor served by itself.

This distinction matters because a flavor name and a specialty name can sound similar. The hand-dipped catalog's Cookie Dough entry describes an individual scoop product; the specialty page describes a baked base topped with a scoop. Their written descriptions answer different questions. Do not use the scoop's nutrition or allergen record as the complete information for the composed specialty.

If you are comparing the Cookie Dough Base with Brownie Base, both are listed with a baked foundation and one ice cream scoop. The foundation is the published difference: chocolate-chip cookie dough for one, warm brownie for the other. Our warm-base comparison keeps that decision separate from choosing between the cookie formats in this guide.

Cookie Ice Cream Sandwiches: the cookie surrounds the scoop

The Cookie Ice Cream Sandwich page gives a compact description: your choice of ice cream pressed between two chocolate-chip cookies. That structure puts the scoop in the middle, held between two cookie components. It is different from a scoop atop a cookie-dough base.

The published menu does not identify a fixed ice cream flavor. If you are interested in a particular scoop, look at the current in-stock menu and check the Scoops section. Then confirm the current sandwich availability and choice with the shop if your visit depends on it. The presence of an ice cream flavor in stock does not by itself guarantee that the specialty is currently available or that every scoop can be used in it.

These details leave room for personal preference without promising substitutions. Ask what choices are available rather than assuming that a yogurt or gelato flavor can replace the ice cream specified by the specialty description.

Think about what you want the cookie to contribute

Some visitors may be drawn to the cookie component as the main idea, while others are interested in the scoop and see the cookies as the format around it. This is a question of preference, not a documented claim about relative texture, temperature, or portion size.

With the Cookie Dough Base, the written description emphasizes the base beneath the scoop. With the sandwich, the written description emphasizes the cookies surrounding the ice cream. You can choose by imagining which structure you would rather order, without needing to rank which one is more indulgent or larger.

If neither format is quite what you mean, Sweet Escape lists Ice Cream Nachos and Dubai Dream as other specialties. Ice Cream Nachos are described as two scoops served with crisp round waffle chips for dipping. Dubai Dream is described with two scoops, Dubai chocolate filling, pistachio cream, and crisp kataifi. Those are different dessert structures rather than cookie substitutes.

Choose the scoop after the specialty

Once you know which cookie format interests you, browse the current scoop options. The larger scoop catalog helps you find a named flavor and its published nutrition or allergen details. The live stock page helps you see which scoops the shop currently reports. They are separate resources with different purposes.

Read the exact card for a flavor you are considering. Similar flavor names can have different ingredient information and additional cautions. A flavor photograph may illustrate one example, but the written page and current list are better sources for planning.

If you are flexible, keep a first choice and another option in mind. If you need one exact flavor, contact the shop before traveling. Availability can change, and the website does not reserve an item by listing it.

Separate the cookie components from the ice cream details

Individual scoop product cards do not provide a complete ingredient, nutrition, or allergen statement for the cookie dough base or sandwich cookies. The specialty pages describe their formats, but the available information does not publish every cookie-component detail. That information gap matters when you are combining records: the scoop label applies to the scoop, not automatically to the entire assembled dessert.

For an ingredient or allergy question, read the selected scoop card and ask the shop to verify current labels for the other components. Discuss handling questions as well. Product information can change, and the website does not guarantee that either specialty is appropriate for an individual dietary need or free from cross-contact.

The nutrition and allergen guide explains how to read declared allergens, equipment or facility cautions, and incomplete product records. Use it as a way to understand the fields, not as a substitute for current label confirmation.

Any additions beyond the published specialty description would need their own product information. This guide does not claim that toppings are included or that a particular modification is offered. Ask before planning around one.

Use a simple decision path

First, choose the cookie structure: base below the scoop or cookies around it. Next, choose a scoop from the current listed options, if a choice is available. Finally, review product details that affect your order and confirm any important unknowns with the shop.

This sequence helps keep the decisions distinct. The cookie format is described on the specialty page. The flavor and label information live in the scoop product card. The current lineup appears on the live menu. Putting those steps together gives you a clear plan without mixing up the products or assuming one page contains the details for all components.

If you are visiting with someone else, you do not have to choose the same specialty. One person can start with the warm Cookie Dough Base while another explores the sandwich format or selects a scoop. The group planning guide has suggestions for sharing menu links while keeping logistics grounded in confirmed details.

Check specialty availability, then make the trip

The stock page tracks reported scoop, yogurt, and gelato flavors. It does not publish a daily stock status for each cookie specialty. That means checking a scoop flavor is helpful for planning the ice cream part, but it may not answer whether the Cookie Dough Base or sandwich itself is currently available.

If a specialty is central to your visit, call Sweet Escape to confirm. The shop is at 1674 Whitten Road, Suite 106, Memphis, Tennessee 38134, near Bartlett.
