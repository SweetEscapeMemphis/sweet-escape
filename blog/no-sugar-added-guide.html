Read the label

What No Sugar Added Means: Six Sweet Escape Catalog Choices

Published September 5, 2026 · Updated September 29, 2026 · Sweet Escape, Memphis

“No Sugar Added” is a useful label to notice, but it is not the end of a product comparison. Sweet Escape's Memphis catalogs document three scoop products and three frozen yogurt products in that category. Each has its own name, serving information, ingredients or available label details, and allergen cautions.

This guide explains how to find those six entries and read the wording accurately. It does not recommend a dessert for a medical condition or declare any choice suitable for a particular diet. The aim is more practical: help you distinguish added sugars from total sugars, keep similar names straight, and check the exact product information you need before visiting.

No sugar added does not mean zero total sugar

The FDA's explanation of added sugars distinguishes total sugars from sugars introduced during processing. Total sugars includes naturally present sugars as well as added sugars. The two label lines therefore answer different questions; you should not read a zero on the added-sugars line as a zero for total sugars.

Sweet Escape's documented scoop records make that distinction visible. The three No Sugar Added entries each list zero grams of added sugars for their reference serving, while their total-sugars numbers are not zero. Read both lines with the serving amount instead of relying on the category heading alone.

The same care applies to other numbers. No Sugar Added does not mean no carbohydrates, no calories, or no milk. It also does not tell you whether a finished dessert with additional components matches the information for the base product.

The three no-sugar-added scoop entries

The scoop catalog documents Butter Pecan - No Sugar Added, Chocolate Moose Tracks - No Sugar Added, and Country Apple - No Sugar Added. All three use a published reference serving of 2/3 cup, or 85 grams. Their source records are dated June 18, 2019, so current labels should be checked rather than assuming the information has never changed.

Keep “No Sugar Added” with the full name when searching. A similarly named product elsewhere in the catalog is not automatically the same formula. A saved note that says only “Butter Pecan,” for example, leaves out the qualifier that brought you to this category.

These are documented catalog options, not a permanent in-stock promise. You can use the cards to prepare questions even when a flavor is absent from the current lineup, but the availability check is a separate step.

Butter Pecan - No Sugar Added

This record lists 7 grams of total sugars, zero grams of added sugars, and 20 grams of total carbohydrates per 2/3-cup, 85-gram serving. It also lists 160 calories for that reference amount. These are published label figures, not a measurement of a particular shop serving.

The declared allergens are milk and pecans. The additional equipment note names peanuts, other tree nuts, wheat, soy, and eggs. Preserve the distinction between the declared allergens and that additional caution. Neither the category label nor the nutrition figures reduces the importance of checking ingredients and relevant handling information.

The available scoop record does not provide a complete ingredient statement. We do not infer a sweetener system or manufacturing method from its name or nutrition numbers. If you need that information, ask about the current container label.

Chocolate Moose Tracks - No Sugar Added

The published reference for this entry lists 5 grams of total sugars, zero grams of added sugars, and 26 grams of total carbohydrates per 2/3-cup, 85-gram serving, with 180 calories. Again, the zero belongs to added sugars—not to the entire nutrition panel.

Its declared allergens are milk, peanuts, and soy. The equipment-related note includes tree nuts, wheat, and eggs. This is a different allergen record from the Butter Pecan entry, even though both sit under the same catalog category.

If you are looking for a chocolate direction within the documented No Sugar Added choices, this is the full scoop name to investigate. It should not be confused with Chocolate yogurt, another No Sugar Added entry in a different catalog, or with a chocolate scoop outside this category.

Country Apple - No Sugar Added

Country Apple's reference lists 6 grams of total sugars, zero grams of added sugars, and 25 grams of total carbohydrates per 2/3-cup, 85-gram serving, with 130 calories. Its record declares milk, and the additional equipment note names peanuts, tree nuts, wheat, soy, and eggs.

The apple name does not establish that the dessert is fruit-only or dairy-free. The milk declaration is a concrete example of why the detailed card matters. Nor does a lower number in one field make this a universal recommendation over another flavor; personal dietary decisions require more context than this menu guide provides.

Keep the exact reference information with the product name if you are comparing it later. Removing the serving line or qualifier can make a copied number much less useful than the original card.

The yogurt catalog has a separate group of three

In the frozen yogurt catalog, the No Sugar Added entries are Chocolate, Tahitian Vanilla (NSA), and Wild Strawberry. Each has its own ingredient statement, allergen table, facility note, and manufacturer nutrition label. The scoop figures above do not apply to these yogurt products.

All three published yogurt statements include nonfat milk, maltodextrin, sorbitol, whey, milk, and sucralose among their components. That shared detail is useful when locating ingredients you want to ask about, but it is not a complete substitute for reading each full statement.

We do not infer how those ingredients will affect an individual or recommend these products for a health condition. If you need guidance on whether a food fits a medical dietary plan, use the current label with advice from your qualified healthcare professional.

Keep the three yogurt names distinct

Chocolate yogurt includes cocoa powder processed with alkali in its published statement. Tahitian Vanilla (NSA) includes artificial vanilla flavor and caramel color. Wild Strawberry includes natural and artificial strawberry flavor, along with the other listed ingredients. Those details help identify the actual records without inventing taste comparisons.

Tahitian Vanilla without the NSA qualifier is another catalog entry, in the Nonfat category. It has a different ingredient statement. Do not drop the qualifier when comparing the cards. Similarly, Wild Strawberry yogurt is not the same product as a Wild Strawberry sorbetto entry in the gelato catalog.

Our vanilla guide follows the vanilla names across categories, while the fruit-flavor guide explains other overlapping fruit names. Use those pages when the naming distinction is the question you are trying to resolve.

Read milk declarations and facility notes together

The three No Sugar Added yogurt records mark milk “Yes.” Each also carries the statement that the product is manufactured in a facility that processes peanuts, tree nuts, eggs, soy, milk, and wheat products. A “No” for another allergen in the table does not cancel this facility information.

The facility statement also does not describe every aspect of handling in the shop. Before ordering for an allergy or dietary need, ask about current labels and relevant cross-contact questions. The website alone cannot establish suitability, and an online filter result is not a safety certification.

For help interpreting the different kinds of information, use the nutrition and allergen guide. It separates declared ingredients, additional cautions, serving references, and gaps in the published records.

Do not extend a base label to the whole dessert

A No Sugar Added base does not automatically make every dessert assembled with it a no-sugar-added dessert. A topping, cookie, brownie, filling, or other component has its own ingredients and nutrition information. The base's manufacturer panel does not account for those additions.

If you are researching a complete specialty or a yogurt cup with toppings, ask about every component. Without verified amounts and product information, this guide cannot supply a reliable total. Keeping the base and finished dessert separate prevents an appealing category name from becoming a broader claim than the information supports.

Check availability before making a special trip

Earlier blog references to a different dated lineup should not be read as current availability. The catalog remains available for research even when these products are absent from that snapshot.
