One flavor family, three dessert formats

Caramel Desserts in Memphis: Ice Cream, Yogurt & Gelato

Published October 9, 2026 · Sweet Escape, Memphis

Looking for a caramel dessert in Memphis, but not sure whether to start with ice cream, frozen yogurt, or gelato? Sweet Escape’s online catalog lists caramel-forward choices in all three categories. The names overlap, but the product descriptions point to different combinations: caramel with a caramel swirl, vanilla with pretzel pieces, or dulce de leche paired with sea salt or roasted banana.

This guide compares those published details so you can narrow your search without guessing about taste, ingredients, or what is in the case today. Catalog pages describe products; check the live menu for the current posted lineup, and open the individual item page for its ingredient and allergen information.

Start with the kind of caramel combination you want

Rather than treating every caramel name as interchangeable, look at what else the description names. Some listings put caramel in the base and swirl; others combine caramel with vanilla, pretzel pieces, truffles, fruit, or salt. That extra detail is a useful way to compare the menu before you decide which category to browse.

Ice cream: caramel in the base, swirl, and mix-ins

Dulce de Leche is described as caramel ice cream with a caramel swirl. The supplier’s catalog identifies it as a Super-Premium ice cream product. That is the straightforward caramel-on-caramel description in this group: the page names both the ice cream and its swirl, without listing additional pieces. Its record declares milk and notes equipment that also processes peanuts, tree nuts, wheat, soy, and eggs.

For a vanilla-based listing with something to bite into, Sea Salt Caramel Pretzel is described as vanilla ice cream with pretzel pieces and caramel swirl. Its named components make it distinct from Dulce de Leche: here, vanilla is the stated base and pretzel pieces are part of the description. The published product record lists milk, wheat, and soy as allergens, and notes equipment that also processes peanuts, tree nuts, and eggs.

Sea Salt Caramel Truffle also starts with vanilla ice cream and caramel swirl, but its description names caramel truffles rather than pretzel pieces. Its product record lists milk and soy and says the equipment also processes peanuts, tree nuts, wheat, and eggs. Check each page separately; similar names do not mean the ingredient or allergen notes are identical.

These examples are identified in the supplier records as Super-Premium products. As explained in our ice cream category guide, that phrase refers to the supplier’s product line, not a guarantee about when a particular flavor is available. It also does not replace the item-specific ingredient and nutrition information.

Frozen yogurt: the pretzel pairing, in a different category

The catalog also lists Sea Salt Caramel Pretzel frozen yogurt. Its published description calls it sea-salted caramel yogurt with crunchy pretzel pieces. That is a different product record from the similarly named scoop: the yogurt entry describes a caramel yogurt base, while the ice cream entry describes vanilla ice cream with pretzel pieces and a caramel swirl.

The yogurt page’s ingredient statement includes milk and wheat ingredients, including pretzel components, and its allergen table marks milk, wheat, and soy. It also carries a facility statement that the product is made in a facility that processes peanuts, tree nuts, eggs, soy, milk, and wheat products. Those notes are specific to this yogurt record and should not be substituted for the scoop product’s equipment note.

If you are browsing self-serve frozen yogurt, the format gives you a different way to build a cup than choosing a single hand-dipped scoop. For a practical overview of that format, read our frozen-yogurt cup guide. The guide can help you think through a combination, while the live menu and exact product pages remain the sources for current listings and product-specific cautions.

Gelato: dulce de leche with salt or roasted banana

Argentine Sea Salt Caramel is a gelato listing described as Argentine dulce de leche and French sea salt. Its published allergen record lists milk and soy and notes that it may contain peanuts, tree nuts, sesame, or egg. That combination gives the listing a different point of comparison from the caramel-swirl ice creams: its description explicitly names dulce de leche and sea salt.

Another gelato listing, Roasted Banana Dulce De Leche, is described as roasted banana gelato woven with thick Argentine dulce de leche. The product page lists milk and asks guests to verify the current container label. The description names banana and dulce de leche; it does not provide a complete ingredient list on the page, so there is no basis here to add recipe details or assume it shares another product’s allergen profile.

For a broader explanation of how gelato and other frozen-dessert categories are presented in the catalog, see our gelato, ice cream, and frozen-yogurt guide. It is a category overview—not a claim that every gelato has one universal recipe or that a category alone answers nutrition questions.

How to choose a starting point

  • Want the caramel-and-swirl description? Start with Dulce de Leche.
  • Prefer a description that names pretzel pieces? Compare Sea Salt Caramel Pretzel in the ice cream and yogurt catalogs; their bases and product records differ.
  • Interested in caramel truffles? Open Sea Salt Caramel Truffle and review its separate details.
  • Looking for the listed sea-salt or banana pairing? Compare Argentine Sea Salt Caramel and Roasted Banana Dulce De Leche gelato by their published descriptions.

Those are ways to navigate the written descriptions, not promises about what any guest will prefer. If you already know you want a certain category—scoop, yogurt, or gelato—use that as your first filter, then read the product page for the exact combination. If you are choosing with someone else, each person can name one detail they want to see in the description, such as pretzel pieces, a swirl, or a fruit pairing.
