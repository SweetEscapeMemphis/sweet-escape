What to Order at Sweet Escape: A First-Timer's Guide

Published August 28, 2026 · Updated September 29, 2026 · Sweet Escape, Memphis

Your first visit to Sweet Escape does not need to begin with memorizing a wall of flavor names. Start with a smaller question: do you want a scoop, a frozen yogurt cup you finish with toppings, or a dessert built around ice cream? That answer gives you a useful starting point before you explore the rest of our Memphis menu.

Our Whitten Road shop brings together hand-dipped ice cream, self-serve frozen yogurt, gelato, sorbetto, milkshakes, and specialty treats. Those choices serve different dessert moods. This guide walks you through the formats, explains a few easy-to-miss distinctions, and helps you arrive with an idea you are excited about rather than a long list you feel obliged to study.

Choose the kind of dessert before the flavor

Think about what you want to do with your dessert. A scoop keeps the focus on one ice cream choice. Self-serve frozen yogurt lets you start with a swirl and finish at the topping station. A cookie sandwich puts ice cream between two chocolate-chip cookies. A warm base adds a baked component underneath a scoop. These are different starting points, even when a similar flavor name appears in more than one place.

You do not have to decide everything at once. If cookies are what brought you here, begin with the cookie formats. If you want to explore frozen desserts, compare the scoop and gelato menus. If choosing the finishing touches sounds fun, look at frozen yogurt. Picking a format first reduces the number of decisions without limiting your chance to discover something new.

Get your bearings with the live menu

The in-stock menu separates the reported lineup into scoops, yogurt, and gelato. It is the place to check which flavors the shop currently lists. Our larger catalogs serve a different purpose: they let you research product names and published details, including entries that are not in the current lineup.

Check the stock page's update time as well as its names. An online listing is a planning aid, not a reservation of a particular flavor. If your visit depends on one specific item, call before traveling. For a more flexible visit, choose one first choice and one alternative in the same category. You can save the catalogs for the few flavors that actually interest you instead of opening every card.

When you want hand-dipped ice cream

Start with the scoop flavor catalog when ice cream is the main event. It includes names such as Chocolate, Cookie Dough, Oregon Strawberry, and Pistachio Almond, alongside many other documented options. These examples introduce the catalog; they do not mean every flavor is available every day.

Search for a flavor you already recognize, then look at the individual product card. The card's serving information and allergen fields are more useful than guessing from the name. You might also notice sherbet or other product types within the broader scoop catalog. Read the item's category rather than treating everything on that page as the same kind of ice cream.

A familiar name can be a helpful anchor on a first visit. There is no requirement to order the most elaborate dessert or choose an unfamiliar flavor. Equally, if you came to explore, browsing one neighboring option gives you a manageable comparison instead of an entire menu to rank.

When you want to build a frozen yogurt cup

Self-serve frozen yogurt is a distinct part of Sweet Escape's menu. The published specialty description is straightforward: swirl your frozen yogurt, then visit the self-serve topping station to finish it your way. Begin with the yogurt catalog to learn about the flavors, and check the live lineup for the choices currently reported.

Keep the first decision simple: what do you want at the center of the cup? After that, decide whether you want toppings to play a supporting role or become a bigger part of your dessert. You can prefer a straightforward cup without treating the topping station as a checklist. Specific toppings and their availability should be checked in store; this guide is not a promise of a particular topping assortment.

The yogurt menu also includes products labeled as sorbet. Read those entries individually rather than assuming every option in the yogurt section has the same ingredients. For a closer look at the build-your-own format, our frozen yogurt cup guide is a useful next stop.

When gelato or sorbetto catches your eye

Our gelato and sorbetto catalog is separate from the scoop and yogurt pages. It organizes documented products by category and provides descriptions, available allergen information, and source details. Browse it when you want to compare that part of the menu rather than assuming a gelato flavor is simply another name for a scoop flavor.

Similar words can appear across categories without describing identical products. Birthday Cake yogurt and Birthday Cake gelato, for example, have separate entries. The same principle applies to fruit or chocolate names. Compare the specific product you intend to order, not just the broad theme. Some gelato records have less published nutrition information than others; missing information is a reason to verify details, not fill in the gaps yourself.

Meet the desserts built around a scoop

The specialties menu is where the dessert's structure becomes especially important. Cookie Dough Base is described as freshly baked chocolate-chip cookie dough with your choice of one ice cream scoop on top. Brownie Base pairs a warm brownie with one scoop. If you want to compare those two directly, our cookie dough and brownie base guide focuses on that decision.

A Cookie Ice Cream Sandwich takes another route: your choice of ice cream is pressed between two chocolate-chip cookies. Ice Cream Nachos are two scoops served with crisp round waffle chips for dipping. The names are playful, but the descriptions make the practical differences clear. One centers on cookies around the ice cream; the other gives you waffle chips alongside it.

Dubai Dream is listed as two scoops layered with Dubai chocolate filling, pistachio cream, and crisp kataifi. Read the complete description before choosing it, particularly if any component raises an ingredient question. The published format does not establish which substitutions are possible. Ask about changes instead of assuming that any base, filling, or frozen dessert can be exchanged for another.

A flavor name and a dessert name are not interchangeable

Cookie Dough ice cream and Cookie Dough Base are a good example. The first is a scoop-catalog product; the second is a specialty with a baked base and a scoop on top. Knowing which one you mean makes it easier to browse the right page and explain your choice when you arrive.

Likewise, a photograph shows an example, not necessarily the full range of current choices. Use the written specialty description to understand what defines the dessert, then confirm the available ice cream options. If a milkshake is what you have in mind, ask about its current choices rather than assuming every catalog item can be made into one.

Handle ingredient questions before the final decision

Take a moment to read the product's ingredients and allergen notes if those details affect your choice. Our catalogs include published information and cautions, but they are not a guarantee of dietary suitability or freedom from cross-contact. A category name such as vegan, sorbetto, or no sugar added does not answer every question about the finished dessert.

For a specialty, the base, scoop, and additional components all matter. Information for the ice cream alone is not a complete statement for the assembled treat. Ask the shop to verify the current labels and discuss relevant handling questions before ordering. The nutrition and allergen guide explains how to read the different catalog fields without confusing them.

Make the last step an easy one

Sweet Escape is at 1674 Whitten Road, Suite 106, Memphis, TN 38134, near Bartlett. Regular hours are noon to 9 p.m. Monday through Thursday and noon to 10 p.m. Friday through Sunday. The Visit page brings together directions, hours, and the phone number, (901) 718-5812.

Before leaving, keep three things in mind: your preferred dessert format, a flavor you want to check, and any question you need answered. That is enough preparation for a first visit. You can leave the rest of the catalog for another day and enjoy finding your own starting point at Sweet Escape.
