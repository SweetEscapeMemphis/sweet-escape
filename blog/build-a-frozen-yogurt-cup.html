How to Build Your Frozen Yogurt Cup at Sweet Escape

Published September 3, 2026 · Updated September 30, 2026 · Sweet Escape, Memphis

A self-serve frozen yogurt cup gives you two decisions in a row: choose the yogurt, then decide whether toppings belong in the picture. At Sweet Escape in Memphis, that order is useful because the base and the finishing touches have different product details and may have different availability. A simple plan lets you enjoy the choices without assuming a topping or flavor will be in the shop every day.

This guide focuses on the choices you can make from the menu information Sweet Escape publishes. It walks from the live yogurt list to the individual product cards, then to the topping station. It does not promise a particular topping selection, portion, price, or ingredient-handling procedure. If one of those details affects your visit, ask the shop to verify it.

Begin with the live yogurt list

Open the live in-stock menu and choose the Yogurt tab. The page shows the yogurt flavors currently reported and the time the list was updated. Check that time as well as the flavor names, especially if you are planning around one particular choice.

A catalog entry and a current stock listing serve different purposes. The catalog is a reference for documented products and their published descriptions. The live menu is the place to see which items the shop reports at the time of its latest update. A flavor can appear in the catalog without being listed in stock today.

Choose a first option and, if flexibility matters, one alternative. You are not reserving either flavor by reading the website. For a specific must-have, call the shop before you travel.

Use the yogurt catalog to understand the base

After you find a yogurt name in the live list, open the yogurt catalog and search for that exact flavor. The card can include a description, ingredients, nutrition information, declared allergens, and facility note. Not every question has a published answer, and the catalog should be read as product information rather than a guarantee about a future cup.

Check the category label attached to the flavor. The yogurt page includes entries grouped as Yogurt, Nonfat, No Sugar Added, Seasonal, and Sorbet. These labels help you navigate the records; they do not replace the ingredient statement. A Nonfat product may still list milk, and a sorbet entry within the yogurt page remains its own category.

When a card includes a nutrition panel, read its serving amount before looking at the numbers. Manufacturer serving information does not automatically describe the amount you dispense into a self-serve cup. Additions require their own product details and amounts for a complete comparison.

Choose the base before planning toppings

First decide what you want the frozen yogurt itself to contribute. A flavor whose published description appeals to you can be the center of the cup. You can also browse a category or try a different name that is currently listed. There is no requirement to pick the most elaborate combination or to add toppings at all.

Choosing the base first gives you a reference point. If you decide on toppings afterward, you can consider whether an addition complements that flavor, changes the direction, or is not needed. That is a way to make your own decision, not a claim that any particular pairing will taste a certain way.

If you want to compare similar names, keep the full name and category together. Birthday Cake yogurt and Birthday Cake gelato, for instance, are separate catalog entries. The ingredients and descriptions for one should not be transferred to the other simply because their names share a theme.

Make a toppings plan that can adapt

Sweet Escape describes its specialty as a self-serve yogurt cup followed by a self-serve topping station. The website does not publish a guaranteed daily toppings list. Browse the in-store selection when you arrive rather than setting a plan around an ingredient you have not confirmed.

One flexible method is to decide what role you want toppings to play before choosing them. Do you want the yogurt to stay most noticeable, or do you want to build a combination where several additions share the attention? That question helps you stop at the point that feels right for you.

You can also make one choice at a time. Look at the available options, consider whether each addition answers something you actually want from the cup, and leave the rest. There is no “correct” number of toppings or required recipe listed on the menu. The cup is yours to finish in a way that suits your preference and the options available.

If the base already has a distinct flavor description, you may want to keep the rest of the cup straightforward. If you are simply curious about exploring, choose additions based on what is available at the station. This guide cannot promise exact textures, combinations, or results.

Consider a plain first taste

If you have not tried the yogurt flavor before, starting with the base on its own can help you understand what the description refers to. You can add toppings afterward if you want them. This is an option for learning your preference, not a prescribed way to order.

It can be especially helpful when comparing a flavor with a similar product elsewhere in the catalog. Try to understand which base you selected before layering in other items. For example, Cookie Monster is described as yogurt inspired by frosted cookies, while Cookies 'N Cream identifies chocolate sandwich cookies in its ingredient information. Our comparison guide explains that distinction in more detail.

You do not need to conduct a formal taste test. A simple first choice and a note to yourself about what you liked are enough if you want to remember the flavor for another visit.

Read allergy information for every part of the cup

If ingredients or allergens matter to you, review the exact yogurt entry and its full published cautions. The catalog's allergen filter is a lookup tool, not a “safe for” certification. A blank field or a “No” in a status table does not establish freedom from cross-contact.

Toppings introduce separate ingredients and handling questions. The website does not provide a full daily topping list or a complete allergen statement for every possible finished combination. Ask the shop to verify the current product labels and discuss the handling details that matter before choosing.

Our nutrition and allergen guide explains how to distinguish product declarations, facility notes, and information gaps. It is particularly useful when you are comparing two bases or deciding whether to add a component.

Neither a sorbet category, a No Sugar Added label, nor a vegan description answers every dietary question. Read the specific entry and verify current labels. The website alone cannot declare that a product or a custom cup is suitable for an individual's needs.

Keep nutrition numbers attached to the right serving

Nutrition information belongs to the product and serving amount shown on its label. A yogurt card's serving measure is not automatically the same quantity as your finished self-serve cup. Toppings have their own labels and amounts, so a complete calculation would require information for each part.

Do not add numbers from different serving sizes and treat the result as a precise cup total. If the online panel does not provide the detail you need, ask whether the current container or topping label answers it. If there is no verified amount, keep that uncertainty visible rather than estimating from appearance.

The No Sugar Added guide is available if you are comparing that specific category. It explains why a label about added sugars should be read alongside total sugars and the full ingredient statement.

Build your cup around your own preference

A frozen yogurt cup can be simple, exploratory, or somewhere between. You might choose one base and leave it as is. You might sample a different category when it is listed. You might choose a topping because you want an additional component. None of those approaches needs a universal rule.

If the decision feels large, reduce it to two steps: which yogurt sounds good, and do you want anything from the topping station? You can answer the second question after you see what is actually available. That leaves your plan open while still giving you a direction.

For a broader view of all the dessert formats, our first-visit guide compares scoops, froyo, gelato, sorbetto, and specialties. If your group has different preferences, each person can browse the relevant section separately.

Plan the Memphis stop

Bring any ingredient questions that the product cards do not resolve, and leave the topping choice until you can see the current selection. That is enough planning to start your cup with a flavor you want and finish it with decisions you make in the moment.
