Fruit-Named Frozen Desserts: Find the Flavor and Format You Want

Published September 14, 2026 · Updated September 29, 2026 · Sweet Escape, Memphis

A fruit name can lead you to very different desserts at Sweet Escape. Strawberry might point you toward ice cream, frozen yogurt, cheesecake gelato, or sorbetto. Blueberry Burst has its own documented ice cream description, while Watermelon Sorbet belongs to another category altogether. The fruit is a helpful starting point, but it is only part of the choice.

If you are browsing for a fruit-named dessert in Memphis, begin with the combination you want: fruit with an ice cream base, fruit in a yogurt cup, a dessert-inspired gelato, or a sorbet entry. This guide follows those paths and shows where names can overlap without describing identical products.

Start with a fruit, then choose the category

Searching a fruit name is useful when you already know the direction you want. The next step is to keep the category attached to each result. A Strawberry ice cream card and a Wild Strawberry sorbetto card are not substitutes for each other's product information, even if both appear relevant to your search.

The documented item directory brings the catalog entries together, while the separate scoop, yogurt, and gelato pages provide their respective details. The directory is a research tool. It is not a declaration that every item can be ordered on the day you open it.

You can make the search manageable by choosing one fruit and one format first. That gives you a short list to investigate rather than asking yourself to compare every fruit-related product on every menu.

Blueberry Burst has a specific published description

Blueberry Burst is categorized as Ice Cream, and its record describes a mascarpone-style ice cream with blueberry swirl and blueberry pieces. That is more informative than the name alone: it tells you the documented base style and the two blueberry components in the description.

The phrase “mascarpone-style” should stay intact. It is not a reason to invent a complete recipe or claim a particular amount of mascarpone cheese. The available record declares milk and includes additional equipment-related cautions for peanuts, tree nuts, wheat, soy, and eggs. Read those notes with the product information rather than treating a fruit-forward name as a dietary claim.

If blueberry is the part of the menu you came to explore, begin with that exact card and then check the live stock list. There is no need to move to a different fruit category unless you want an alternative.

Strawberry ice cream has more than one name

The scoop catalog includes Strawberry, Oregon Strawberry, Strawberry Cheesecake, and Chocolate Covered Strawberry. These are separate product records. The extra words are useful because they identify the exact entry, not because they give us permission to infer every ingredient from the name.

The published allergen information differs. Strawberry declares milk. Oregon Strawberry declares egg and milk. Strawberry Cheesecake declares milk, eggs, and wheat. Chocolate Covered Strawberry declares milk and soy. Additional cautions or missing-information notes should also be read on the relevant card.

These distinctions do not establish that one choice is suitable for someone avoiding a particular allergen. They show why a broad request for “strawberry” is not enough for ingredient research. Keep the complete product name with the information you save, and verify the current label if it affects your decision.

Peaches and cheesecake names offer other scoop directions

Peaches & Cream appears as an Ice Cream entry and declares egg and milk in the available record. The record does not give us a verified recipe, fruit percentage, or preparation method. Its name introduces the product; it does not answer all the questions you might have about it.

Raspberry Chip Cheesecake is another documented Ice Cream entry, with milk and soy declared and an equipment-related note covering peanuts, tree nuts, wheat, and eggs. Lemon Cheesecake Bar has a different declaration: milk, soy, wheat, and eggs, with peanuts and tree nuts in the additional note.

If the dessert-inspired part is more important to you than choosing a particular fruit, our cake and cheesecake flavor guide is the more focused next step. Here, the useful distinction is that fruit can be one element within a broader named dessert flavor.

Strawberry frozen yogurts deserve their own comparison

In the yogurt catalog, Strawberry Sensations is described with strawberry puree. Its ingredient statement identifies the puree component and lists milk, nonfat milk, whey, and cream among the other ingredients. It is a separate product from the strawberry scoop entries.

Wild Strawberry yogurt belongs to the No Sugar Added category. Its statement includes natural and artificial strawberry flavor, maltodextrin, sorbitol, and sucralose, alongside milk ingredients and other components. It should not be treated as a different size or spelling of Strawberry Sensations.

Both yogurt records declare milk and carry a facility note about processing peanuts, tree nuts, eggs, soy, milk, and wheat products. No Sugar Added is not the same as no total sugar, and a strawberry name does not establish that a product is fruit-only. Open the full statement rather than relying on the front of the card.

Three fruit-named sorbets in the yogurt catalog

Pink Lemonade Sorbet, Valencia Orange Sorbet, and Watermelon Sorbet appear in the yogurt catalog under Sorbet. That category matters: appearing on the yogurt page does not make them yogurt products. It identifies where they are organized on the website.

Valencia Orange Sorbet's published statement includes orange juice concentrate and natural orange flavor, among the other ingredients. Watermelon Sorbet includes a watermelon flavor syrup whose components include pear juice concentrate and natural and artificial flavors. Pink Lemonade Sorbet lists natural pink lemonade flavor within its statement.

Those examples explain why a fruit name is not a complete ingredient list. The product descriptions can help you narrow your interest, but they do not establish that a flavor is made exclusively from the fruit in its name. The sorbet entries also retain the published facility caution; their status tables should not be interpreted as cross-contact guarantees.

For a closer look at those specific products, the yogurt-menu sorbet guide keeps that comparison in one place.

Fruit can be paired with gelato's other components

The gelato catalog describes NY Strawberry Cheesecake with strawberry swirls and graham-crust pieces. Its record lists milk and cautions that crust components may introduce wheat or soy. Wild Strawberry Swirl is a different gelato entry, described with strawberries and a strawberry ribbon, and its record lists milk.

These are distinct from Wild Strawberry sorbetto, which appears in the Sorbetto category. Similar names can make it easy to open the wrong card, particularly when you are moving quickly between pages. Check whether the entry says Gelato or Sorbetto before using its details.

A flavor description can tell you that a product includes a swirl or a crust component. It does not establish a serving's exact composition or replace a complete current label. If the additional component is what attracts you—or what raises a question—make that part of the conversation before ordering.

Alphonso Mango and Wild Strawberry sorbetto

Alphonso Mango is documented as Sorbetto, with a description centered on Alphonso mango. Wild Strawberry is also documented as Sorbetto. In both cases, the available notes explain that a complete major-allergen statement was not published online and direct visitors to verify the current container label.

That limitation is important. An empty online allergen list is not a confirmed absence of allergens. The word sorbetto also does not answer every question about ingredients or handling. If you want to explore the category itself, our sorbetto guide provides the broader context without treating all entries as identical.

Choose the fruit direction, then the dessert category, then one exact product. That sequence keeps the comparison practical. You might investigate blueberry ice cream without comparing it to every sorbet, or look at strawberry gelato without treating a yogurt entry as an equivalent replacement.
