Vanilla Ice Cream, Frozen Yogurt, and Gelato: Find Your Starting Point

Published September 13, 2026 · Updated September 29, 2026 · Sweet Escape, Memphis

Vanilla is a flavor family, not a single product. At Sweet Escape in Memphis, the documented choices include several vanilla ice creams, vanilla frozen yogurts, and vanilla entries in the gelato catalog. If you have ever searched for “vanilla” and wondered why more than one card appeared, the answer is that those cards describe different products—not interchangeable names for the same dessert.

This guide helps you choose where to begin. Perhaps you want a familiar scoop, perhaps you want a vanilla yogurt cup, or perhaps you are looking at a specialty with ice cream on top. Start with that format, then compare the exact vanilla entry. You can keep the decision enjoyable without overlooking the details that distinguish one option from another.

Meet the vanilla names in the scoop catalog

The hand-dipped flavor catalog includes Vanilla, Vanilla Bean, French Vanilla, and Signature Vanilla as separate ice cream records. It also includes Vanilla Yogurt, categorized as Yogurt. Seeing all five together is a useful reminder to read the category as well as the name.

The available scoop records provide nutrition and allergen information, but they do not supply a complete flavor-by-flavor ingredient narrative. That means we should not invent a ranking of sweetness, intensity, or vanilla quality from the names alone. “Signature” is a product name, not proof of a particular manufacturing method, and “Vanilla Bean” is not a reason to apply another product's label to it.

If you want to compare these entries, keep the full names in your notes. “French Vanilla ice cream” identifies a different record from “French Vanilla yogurt.” The extra word saves confusion when you move between catalogs or ask whether a choice is currently available.

What the published scoop records actually show

Vanilla, Vanilla Bean, and French Vanilla each have a published reference serving of 2/3 cup, or 95 grams, with 210 calories. Identical figures in those fields do not establish identical recipes. For example, the Vanilla and Vanilla Bean records declare milk, while the French Vanilla record declares milk and eggs. All have additional equipment-related allergen notes that must be read separately.

Signature Vanilla's documented serving is also described as 2/3 cup, but its listed weight is 136 grams. That difference matters if you are comparing nutrition numbers. A matching household measure does not necessarily mean a matching weight. Use the complete serving line rather than comparing a single number in isolation.

These scoop records draw on source information dated in 2018 and 2019. They are useful reference points, not confirmation that a current container has an unchanged label. When nutrition or ingredients are important to your decision, verify the current product information before ordering. This guide does not estimate the weight of a scoop served in the shop.

French Vanilla yogurt is its own choice

In the frozen yogurt catalog, French Vanilla is described as a full-bodied vanilla with a custard-like character. Its published ingredient statement includes milk, nonfat milk, cream, natural and artificial vanilla, and yogurt cultures, along with other listed ingredients. The description is an introduction; the complete statement is where the product details live.

The yogurt allergen table marks milk “Yes” and egg “No.” That is different from the French Vanilla ice cream declaration. It also comes with a facility note: the product is manufactured in a facility that processes peanuts, tree nuts, eggs, soy, milk, and wheat products. A “No” in a table is not a promise about cross-contact or a substitute for discussing your needs with the shop.

Choose this part of the menu when the self-serve yogurt format is what you want to explore. Sweet Escape's published froyo specialty lets you swirl a base and finish it at the topping station. The yogurt's product information describes the base, not the finished cup after additions.

Tahitian Vanilla and Tahitian Vanilla (NSA) are different entries

Two especially easy names to confuse are Tahitian Vanilla and Tahitian Vanilla (NSA). The first appears in the Nonfat category. The second appears in No Sugar Added. Keep the qualifier attached to the name: dropping “NSA” loses a meaningful distinction between the documented products.

The regular Tahitian Vanilla statement includes liquid sugar and corn syrup. The NSA statement instead lists ingredients including maltodextrin, sorbitol, and sucralose. Both records include milk ingredients and declare milk. These are examples of why reading a category and a complete ingredient statement together is more informative than using a short name alone.

No Sugar Added is not a blanket statement that the product has no total sugar or fits every dietary need. If that wording is central to your choice, use our no-sugar-added guide and verify the current label. Neither vanilla name gives you a reason to assume the two products share the same nutrition panel.

Where Home Churn Vanilla fits

Home Churn Vanilla is another yogurt-catalog entry. Its description points toward an old-fashioned vanilla character, and its ingredient list includes milk, cream, nonfat milk, whey, artificial vanilla, and yogurt cultures among the components. It has a separate record from French Vanilla and both Tahitian Vanilla entries.

The product name does not establish that Sweet Escape churns it in the shop. That distinction matters because an evocative name can sound like a description of how a business makes something. Here, use it as the name of the documented yogurt flavor. If you are choosing between vanilla yogurts, the complete product cards are the reliable starting point.

Vanilla also appears in the gelato collection

The gelato catalog includes Madagascar Vanilla Bean, described with Madagascar bourbon vanilla, cream, milk, and cane sugar. Its published nutrition reference uses a 1/2-cup, 80-gram serving, rather than the 2/3-cup serving shown on several scoop records. That is another reason not to turn a quick glance at two calorie figures into a direct comparison.

A separate Vegan Vanilla entry is described as non-dairy gelato highlighting Madagascar vanilla bean. Its record also states that a complete major-allergen statement was not published online and asks visitors to verify the current container label. The category is useful for finding the product, but the incomplete record should not be interpreted as a complete allergy statement.

These entries broaden the vanilla choices you can research. They do not mean both are currently in the case. The item directory remains a reference for documented products, while the live stock list answers a different question.

Check which vanilla option is listed now

When this guide was updated on September 29, the live stock data carried a September 25 update time and listed French Vanilla under yogurt. The vanilla scoop names and vanilla gelato names discussed here were not listed in that snapshot. That is a dated observation, not a prediction about a later visit.

Open the current in-stock menu close to your trip and look in the appropriate section. If you want a particular vanilla product, check the full name and category together. Call before traveling when that exact choice is essential; a catalog entry or an older article should not be treated as a guarantee of availability.

Think about vanilla's role in your dessert

Sometimes the decision begins with the flavor. Other times it begins with the dessert around it. Sweet Escape lists Cookie Dough Base and Brownie Base with one ice cream scoop, Cookie Ice Cream Sandwiches with ice cream between two chocolate-chip cookies, and Ice Cream Nachos with two scoops and waffle chips.

If you are considering one of those formats, choose the specialty first and then ask about the available ice cream choices for it. A vanilla yogurt listing does not establish that yogurt can replace a scoop in a specialty. Likewise, a photograph featuring vanilla does not promise that the photographed flavor is available or required.

For a self-serve cup, you can decide whether you want the vanilla base to remain the focus or whether you want additions to define more of the dessert. Specific toppings should be checked in store. There is no need to add something simply because a topping station is part of the experience.

Bring a precise choice, not a complicated plan

Before visiting, narrow your notes to the full product name, its format, and any question you need answered. That might be “French Vanilla yogurt” rather than a general request for vanilla. Read the ingredient and allergen details for that exact product, and include every component if you are considering a specialty.
