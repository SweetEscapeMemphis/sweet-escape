What the menu says the sandwich includes

Sweet Escape describes its Cookie Ice Cream Sandwich as your choice of ice cream pressed between two chocolate-chip cookies. On the specialties page, the item is labeled Hand-built. How it differs from a Cookie Dough Base

The Cookie Dough Base and the cookie sandwich both reference a cookie, but they are described as different specialties. The Cookie Dough Base is freshly baked chocolate-chip cookie dough with your choice of one ice cream scoop on top. It is labeled Warm and gooey. The sandwich uses two chocolate-chip cookies with the chosen ice cream pressed between them.

That structural difference can help you choose. In the base dessert, the documented foundation is a warm cookie-dough base and the scoop sits on top. In the sandwich, the cookies form the outside and the scoop is held between them. The website does not publish every operational detail of preparation, so there is no need to infer bake time, temperature at serving, or how long either item takes to prepare. Compare only the structure Sweet Escape actually states.

If you are deciding between them, ask yourself which format sounds more appealing: a scoop on a warm base or ice cream between two cookies. Then check the current menu and use the exact product cards for the scoop choice. Those steps give you a grounded comparison without guessing about size, shareability, or how the dessert will be packaged.

How it differs from Brownie Base and Ice Cream Nachos

The Brownie Base is described as a warm brownie finished with your choice of one ice cream scoop. Its menu label calls it a fudgy favorite. This is another base-and-scoop format, but the documented base is brownie rather than cookie dough. As with the cookie base, use the product’s own page if you need current component or label information.

Ice Cream Nachos are a different arrangement again: two scoops of your choice served with crisp round waffle chips for dipping. The published description gives the number of scoops and the accompanying chips. It does not say that the dish includes cookies or that it is interchangeable with a sandwich. The specialties menu provides a useful side-by-side look at these formats.

These comparisons can help when you know you want ice cream but have not decided how it should be served. A cookie sandwich is defined by two cookies surrounding a scoop; a base specialty puts a scoop on cookie dough or brownie; nachos pair two scoops with waffle chips. Each description is concise, but the product names and component details make the differences clear.
