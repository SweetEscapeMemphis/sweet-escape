Butter Pecan Flavors in Memphis: Three Sweet Escape Choices

Published October 3, 2026 · Sweet Escape, Memphis

At Sweet Escape, “butter pecan” can point to three separate entries: Butter Pecan and Butter Pecan - No Sugar Added in the scoop catalog, plus Maple Roasted Butter Pecan in the gelato catalog. The names are related, but the records publish different details. This guide helps you find the exact item without guessing from a title or photograph.

Start with the category and full product name. The scoop entries have dated nutrition panels; the gelato page has a more descriptive sentence but no complete numeric panel. The records help identify each choice, not rank them or predict an individual flavor experience.

At a glance: three distinct menu entries

These are separate records. Two belong to the hand-dipped scoop catalog; the third is filed under gelato. “No Sugar Added” is part of one exact name, not a description of the other entries.

Butter Pecan: the standard scoop record

Butter Pecan is listed in the Ice Cream category. Its item page does not provide a long recipe or tasting narrative. The linked nutrition record gives the serving basis and nutrient values; the allergen section lists milk and pecans under “Contains,” plus a shared-equipment note. That is the documented detail—not a basis for inventing texture or a fuller ingredient list.

Open this exact record when you mean the standard scoop. The nutrition source is dated June 12, 2019, so it is a dated reference, not a guarantee that current packaging is unchanged. The page advises guests to verify the current container label when an allergy matters.

Butter Pecan - No Sugar Added: keep the full name attached

The second scoop is filed under No Sugar Added with the exact name Butter Pecan - No Sugar Added. It has its own nutrition record. Keep the full name when searching or asking about it so it is not confused with the standard scoop.

Its June 18, 2019 panel lists a 2/3 cup (85 g) serving: 160 calories, 7 g total fat, 3 g saturated fat, 115 mg sodium, 20 g total carbohydrate, less than 1 g fiber, 7 g total sugars, 0 g added sugars, and 4 g protein. “No Sugar Added” does not mean zero total sugars or establish suitability for an individual diet. If a label detail matters, check current packaging and ask about this exact item.

The allergen section lists milk and pecans under “Contains,” with a shared-equipment note for peanuts, other tree nuts, wheat, soy, and eggs. Similar caution fields do not make the two scoop products interchangeable; their names and nutrition records are separate.

Maple Roasted Butter Pecan: a gelato entry with its own description

Maple Roasted Butter Pecan is filed under gelato. Its published description says “butter-pecan gelato with crunchy candied pecans and a maple-syrup ribbon.” That gives a grounded picture of the named components without implying proportions or extra ingredients.

The page lists milk and tree nut (pecan) and asks guests to verify the current container label. It does not provide a complete numeric nutrition panel, so there is no published basis for adding calories, sugars, fat, or serving size to the two-scoop comparison. Use its gelato page for this item and the matching scoop page for either of the other names.

Frequently asked questions

Are Sweet Escape’s three butter-pecan products the same flavor?

No. They are separate records: two scoop entries and one gelato entry. Use the exact page for the item you mean.

Does “No Sugar Added” mean zero total sugars?

No. Its dated panel lists 7 g total sugars and 0 g added sugars per 2/3 cup (85 g). Check the current label because information can change.

Can I compare nutrition for all three?

The two scoop pages link to numeric panels with different gram weights and dates. Maple Roasted Butter Pecan has no complete numeric panel online, so the records do not support a three-way comparison.

What if I have a pecan or milk allergy?

All three list milk and pecans; the scoop pages also include a shared-equipment caution. Ask Sweet Escape to confirm current labels and handling. Online information cannot guarantee freedom from cross-contact.
