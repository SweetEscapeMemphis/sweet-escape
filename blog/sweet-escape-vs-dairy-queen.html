Sweet Escape vs. Dairy Queen: A Frozen-Dessert Comparison

Published September 22, 2026 · Sweet Escape, Memphis

Sweet Escape and Dairy Queen both appear in frozen-dessert searches, but their official menus organize the experience differently. Dairy Queen is a quick-service restaurant system closely associated with soft serve and blended Blizzard Treats. Sweet Escape’s Memphis website starts with several dessert formats: hand-dipped ice cream, self-serve frozen yogurt, gelato, sorbetto, milkshakes, and specialty desserts. This comparison sticks to documented facts so you can choose the format that fits your visit.

Dairy Queen centers soft serve and Blizzard Treats

Dairy Queen’s official history says its first restaurant opened in Joliet, Illinois, in 1940. Shakes and malts arrived in 1949, while Blizzard Treats were introduced in 1985. The company describes itself as a quick-service restaurant system, which matters: its stores may combine frozen treats with a broader food menu rather than operating only as dessert counters.

Official Dairy Queen product pages describe Blizzard Treats as soft serve blended with mix-ins. For example, the OREO Cookie Blizzard Treat page pairs cookie pieces with soft serve and specifically says availability is limited to participating locations. That location note is important. A national product page is useful background, but the relevant store’s current menu is the better source for what can actually be ordered.

Sweet Escape separates several dessert formats

Sweet Escape lets visitors browse by dessert type. The scoop catalog documents hand-dipped ice cream. The frozen yogurt catalog covers self-serve flavors, and the gelato catalog includes gelato and sorbetto entries. Milkshakes and cookie-based loaded treats appear on the specialties page. Blizzard is a Dairy Queen trademark, so Sweet Escape does not describe its milkshakes or specialties with that name.

Those Sweet Escape catalogs are reference pages, not daily inventory promises. Check the live in-stock menu close to your trip for the latest published scoop, yogurt, and gelato availability. If an allergy or dietary need matters, read each product’s published cautions and ask the shop to verify the current container label; a category, flavor name, or blank field is not a safety guarantee.

Which format fits your plans?

Dairy Queen may match a search centered on soft serve, Blizzard Treats, or a quick-service restaurant menu. Sweet Escape may fit someone who wants to compare hand-dipped scoops with self-serve frozen yogurt, gelato, sorbetto, milkshakes, and specialty desserts at one Memphis shop. That is a difference in menu structure, not a claim that either experience is universally better.

Sweet Escape is at 1674 Whitten Rd, Suite 106, Memphis, TN 38134, near Bartlett. Posted hours are Monday–Thursday noon–9 PM and Friday–Sunday noon–10 PM. Check the live menu before leaving home because current availability can change.

Frequently asked questions

What is the main menu difference between Sweet Escape and Dairy Queen?

Dairy Queen’s official history and menu emphasize soft serve, Blizzard Treats, shakes, malts, and other restaurant items. Sweet Escape documents hand-dipped ice cream, self-serve frozen yogurt, gelato, sorbetto, milkshakes, and specialty desserts.

Does Sweet Escape serve Blizzard Treats?

No. Blizzard is a Dairy Queen trademark. Sweet Escape’s website lists milkshakes and its own specialty desserts, but it does not represent them as Blizzard Treats.

How can I check Sweet Escape’s current choices?

Use Sweet Escape’s live in-stock menu close to your visit. Its catalogs document a broader selection, while the live menu shows the latest published availability.

Is Sweet Escape affiliated with Dairy Queen?

No. Sweet Escape is not affiliated with or endorsed by Dairy Queen.
