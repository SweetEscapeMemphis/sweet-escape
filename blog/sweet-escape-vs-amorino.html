Sweet Escape vs. Amorino: A Gelato Guide

Published September 25, 2026 · Sweet Escape, Memphis

Sweet Escape and Amorino both give gelato a prominent place, but they organize the visit differently. Amorino’s signature presentation shapes gelato into flower-like petals in a cup or cone. Sweet Escape treats gelato and sorbetto as part of a broader Memphis dessert menu that also includes hand-dipped ice cream, self-serve frozen yogurt, milkshakes, and specialty desserts. This guide compares those documented formats without turning personal preference into a quality ranking.

Amorino centers its experience on gelato

Amorino’s official history says childhood friends Cristiano Sereni and Paolo Benassi opened the first shop on Paris’s Île Saint-Louis in 2002. The company describes gelato as its signature product and says team members use a spatula to shape it into a flower. Customers can choose multiple flavors as petals, according to that page.

The same official source lists cups, cones, gelato-filled macarons, hot and cold beverages, milkshakes, and sorbet drinks among the brand’s formats. Amorino’s U.S. flavor directory separates gelato and vegan sorbets and publishes allergen markers with individual listings. Those pages describe a brand-level collection, not guaranteed inventory at every shop, so location-specific availability still needs confirmation.

Sweet Escape offers gelato within a wider menu

Sweet Escape’s gelato and sorbetto catalog gathers the shop’s documented collection, including product details and the cautions attached to those records. The catalog is useful for research, while the live in-stock menu is the better page to check near a visit. A catalog entry should never be read as a promise that the item is available today.

The choice does not stop at gelato. Visitors can browse the hand-dipped scoop catalog, review self-serve frozen yogurt choices, or explore milkshakes and cookie-based loaded desserts on the specialties page. The complete item directory brings every verified catalog entry together when a group wants to compare categories before leaving home.

Ingredient and allergen needs deserve more than assumptions based on a product name or category. Read the published item details and facility cautions, then ask the relevant shop to verify current labels and handling conditions. Neither this comparison nor a “gelato,” “sorbet,” or “vegan” label establishes personal dietary safety or freedom from cross-contact.

Which format fits your dessert plans?

Amorino may appeal to someone specifically researching the brand’s flower presentation, multi-flavor petal concept, or gelato-centered treats. Sweet Escape may suit a Memphis or Bartlett group that wants to compare gelato and sorbetto with scoops, self-serve frozen yogurt, milkshakes, and specialty desserts at one stop. The meaningful distinction is the documented menu structure and presentation—not an unsupported claim that one is universally better.

Sweet Escape is at 1674 Whitten Rd, Suite 106, Memphis, TN 38134, near Bartlett. Posted hours are Monday–Thursday noon–9 PM and Friday–Sunday noon–10 PM.

Frequently asked questions

Yes. Sweet Escape publishes a gelato and sorbetto catalog, while its live in-stock menu shows the latest documented availability.
