What people usually mean by a s’more

A familiar campfire s’more brings together a toasted marshmallow, chocolate, and graham crackers. The ingredients are simple, but the finished snack can vary: people choose different crackers, chocolate, and ways to heat or assemble it. A name that evokes s’mores can therefore point toward a recognizable combination without promising that every dessert named for it uses exactly the same pieces.

The history is easier to describe when we separate a documented recipe from an origin story. Girl Scouts of the USA’s Junior Girl Scout Way activity guide says a s’more recipe appeared in a 1927 Girl Scout handbook and presents “some more” as the explanation behind the name. Smithsonian Magazine likewise points to the 1927 handbook as an early written reference, while noting that the treat’s true origin is unclear. It is accurate to call that an early documented recipe; it would be too strong to claim the handbook proves who invented the snack.
