A scoop of history

A Brief History of Gelato and How to Browse It in Memphis

Published September 8, 2026 · Sweet Escape, Memphis

Gelato has a long story, but it is not a tidy tale with one undisputed inventor and one exact birthday. Reliable histories connect today's gelato to older frozen refreshments, Renaissance Italy, and later shops that helped the dessert travel. Here is a short, carefully sourced path through that history, followed by a practical way to explore gelato and sorbetto at Sweet Escape in Memphis.

Frozen refreshments came before modern gelato

People were cooling drinks and sweet mixtures with snow and ice long before modern refrigeration. The Carpigiani Gelato Museum traces that wider background through ancient Mediterranean practices. It describes Roman use of snow-filtering vessels and the Arab development of sugar syrups connected with early sorbets. These preparations were not necessarily the creamy gelato served today, but they show that frozen refreshment has a much older family tree.

Renaissance Tuscany shaped the familiar story

Italy's Treccani encyclopedia says gelato was probably first made in Tuscany during the 16th century. It notes that the invention has been attributed to Florentine architect Bernardo Buontalenti, while also warning that people in several cultures likely knew how to chill sweet foods earlier. The Gelato Museum likewise credits Buontalenti with an egg-cream gelato and points to Francesco Redi and Lorenzo Magalotti as writers who later described ingredients and celebrated the dessert.

Paris helped the tradition reach a wider audience

The story then moves beyond Italy. Treccani describes Italian frozen desserts reaching France in the 1500s and spreading from there. The museum's timeline highlights Francesco Procopio Cutò, a Sicilian who sold sorbets at his Paris café. That chapter matters because it turns the history from a courtly or regional specialty into something served in a public gathering place and encountered by a broader audience.

Why the careful wording matters

Food histories often blend documented developments with stories repeated over generations. Both sources treat Buontalenti as credited or associated with an important step, not as proof that no similar frozen dessert existed before him. The most useful summary is that older cooling traditions came first, Renaissance Italy helped shape gelato's familiar form, and later makers carried it farther.
