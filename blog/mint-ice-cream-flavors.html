Mint Ice Cream, Frozen Yogurt, and Gelato: Which Choice Do You Mean?

Published September 14, 2026 · Updated September 29, 2026 · Sweet Escape, Memphis

If mint is the flavor you look for first, the next question is not simply “Do you have mint?” At Sweet Escape, mint-related names appear in the scoop, frozen yogurt, and gelato catalogs. A mint cookie ice cream, a mint chip ice cream, and a mint chocolate chip gelato are separate products. Knowing which one you mean makes browsing—and ordering—much easier.

This guide follows the mint choices through those menus. It focuses on what the published records actually tell you, where the information differs, and how to turn a preference for mint into a specific dessert choice. The examples are catalog entries, not a promise that every mint option is available together at our Memphis shop.

Start with the difference between a name and a format

“Mint” identifies a flavor direction. “Ice cream,” “yogurt,” and “gelato” identify different product categories. The words after mint can add another distinction: a cookie-named product is not necessarily the same as a chip-named one. Keep all of those parts together when comparing the cards.

In our scoop catalog, Cool Mint Cookie, Mint Chip, and O-O-Peppermint each have a separate record. The yogurt catalog has Mint Chocolate Chip, and the gelato catalog also has a Mint Chocolate Chip entry. Those last two share a name, but they should never share a label by assumption.

A useful note for your phone is therefore “Mint Chocolate Chip gelato,” not just “mint chip.” If you later open a different catalog, the format tells you whether you are still looking at the item you originally chose.

Cool Mint Cookie: begin with the scoop record

Cool Mint Cookie is categorized as Ice Cream. Its published record declares milk, soy, and wheat, with an additional equipment-related note naming peanuts, tree nuts, and eggs. Those are two different pieces of information: a declaration about the product and a separate caution that should remain visible alongside it.

The documented nutrition serving is 2/3 cup, or 95 grams. The reference lists 280 calories, 18 grams of total fat, 28 grams of carbohydrates, and 22 grams of total sugars for that serving. The source date is June 12, 2019. Treat those figures as published reference information to verify against the current label, not a newly measured description of a shop serving.

The scoop data does not provide a complete ingredient statement for this entry. We therefore do not invent a cookie brand, a cookie quantity, or a claim about how the mint flavor is made. The name helps you find the record; the current label is needed for ingredient details that the record does not supply.

Mint Chip is not another name for Cool Mint Cookie

Mint Chip has its own Ice Cream record. It declares milk and soy, while its additional equipment note names peanuts, tree nuts, wheat, and eggs. Compared with Cool Mint Cookie, the way wheat appears in the available information differs. That distinction is important to preserve, but it is not an assurance that either product is appropriate for someone avoiding wheat.

Its published serving is also 2/3 cup, or 95 grams. The reference lists 230 calories, 15 grams of total fat, 23 grams of carbohydrates, and 19 grams of total sugars. These figures come from a June 2019 source. They describe that documented serving, not the nutritional value of every possible dessert made with the flavor.

The practical takeaway is that the two scoop entries deserve separate attention. Do not choose one card because it happens to be the first mint result, then apply its information to the other. If your interest is specifically in a cookie-named flavor, retain “Cookie” in your search and in your question to the shop.

What about O-O-Peppermint?

O-O-Peppermint adds a third mint-related name to the scoop catalog. The record categorizes it as Ice Cream and declares milk, soy, and wheat. Its additional equipment note names peanuts, tree nuts, and eggs. As with the other scoop entries, read the complete record rather than treating the flavor family as one shared allergen profile.

The published information does not justify an invented description of candy pieces, a holiday-only schedule, or a comparison of mint strength. A peppermint name may suggest questions you would like answered, but it is not evidence for those answers. Look up the exact item and ask about the current product if its ingredient details will determine your choice.

This is also a good example of why a catalog is worth separating from a seasonal assumption. A product can remain documented online without being available on a particular date. The live menu, not an association between peppermint and a season, is the appropriate availability check.

Mint Chocolate Chip frozen yogurt

The yogurt catalog describes Mint Chocolate Chip as mint yogurt with chocolate pieces and categorizes it as Nonfat. Its ingredient statement includes nonfat milk, liquid sugar, corn syrup, whey, milk, and maltodextrin. A listed component called Choco Mint Droplets includes cocoa ingredients and peppermint oil, among its components.

This detailed statement gives you information that a short flavor name cannot. The published allergen table marks milk “Yes.” It also includes the manufacturing-facility caution about peanuts, tree nuts, eggs, soy, milk, and wheat products. The Nonfat category does not mean milk-free, and the other status-table entries do not cancel that facility note.

At Sweet Escape, the self-serve frozen yogurt format lets you start with a base and then visit the topping station. If mint yogurt is listed when you visit, you can decide whether the base itself is what you want to emphasize or whether you want additions. Check specific toppings in store rather than planning around an unverified list.

The gelato with the same name

Mint Chocolate Chip also appears in the gelato catalog, where its description identifies mint gelato with dark chocolate pieces. Its record lists milk and cautions that chocolate components may introduce soy. It directs visitors to verify the current container label before ordering.

That is a separate record from the yogurt entry. Do not transfer the yogurt's ingredient statement, category, or nutrition label to the gelato simply because both names match. The gelato record does not provide the same complete set of details, and an information gap should remain a question rather than becoming an assumed answer.

If you want to understand the broader format decision, our gelato, ice cream, and frozen yogurt comparison explains where each category fits. For a mint-focused visit, the important step is choosing which category you intend to investigate.

Choose what you want mint to do in the dessert

You may want mint to be the entire starting point, or you may be choosing an ice cream flavor for a larger specialty. Those are different decisions. Our specialties page lists formats such as Cookie Dough Base, Brownie Base, Cookie Ice Cream Sandwiches, and Ice Cream Nachos, each with its own written description.

When considering a specialty, ask which scoop choices are available for it. A gelato or yogurt listing does not establish that it can be substituted into a dessert described with ice cream. Likewise, the nutrition information for a mint scoop does not include the cookies, brownie, waffle chips, or other components of an assembled treat.

You do not need to decide that one mint product is objectively better than another. The useful comparison is whether you want the scoop format, the self-serve cup, or a gelato choice, and whether the exact product meets the questions you need answered.

A short checklist for a mint-focused visit

Keep the complete name, the product category, and one backup choice together. Read the declared allergens and additional cautions, and ask about the current label when ingredients matter. Neither a familiar name nor a blank online field establishes dietary suitability or freedom from cross-contact.

Then use the Visit page for directions and contact details. A little precision before you arrive leaves more room for the enjoyable part: finding the mint dessert you actually came to explore.
