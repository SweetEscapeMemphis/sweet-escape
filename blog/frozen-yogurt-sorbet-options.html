A closer look at sorbet

Sorbet at Sweet Escape: Four Catalog Choices to Explore

Published September 17, 2026 · Updated September 30, 2026 · Sweet Escape, Memphis

Sweet Escape’s frozen-yogurt catalog includes a separate Sorbet category. Four entries appear there: Mango Sorbet, Pink Lemonade Sorbet, Valencia Orange Sorbet, and Watermelon Sorbet. The names point to different product records, and those records include descriptions, ingredients, nutrition references, and allergen information. They are useful for deciding which card to read, but they do not tell you which item is being served today.

This guide brings together what the catalog actually documents and explains how to use it without turning a category label into a broader claim. You will see where the four records differ, what to review on each page, how to check current availability, and why an online allergen summary is not a substitute for a current package label. No flavor is promised to be in stock based on appearing in the directory.

What “Sorbet” means in this particular menu

On Sweet Escape’s frozen-yogurt page, Sorbet is one of the category buttons used to narrow the catalog. It is a way to find those product records within this menu. The category is not a daily stock update, and it does not make every item identical. Choose Sorbet to browse the entries filed there, then read the exact description and product details for the name you are considering.

The supplier, Honey Hill Farms, also groups these products under its Sorbets heading and describes the category as non-dairy and nonfat. Its official sorbet flavor directory describes the individual items. That first-party information helps explain the product category, but it does not confirm how a particular serving is handled at Sweet Escape or whether a particular item is currently available at the shop.

Sweet Escape maintains separate lists for yogurt flavors, scoop flavors, and gelato and sorbetto. Those menus can contain different products and fields. If you are comparing formats, use the page that corresponds to the product you mean: the frozen-yogurt menu for these four sorbet entries, the scoop list for hand-dipped options, and the gelato and sorbetto directory for that collection.

Four names, four records

Mango Sorbet has a catalog description that names real mango puree. The ingredient record lists water, liquid sugar, mango syrup concentrate containing mango puree, and other ingredients. If mango is the item you want to explore, open the Mango Sorbet page and read its own nutrition and ingredient links rather than borrowing information from another fruit entry.

Pink Lemonade Sorbet has a separate description identifying a lemonade-style citrus profile. Its record lists water, liquid sugar, corn syrup, natural pink lemonade flavor, citric acid, and other ingredients, including color additives. The exact wording belongs to this one item. Open the Pink Lemonade Sorbet page to view the product-specific record and linked nutrition source.

Valencia Orange Sorbet is described with Valencia orange flavor, and its ingredient record lists orange juice concentrate and natural orange flavor among the ingredients. That is a published product detail, not a guarantee about how pronounced the flavor will seem to an individual. Its dedicated card is the place to review the exact online information.

Watermelon Sorbet has its own description and ingredient statement, including watermelon flavor syrup and listed color ingredients. The name alone is not an ingredient declaration. Open the Watermelon Sorbet page for its separate record instead of assuming that it shares every detail with another sorbet.

How to compare the descriptions

The descriptions help identify each item: Mango names puree, Pink Lemonade references lemonade, Valencia Orange names an orange variety, and Watermelon is a separate melon-flavored option. These cues help you choose a card; they do not establish a full recipe, sourcing, sweetness, texture, or anyone’s personal taste.

For a side-by-side comparison, open each card and look at the same fields in the same order: product name, description, ingredients, allergen status, facility note, and nutrition source. When a supplier link is present, use it for the manufacturer’s latest product information. A page from Sweet Escape is a local catalog record; the supplier’s current packaging or direct confirmation is the better next step when an exact current ingredient detail matters.

A shared category does not mean identical formulas. Compare the exact current labels: an ingredient in one record cannot be assumed for another, and its absence from a short description does not prove it is absent from the full formula.

Read allergen fields with the separate facility note

Sweet Escape’s yogurt data records the listed major-allergen status for each of these four entries. The current online data shows “No” for the listed Milk, Egg, Wheat, Soy, Tree nut, Peanut, and Sesame fields. Each also carries a supplier facility note saying the product is manufactured in a facility that processes peanuts, tree nuts, eggs, soy, milk, and wheat products. Those are two different parts of the record: a declared product status and a separate facility statement.

Neither part should be read in isolation. A “No” in an online status field is not a promise that there is no possible contact, and a facility statement does not describe every detail of in-store service. Sweet Escape notes that handling at the shop can involve cross-contact. Ingredient labels and supplier information can change, so if you have an allergy, ask Sweet Escape to verify the current container and talk through the specific question before ordering.

The nutrition and allergen guide describes how to interpret declared fields, facility cautions, and incomplete information across the menu. Use it alongside the exact sorbet card, not instead of it. A category filter can narrow the list, but it cannot assess a person’s individual risk or certify a scoop as safe.

Nutrition panels are attached to individual items

Every sorbet entry has its own nutrition image and a product-specific PDF link. Open the relevant record rather than comparing one nutrition panel with another product’s name. Check the serving basis shown on the source and confirm that you are using the same basis if you compare values. Some records link directly to manufacturer-hosted files, which can provide the full panel beyond the brief summary visible in a card.

Nutrition facts are product information; they are not a recommendation about what a person should select. This guide does not rank the four sorbets or call any one of them healthier. If a nutrition value or ingredient must meet a particular need, use the current label for the exact product and consult an appropriate professional for personal dietary advice.

The supplier’s sorbet directory and Sweet Escape’s cards link to the available product sources. Because packaging can be updated, ask the shop to check the container currently in use if a file’s version is unclear.

Catalog presence is not current availability

A catalog entry explains that Sweet Escape documents the product. It does not mean the flavor is currently in the serving line. To check current reported availability, open the live in-stock menu, choose the Yogurt tab, and search the exact product name. Read the update time displayed with the list. If a particular choice is important and not shown, call the shop to ask whether staff can confirm its present status.

This is why an older article or saved menu should not be used as a same-day inventory report. The product catalog is more stable and contains product details; the stock page is a changing snapshot. A sorbet may remain documented even when it is not listed in the current snapshot, and the item page does not promise that a specific flavor is available on a future visit.

If you are planning broadly, browse all four names first and choose which item you want to ask about. If you are planning around a specific sorbet, check the live page close to your visit and confirm directly when necessary. The live-menu guide explains how to use its categories, search field, and timestamp.

A simple path from search to a useful answer

  1. Open the yogurt catalog and choose the Sorbet category.
  2. Search a full flavor name or browse Mango, Pink Lemonade, Valencia Orange, and Watermelon.
  3. Open the individual card and review its description, ingredients, allergen fields, facility note, and nutrition source.
  4. For allergy questions, have the shop check the current package and answer the handling question directly.
  5. Check the live stock page separately for current reported availability.

This sequence keeps the research practical: category first, exact product second, current availability last. It also makes it easier to ask a clear question if the online record leaves something unresolved. Give the shop the complete flavor name and tell them whether you are asking about stock, ingredients, or handling; those are distinct answers.

Explore the four catalog choices carefully

Sweet Escape’s Sorbet category provides four different yogurt-menu records: Mango, Pink Lemonade, Valencia Orange, and Watermelon. Their descriptions and ingredients are product-specific, their nutrition sources belong to their own cards, and their supplier notes deserve the same careful reading. The manufacturer’s category description offers additional context, but no online directory replaces the current label or a direct answer about shop handling.

Begin with the frozen-yogurt menu to browse the records. Use the stock page for today’s reported choices, and the Visit page for contact and directions if a detail needs confirmation. That approach lets you explore the four names without mistaking the catalog for a current menu or turning a broad category into an unsupported dietary promise.
