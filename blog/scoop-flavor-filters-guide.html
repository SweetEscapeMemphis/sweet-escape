Find the right catalog card

How to Search Sweet Escape’s Scoop Catalog

Published September 18, 2026 · Updated September 30, 2026 · Sweet Escape, Memphis

Sweet Escape’s scoop catalog is designed to help visitors find a documented flavor and open its product card. Search, category buttons, and a declared-allergen selector can narrow a large list, but each control answers a different question. Learning what they include—and what they leave out—makes browsing faster and helps prevent a filter result from being mistaken for a complete ingredient statement or a same-day stock report.

This walkthrough follows the controls on the full scoop catalog. It explains how to combine them, what information appears on a card, what an allergen filter cannot establish, and where to check availability. It is a guide to using Sweet Escape’s published menu tools, not a recommendation about what anyone should eat.

Start with the kind of answer you need

Before typing, decide whether you are browsing by name, narrowing by category, checking a declared-allergen field, or confirming current stock. The scoop catalog is a directory; the live menu is separate. Catalog search helps with discovery and product details, but does not show that a result is physically available today.

Known flavor name? Search. Browsing a general type? Choose a category. Reviewing disclosed allergens? The selector can locate cards with a particular declared value, but it is not an allergy-safety check. Keeping those tasks separate gives each page the right job.

Search checks the name, category, and declared-allergen text

The search field looks for text in three recorded places: a flavor’s name, its category, and the catalog’s declared-allergen field. For example, searching “mint” can find a flavor whose name contains Mint; searching “sherbet” can match the Sherbet category; and searching “milk” can match a product whose declared-allergen text includes Milk. Search terms are not interpreted as natural-language questions, so a long phrase may be less effective than a short product word.

The search does not scan every word in a supplier ingredient statement, nutrition panel, equipment caution, or paragraph on the page. A search for “may contain,” “peanuts,” or a particular ingredient is not a reliable way to find every card carrying that detail unless that text happens to be part of the searchable fields. When a specific ingredient matters, open the exact product card and read all its published fields rather than treating a search result—or the absence of one—as confirmation.

Try one clear term at a time. A partial name may be enough to bring up a likely entry; if the result is unclear, broaden the search or select the All category and browse. Names can be similar across formats, so note whether a result belongs to the scoop collection or to a separate yogurt or gelato catalog before relying on it.

Use category buttons to narrow the collection

The category controls include All, Ice Cream, Sherbet, Sorbet, Yogurt, and No Sugar Added. Selecting one keeps cards assigned to that category; selecting All returns the full scoop catalog view. Category is a menu classification, not a complete description of a product’s ingredients, nutrition, or current availability. A category label helps navigation, but the product’s own card carries the item-level information.

You can also open a category directly using a supported query such as the Sherbet view. This can be useful when sharing a browsing path or returning to a subset of the catalog. The page still contains product records, not a guarantee that every listed choice is in stock. For another category or the full collection, use the on-page buttons and check that the selected category is visibly active.

Similar labels may belong to different collections. Use category as a starting point, then verify the exact name and description. Sweet Escape’s frozen yogurt catalog and gelato and sorbetto catalog have their own records; a scoop search does not cover them.

Understand the declared-allergen selector

The scoop page’s declared-allergen selector offers Milk, Soy, Eggs, Wheat, Peanuts, and Tree nuts, as well as All. It filters by the card’s declared-allergen flags. This is deliberately more specific than searching every caution note, and that distinction matters: a card may show additional information about equipment or possible cross-contact in a separate field.

Choosing “Peanuts” is not a guarantee that every other card is peanut-free. It only narrows based on the declared-allergen data used by the control. A record may carry another caution, an incomplete online statement, or a request to verify the current package that is not represented by that selector. A result list cannot certify a product, scoop, utensil, or serving as safe for a person with an allergy.

Sweet Escape’s cards separate “Contains” from additional allergen or equipment notes. Read both sections for each exact flavor. If an allergy or ingredient question affects your decision, contact the shop and ask for the current container label and relevant handling information to be checked. The nutrition and allergen guide explains how to interpret those fields without extending one product’s information to another.

Filter controls work together

Search, category, and declared-allergen choices are combined: a card must match the typed search and the selected category and the selected declared allergen. This is useful when the full catalog is large. For example, selecting Sherbet and Milk narrows the page to records categorized as Sherbet whose declared-allergen data includes Milk. Adding a search word narrows that subset again.

If the page becomes empty, a choice may be too restrictive or the text may not occur in searchable fields. Clear the text, return category and allergen to All, then add one control at a time. An empty view describes catalog records matching those filters; it does not establish store availability.

The number shown near the catalog heading represents the catalog’s loaded flavor collection, not necessarily the number currently visible after filters are applied. Use the card list itself to review the matching entries. For live stock, check the dedicated menu, not the catalog count or an empty filter state.

Read a product card after you find a match

A filter result is a doorway to the record, not the final answer. Flavor cards can show a category, nutrition values on the site’s stated serving basis, declared allergens, additional equipment or allergen notes, source details, and a link to a dedicated item page. Read the complete card from top to bottom. A short name or image cannot represent all of that information.

Nutrition values are shown per 2/3 cup serving on the scoop catalog. Compare products on the same serving basis, and note that the figures belong to the published product information rather than a measured serving scooped at the shop. Some cards link to a manufacturer label PDF; when a source date appears, use it to understand how current that information is and verify with the latest packaging if the detail matters.

Declared ingredients and allergen-related notes are also different fields. A value in “Contains” is not a substitute for reading an equipment note, and an equipment note does not identify every ingredient. The site cautions that in-store cross-contact is possible. If you need certainty about a particular product or how it is handled, an online filter cannot provide it. Ask the shop to check the current label and answer your specific question.

Check current stock separately

After finding a flavor you want to explore, use the live in-stock menu to check what Sweet Escape currently reports. Select the scoop section, search the exact name, and look at the update time shown on that page. Catalog records may remain online even when a flavor is not currently listed, and a past stock snapshot can become outdated.

If a specific item is central to your visit and is not shown in the latest menu, call the shop to confirm before making plans around it. The Visit page has the phone number and directions. The live page answers the availability question; the catalog card answers product-description questions. Neither source should be substituted for the other.

A reliable five-step browsing routine

  1. Open the scoop catalog and decide whether to search by name or browse a category.
  2. Use one short search term and note that search covers names, categories, and declared-allergen text.
  3. Add the declared-allergen filter only as a directory aid, not as an allergy-safety screen.
  4. Open the full product card and read every nutrition, ingredient, source, and caution field provided.
  5. Check the live in-stock menu separately, then contact the shop for current availability or label questions.

This routine separates discovery, product details, allergy questions, and current stock. If a filter becomes confusing, return to All and start again with the product name you remember.

Use filters as a map, not a verdict

Sweet Escape’s scoop search and filters are most useful when you know what each control can see. Search looks at a limited set of text fields; category buttons group catalog entries; the declared-allergen selector filters one specific type of product data. None replaces a close reading of the exact flavor card, a check of current packaging, or the separate live menu.

For your next Memphis menu browse, start with the full scoop list, narrow the records with the controls, and open the page for any flavor that interests you. Check stock close to your visit. When an ingredient, allergy, or handling detail matters, ask Sweet Escape to verify the current information instead of relying on an empty result, a filter label, or a similar product name.
