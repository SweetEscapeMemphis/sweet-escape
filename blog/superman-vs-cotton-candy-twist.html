Two scoop names, a closer look at the records

Superman vs. Cotton Candy Twist Ice Cream in Memphis

Published October 9, 2026 · Sweet Escape, Memphis

Superman and Cotton Candy Twist are two colorful-sounding names in Sweet Escape’s scoop catalog. If you are choosing between them, the useful question is not what their names make you imagine; it is what the available product records actually tell you. Both are listed as ice cream, both have the same stated serving size, and both declare milk with the same shared-equipment caution. Their linked nutrition records differ slightly, though those records are dated and should be treated as a reference—not a promise about a current container.

Here is a clear side-by-side guide to the details available for these two Memphis scoop listings, what those details can help you compare, and what still needs a current label or live-menu check.

What the product pages confirm

Superman and Cotton Candy Twist each have an individual page in Sweet Escape’s ice cream catalog. Their current short descriptions identify them as documented scoop flavors, but do not give a longer ingredient or flavor-story explanation. That means we should not invent what either one tastes like, describe an unverified recipe, or assume that a familiar name means the same thing to every manufacturer.

For a guest comparing the two, the most responsible starting points are the full product names, the category, the published serving basis, and the linked nutrition and allergen notes. You can then decide which name interests you more while keeping the documented facts separate from personal expectations.

Compare the published nutrition on the same serving basis

Both pages present their archived nutrition information for a serving of 2/3 cup (95 grams). Matching serving sizes make the listed numbers easier to compare than if the records used different portions. In those records:

  • Superman: 190 calories, 10 grams total fat, 23 grams carbohydrate, 19 grams total sugars, 13 grams added sugars, and 3 grams protein.
  • Cotton Candy Twist: 200 calories, 9 grams total fat, 27 grams carbohydrate, 21 grams total sugars, 16 grams added sugars, and 3 grams protein.

The archived figures show 10 more calories, 4 more grams of carbohydrate, 2 more grams of total sugars, and 3 more grams of added sugars for Cotton Candy Twist at the stated serving size. Superman’s listed total fat is one gram higher; both list six grams of saturated fat and three grams of protein. These are comparisons between published records, not a health ranking or advice about what someone should eat.

Source dates matter here. The Superman nutrition record is dated June 18, 2019; the Cotton Candy Twist record is dated June 12, 2019. The item pages link to the corresponding pages of Sweet Escape’s nutrition PDF. Older panel details can help orient a comparison, but a printed current label is the better source if nutrition values are important to your decision.

The allergen notes are alike, but still worth checking

Each online record declares milk and names shared equipment that also processes peanuts, tree nuts, wheat, soy, and eggs. The product pages make the same request: recipes and labels can change, so ask the shop to verify the current container label before ordering when an allergy matters.

Matching published cautions do not make either flavor safe for a particular person, and they cannot answer an in-store handling question. Do not treat a short product description, the absence of an item from a short summary, or a familiar flavor name as an allergy guarantee. For an allergy or ingredient concern, read the full notes on the exact item and contact the shop to verify the current package. Sweet Escape’s nutrition and allergen guide explains how to interpret the site’s “contains” and shared-equipment information.

Which one should you look up first?

The online records do not establish a sensory difference beyond the names, so there is no factual basis for ranking the two by taste. A practical shortlist can still help: if one name is already familiar or simply catches your attention, open that page first. If nutrition details are part of your decision, compare the matching serving-size rows and remember the source dates. If an allergy question matters, review the exact item’s current label rather than choosing from the name.

For families or friends deciding together, each person can name the detail they care about most: flavor name, product-category preference, or a label detail that needs checking. That keeps the choice simple without claiming that either scoop is better for everyone. If neither short catalog description gives you enough information, the right next step is to ask Sweet Escape rather than guessing at a recipe.

  • The records differ modestly in listed calories and several nutrient values; they do not establish a preference or health ranking.
