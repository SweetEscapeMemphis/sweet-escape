How much ice cream do you need per person?

Start with ½ cup for a small dessert, 1 cup for a larger serving, or 1½ cups for an ice-cream-focused treat. These match the quick calculator’s 1, 2, and 3 planned scoops. A real scoop varies with the utensil and how you fill it. The default is 2 scoops, or 1 cup per guest.

For children or guests also having cake, consider the smaller portion; for adults who want seconds, choose a larger one. Appetite matters more than age alone. The optional detailed planner offers smaller cup-based portions and 0–30% extra. None of these planning portions is a verified shop serving.

How many gallons should you buy?

Divide total cups by 16 for US gallons, by 4 for quarts, or by 2 for pints, then round up to whole containers. A half gallon holds 8 cups. Check the package label: a 1.5-quart carton holds 6 cups, so it is smaller than a half gallon.

A gallon provides 32 half-cup servings, 16 one-cup servings, or about 10 full 1½-cup servings. Volume in fluid ounces does not convert directly to weight ounces; the separate shop estimate uses weight.

How much ice cream for a sundae bar?

Choose the ice cream portion first. If cake or cookies are also offered, a smaller portion may fit your menu. Use the detailed planner for dry toppings: its editable starting point is 15 g per guest, plus your chosen extra allowance.

For 50 guests with 10% extra, that is 825 g total across your dry toppings. Sauces are separate and not estimated. This is a planning assumption, not a measured appetite rule or a promise of Sweet Escape’s topping selection.

Tips for planning an ice cream party

  • Check freezer capacity before buying. Keep reserve containers frozen and bring out smaller batches as needed.
  • Plan bowls or cones, spoons, napkins, a scoop, and separate utensils for each topping. The detailed checklist adds your allowance to guest supplies.
  • Ask about dietary needs, check ingredient labels, and label toppings. Separate utensils help avoid mixing ingredients; they do not guarantee an allergen-free serving.
  • Split the total among a few flavors instead of buying the full quantity of every flavor. Start with modest toppings and replenish to reduce waste.
  • Enter your actual container price and total supplies budget for a cost estimate. Tax, transport, and any other unentered expenses are excluded.
