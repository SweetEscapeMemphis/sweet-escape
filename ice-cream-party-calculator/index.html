A few helpful planning notes

Plan the party, your way

Start with a portion that fits

Use ⅓ cup for a light treat, ½ cup for a standard dessert, or ⅔ cup for a generous dessert. These are editable assumptions for your event, not verified Sweet Escape portions. Appetites and serving utensils vary.

If cake, cookies, or other desserts are on the table, try the light setting. A 10% extra allowance is a starting point for seconds; change it from 0% to 30% to suit your group.

Know your containers

Use the package’s volume. The container calculator measures ice cream in cups; dry topping weights and the Sweet Escape by-weight estimate stay separate.

Round up, then plan for leftovers

Required cups = guests × cups per serving × (1 + extra percentage ÷ 100). Divide by cups per container and round up. Purchased volume is the whole-container count × its capacity.

For 20 guests at ½ cup with 10% extra, you need 11 cups. Three quarts provide 12 cups, one cup more than the requirement. Keep freezer space for unused ice cream.

Build a topping bar that works for you

The optional dry topping estimate starts at 15 g per guest and adds your chosen extra percentage. For 20 guests with 10% extra, that means about 330 g total across your chosen dry toppings. Change the grams per guest for your menu.

Sauces are not included in that weight. Label toppings and use separate utensils; ask guests about dietary needs and check current product labels.

Ice cream party FAQs

How much ice cream for 20 people?

With ½ cup per guest and 10% extra, plan for 11 cups. Buy 3 quart containers for 12 cups total, enough for about 24 half-cup servings. If your entered price is $6 per quart and you add no other expenses, the estimate is $18 total, or $0.90 per guest. This is an example price, not a Sweet Escape price.

How many servings are in a gallon?

A gallon contains 16 cups: 48 light servings at ⅓ cup, 32 standard servings at ½ cup, or 24 generous servings at ⅔ cup. Those are servings before setting aside an extra allowance. At ½ cup per guest plus 10% extra, one gallon covers up to 29 guests.

How much ice cream for 50 or 100 people?

At ½ cup per guest plus 10% extra, 50 guests need 27.5 cups: buy 7 quarts for 28 cups total. For 100 guests, plan for 55 cups: buy 14 quarts for 56 cups total. Change the container size to compare your shopping options.

What if I am serving cake or other desserts?

Try the light assumption of ⅓ cup per guest, then adjust for your guests. For 20 people with 10% extra, that is about 7.33 cups, so buy 2 quarts for 8 cups total. Each guest can have one light serving, with extra for the group.

Can I use this if I buy my supplies elsewhere?

Yes. This free tool works with ice cream from any shop or grocery store. Enter the volume on your container label and your own price. Serving sizes, extra allowance, and dry topping amounts are editable planning assumptions, not verified Sweet Escape portions.
