## Sources checked November 22, 2026

- [Zampini & Spence, Journal of Sensory Studies (2004)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1745-459X.2004.080403.x): front-tooth bite, microphone and headset, independent overall level and high-frequency manipulation, higher perceived crispness and freshness. Methods and discussion verify live feedback, hearing sound as if from the mouth and 15 of 20 participants thinking the similar chips came from different packages. Perceived freshness is not physical change, objective storage history or a safety verdict. No home amplification instructions.
- [Dacremont, Colas & Sauvageot, Journal of Texture Studies (1991)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1745-4603.1991.tb00503.x): vibration transmitted by air and through bone; six foods and different contributions for biting versus chewing; no universal sound-route bins.
- [Çarşanba, Duerrschmid & Schleining, Journal of Food Engineering (2018)](https://ciencia.ucp.pt/en/publications/assessment-of-acoustic-mechanical-measurements-for-crispness-of-w/): nine wafer brands, cutting versus three-point bending while recording sound and force, comparison with descriptive panel ratings. Correlations specific to measures and test conditions. The tested wafers are not Sweet Escape's chips.
- [Vickers, Journal of Texture Studies (1984)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1745-4603.1984.tb00375.x): selected crisp foods generally higher-pitched than selected crunchy foods; eating technique matters. Not a universal definition.
- [de Liz Pocztaruk et al., Food Quality and Preference (2011)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S095032931100019X): three biscuit textures and auditory/visual masking conditions; sound and snapping results depended on starting condition. Prior exposure is the authors' interpretation, not a guarantee for every food or eater.
