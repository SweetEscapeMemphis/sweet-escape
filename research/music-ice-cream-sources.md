## Primary sources and claim boundaries

1. NIDCD, Taste Disorders: https://www.nidcd.nih.gov/health/taste-disorders
   - Basic tastes distinguished from broader flavor involving aroma, temperature, texture and other inputs. Normal sensory explanation only; no treatment claims.
2. Lin, Hamid, Shepherd, Kantono and Spence (2022), Musical and Non-Musical Sounds Influence the Flavour Perception of Chocolate Ice Cream and Emotional Responses. Foods 11(12):1784, DOI 10.3390/foods11121784. Full original: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9223177/
   - 64 adults; personally rated liked/disliked sound/music; chocolate ice cream; temporal dominance and emotional measures. Liked music had longest sweetness-dominance duration. Dominance is attention-capturing sensation, not automatically intensity/liking. Single preparation and real-world generalization limits preserved. No formula, volume or loudness recommendation, or universal genre inference.
3. Xu et al. (2019), Background soundscapes influence the perception of ice-cream as indexed by electrophysiological measures. Food Research International 125:108564, DOI 10.1016/j.foodres.2019.108564. Original abstract: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31554052/
   - Café control plus bird/machine/forest sounds; temporal order of cocoa/sweetness/creaminess/bitterness varied. Specific control and timing retained. No physiological mechanism, mind-reading or health inference.
4. Kantono et al. (2018), The effect of music on gelato perception in different eating contexts. Food Research International 113:43–56, DOI 10.1016/j.foodres.2018.06.030. Original abstract: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30195538/
   - Laboratory/immersive/natural conditions; TCATA citations over time. Natural context had more sweetness/cocoa/milky citations than laboratory; liked/neutral music increased sweetness citations, disliked increased bitterness. Reports not chemical measurements. Correct year 2018.
5. Reinoso Carvalho et al., Smooth Operator: Music modulates the perceived creaminess, sweetness, and bitterness of chocolate. DOI 10.1016/j.appet.2016.10.026. Author/repository abstract: https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid%3A11a1bbe2-be92-4554-8d0e-1fd099c1931c
   - Identical chocolates under contrasting soundtracks; perceived creaminess/sweetness changed, overall enjoyment did not appear to. Explicitly chocolate, not frozen-dessert trial. No publication-year assertion because online/volume dates differ.
6. Kantono et al., The effect of background music on food pleasantness ratings. DOI 10.1177/0305735615613149. Original publisher abstract: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0305735615613149
   - 50 participants; milk/dark/bittersweet gelati; non-preferred music lowered pleasantness for all, preferred improved dark/bittersweet but not milk. Exception retained; no universal enjoyment effect.
