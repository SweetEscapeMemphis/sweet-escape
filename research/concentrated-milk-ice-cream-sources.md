## Sources checked November 24, 2026

- U.S. standards [21 CFR § 131.130, evaporated milk](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-131/subpart-B/section-131.130) and [§ 131.120, sweetened condensed milk](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-131/subpart-B/section-131.120): partial removal of water, evaporated milk’s defined heat processing, and sweeteners as part of the condensed product’s identity. Descriptive only, no home processing/storage or dietary advice.
- Manufacturer’s [sweetened condensed milk](https://www.verybestbaking.com/carnation/products/sweetened-condensed-milk/) and [regular evaporated milk](https://www.verybestbaking.com/carnation/products/nestle-carnation-evaporated-milk/) product labels, dated comparison: two tablespoons each; 22 g total/18 g added versus 3 g total/0 g added as printed. The evaporated ingredient statement lists dextrose, so the article reports the added-sugars line only, not ingredient absence. The pictured packages list 14 ounces net weight and 12 fluid ounces respectively; weight and volume are distinct. No branded product recommendation, special dietary advice or extrapolation to every can.
- [Borden patent US15553A (1856)](https://patents.google.com/patent/US15553A/en), drawing and claim: concentration under vacuum without sugar added in the claim, with earlier approaches acknowledged. [University of Guelph dairy evaporation](https://books.lib.uoguelph.ca/dairyscienceandtechnologyebook/chapter/evaporation-and-dehydration/): lower pressure lowers boiling temperature; no shelf-life or first-inventor claim. [Heller’s Guide for Ice-Cream Makers, Library of Congress copy](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/public/gdcmassbookdig/hellersguidefori00hell/hellersguidefori00hell.pdf), printed pages 34–36 and 114–115: vanilla formulas with concentrated milk and separate granulated sugar, and distinguishes plain/sweetened. Historical standards and storage claims are not treated as current.
- University of Guelph’s [sweeteners](https://books.lib.uoguelph.ca/icecreamtechnologyebook/chapter/sweeteners/), [nonfat milk solids](https://books.lib.uoguelph.ca/icecreamtechnologyebook/chapter/milk-solids-not-fat/) and [freezing/whipping](https://books.lib.uoguelph.ca/icecreamtechnologyebook/chapter/freezing-whipping-of-ice-cream/): freezing point depression, some unfrozen water, powder/concentrate sources and air incorporation. Effects of substitutions qualified by composition and serving temperature; no tested substitution formula.
- Original recipes: [Martha Stewart no-churn vanilla](https://www.marthastewart.com/no-churn-vanilla-ice-cream-recipe-11789070) uses a sweetened can and whipped cream; [Morin, McMillan & Erickson’s Joe Beef cookbook excerpt](https://www.epicurious.com/recipes/food/views/ice-cream-base-388958) uses evaporated milk, other dairy, separately measured sweeteners and a churn; [Carnation no-churn caramel](https://www.verybestbaking.com/carnation/recipes/carnation-no-churn-caramel-ice-cream/) calls for both a sweetened can and a specifically caramel-flavored evaporated milk. Only explanatory comparisons used, not recipe instructions or product endorsements.
