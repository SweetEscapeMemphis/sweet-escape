## Sources checked November 26, 2026

- Cohen and Hartel, [Characterizing the Mechanical and Rheological Properties of Aerated Confectionery Gels](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jfpe.70156) (2025), esp. Table 2, figure 1 and texture profile: experimental averages 1.5 and 0.47 g/cm³, comparable water content, more air reduced probe adhesion, gelatin concentration increased stiffness, response to pressing depends on bubble-size distribution. Commercial comparison was less dense with qualitatively more even bubbles; resolution precluded quantitative size/connectivity analysis. Not generalized to every brand, frozen candy or shop product.
- [Science Museum of Virginia, Marshmallow Madness](https://smv.org/documents/45/Marshmallow_Madness_ENG_SPAN.pdf): water warms, candy softens, gas expands, cooling reduces puff; combined measurements and cylindrical illustration. [Science on Stage Europe, Marshmallows – Air Pressure](https://www.science-on-stage.eu/sites/sons-eu/files/material/food-cooking-and-stem-section-1_experiments-with-food-items_0.pdf), pp. 9–10, includes water vapor as another source of gas without lowering external pressure. [University of Washington chemistry demonstration](https://chem.washington.edu/lecture-demos/boyles-law) specifies initial swelling, possible deflation as gas escapes, and collapse when pressure returns. Descriptive only; no temperature, heating or vacuum operating instructions. Room-temperature tear and press comparison is a simple observation, not a validated texture test.
- [OpenStax gas laws](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/9-2-relating-pressure-volume-amount-and-temperature-the-ideal-gas-law): temperature must be absolute for fixed pressure and gas amount. Illustrative dry-air ratio 333.15 ÷ 293.15 ≈ 1.1364, about 14% larger. Not a marshmallow size or heating prediction; real candy exchanges gas/water vapor and its walls exert force.
- Original recipes: [Emma Duckworth](https://emmaduckworthbakes.com/marshmallow-ice-cream/) dissolves some toasted pieces in warm custard and adds the rest after churning; [Susan Reid at King Arthur Baking](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/recipes/marshmallow-swirl-ice-cream-recipe) uses an egg-white-and-syrup spread folded into soft ice cream as a ribbon. Recipe comparisons only, no directions involving hot sugar or a torch, no endorsement or general substitution. [Goff’s University of Guelph ice cream technology guide](https://books.lib.uoguelph.ca/icecreamtechnologyebook/chapter/freezing-whipping-of-ice-cream/) grounds separate air incorporation into a freezing base.
