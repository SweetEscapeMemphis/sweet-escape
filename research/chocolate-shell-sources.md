# Why Does Chocolate Shell Harden on Ice Cream? — verification notes

- Canonical: https://www.sweetescapememphis.com/blog/why-chocolate-shell-hardens-on-ice-cream.html
- November 24, 2026 AM America/Chicago. Archive reviewed: chocolate in the mouth covers cocoa-butter forms, tempering and lubrication; the stick article covers handling and mold release; the Stracciatella guide covers format and origins. This article covers a pourable topping, its solidification, drainage versus coating thickness and adhesion versus fracture.

## Primary and authoritative sources checked November 24, 2026

- [WO2010072481A1](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/37/4d/a6/fdeaa230c5e8b8/WO2010072481A1.pdf): coating-fat crystallization, rapid coconut-oil setting and first audible crack as an operational measure. [US20180271115A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20180271115A1/en): cold mechanical bend test across two supports and break force. No health, general superiority, exact crystallization timing or local process claim.
- [Jang & Choi, Food Engineering Progress (2007)](https://www.foodengprog.org/download/download_pdf?pid=fep-11-2-103): calorimetry of warming fats used for coatings; narrower melting range for the tested coconut oil than the tested palm oil. No universal oil melting point or confusion between warming and cooling measurements.
- [Canada Agriculture and Food Museum kitchen activity](https://ingeniumcanada.org/sites/default/files/2020-07/CAFM_Magic_Shell_Ice_Cream_Treat_activity.pdf): melt chocolate chips and coconut oil, let cool before applying a thin layer to ice cream. Original saucer comparison is expressly a separate, qualitative extension. Did not repeat the handout’s approximate oil melting points, recipe quantities or setting time.
- [US4414239A, Judith L. Oven (1983)](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4414239A/en): ambient-pour blend with refined cottonseed and coconut oils; example reported flow at 17°C (62.6°F, rounded 63°F) and rapid set on ice cream. [US1883484A (1932)](https://patents.google.com/patent/US1883484): warm-applied nut dip and peanut oil as described modifier of brittleness. [US2364592A (1944)](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2364592): coating adhesion and fractured chips sliding away, distinct from cracking the layer. These are inventors’ examples, not origin, recipe or modern brand claims.
- [Ghorbel et al., Journal of Food Process Engineering 34 (2011), authors’ paper](https://www.academia.edu/10508910/INFLUENCE_OF_FAT_CONTENT_ON_RHEOLOGICAL_PROPERTIES_OF_MOLTEN_ICE_CREAM_COMPOUND_COATINGS_AND_THICKNESS_OF_SOLIDIFIED_PRODUCTS), DOI 10.1111/j.1745-4530.2008.00342.x: nine copra-oil blends, fixed chocolate ingredient ratio, controlled dip, timer and caliper, thinner layers at higher fat content; longer drainage at higher ratios and limits of using drip time as set time. Specific tested formulas and apparatus, not general advice to add any oil.
