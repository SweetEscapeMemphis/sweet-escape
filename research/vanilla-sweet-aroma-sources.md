## Verified sources and limits

- [NIDCD, smell fact sheet](https://www.nidcd.nih.gov/sites/default/files/smell-disorders.pdf): olfactory sensory cells and nostril versus mouth/throat-to-nose routes. Only normal sensory explanation used, not symptom diagnosis or medical guidance.
- [NIDCD, taste fact sheet](https://www.nidcd.nih.gov/sites/default/files/taste-disorders.pdf): basic taste qualities versus combined flavor, including aroma, temperature and texture. No claim that smell measures sugar quantity.
- [INRAE, October 12, 2023](https://www.inrae.fr/en/news/certain-odours-can-trick-brain-eating-less-salt-sugar): institute's explanation of French vanilla versus Vietnamese lemon sweet-aroma associations. Explicit examples, not universal national preferences or evidence that lemon is chemically less acidic. Its health/obesity claims and reformulation recommendations deliberately not used.
- [Stevenson, Boakes and Prescott, 1998](https://doi.org/10.1006/lmot.1998.0996), Learning and Motivation 29, 113–132: original publisher abstract available through indexed ScienceDirect result, two experiments pairing odor with sucrose versus water and increased smelled sweetness, not dependent on explicit contingency knowledge. Full text not claimed reviewed; no unsupported concentration, participant count or vanilla-specific training claim.
- [Fondberg, Lundström and Seubert, 2021](https://academic.oup.com/chemse/article/doi/10.1093/chemse/bjab003/6105024): 60 people; basil/orange-flower chewing gums, 5-day exposure three times daily, sucrose-paired versus unpaired odor. No evidence of the proposed associative learning in their tasks; pleasantness increased independent of pairing. Does not disprove all learning or replicate every detail of 1998 study.
- [Wang et al., 2018](https://www.mdpi.com/2306-5710/4/4/73): 18 vanilla/sucrose skim milks rated by 108 participants, sweetness/vanilla/milk/thickness/liking separate; vanilla enhanced sweetness but sucrose's enhancement of vanilla stronger. Not frozen dessert or shop-specific evidence, no ingredient/reformulation recommendation.
- [Green et al., online 2011; 2012 issue](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3243899/): actual-food experiment included vanilla custard; sucrose enhanced vanilla, added vanillin did not significantly enhance sweetness. Response-category/halo-dumping discussion motivates distinct vanilla versus sweetness questions, not dismissal of all other findings.
- [Bertelsen et al., 2020](https://www.mdpi.com/2304-8158/9/2/146): five-aroma screening, then 129 young adults, 3 sucrose levels with/without vanilla in aqueous blends. Group/condition variation, not inference about every age, a diner's sex or a particular ice cream.
- [Yeomans et al., 2006, author's university record](https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/hedonic-and-sensory-characteristics-of-odors-conditioned-by-pairi/): sensory sweetness and liking dissociable; experiment 3 odor-sucrose pairing increased sweetness in both sweet-liker/disliker groups, increased liking only in likers. Not a study of Sweet Escape or a preference guarantee.
- Shop: data/gelato-flavors.js and /gelato/madagascar-vanilla-bean/ exact description (Madagascar bourbon vanilla, fresh cream, milk, pure cane sugar). Reference 1/2 cup (80 g), 20 g sugars; known milk and current container-label requirement retained with ingredients/nutrition/facility/shared-equipment/handling/cross-contact checks. No additional recipe, vanilla concentration, processing, supplier, sensory performance, health/safety or same-day availability claim. No full nutrition replication needed; product page linked.
