# Ube guide source record — checked October 11, 2026

Distinct intent: explain what ube is, place the ingredient in Filipino dessert context, and distinguish those facts from what Sweet Escape’s specific gelato entry documents.

## Philippine primary and government sources

- Philippine Department of Science and Technology, Food and Nutrition Research Institute (FNRI), [Food Composition Table: entries under B](https://i.fnri.dost.gov.ph/fct/library/food_content/B) — lists purple yam as *Dioscorea alata* / Ubi/Ube and separately lists purple sweet potato as *Ipomoea batatas* and taro as *Colocasia esculenta*.
- Philippine DOST-FNRI, [Purple yam food report](https://i.fnri.dost.gov.ph/fct/library/report/3187) — identifies purple yam as *Dioscorea alata* and Ubi/Ube.
- Philippine Council for Agriculture, Aquatic and Natural Resources Research and Development, [Indigenous Vegetables of the Philippines: *Dioscorea alata*](https://iveg.pcaarrd.dost.gov.ph/crop/dioscorea-alata) — crop profile identifies Ube/Purple yam.
- Department of Science and Technology, Industrial Technology Development Institute, [Ube processing overview](https://www.dost.gov.ph/knowledge-resources/news/67-2020-news/1884-matuto-at-kumita-cook-your-way-to-wealth-with-ube-processing.html) — describes jam, powder, candy, and flakes, with uses in cakes, pastries, flans, and ice cream. Article’s general processing context is not used as a claim about Sweet Escape’s formula or sourcing.
- Philippine Journal of Science, [Antioxidants of Purple and White Greater Yam (*Dioscorea alata*)](https://philjournalsci.dost.gov.ph/images/pdf/pjs_pdf/vol137no1/pdfs/Antioxidants_of_Purple_and_Whte_Greater_Yam.pdf) — contextualizes ube in Filipino sweetened delicacies and lists halaya, puto, halo-halo, hopia, and rice cakes. Article makes no health/antioxidant claim from this source.
