- November 27, 2026 PM America/Chicago, second and final local-day post. Archive overlap: the botanical berry article names cranberries only as a classification example. ## Primary sources checked November 27, 2026

- [Diaz-Garcia et al. (2019)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6760784/), Methods, Figure 1 and Results: ink transfer from equatorial cuts shows four locules; >3,000 stamps from 16 cultivars; destructive measurements were made on different berries. Seasonal comparisons show varying relationships with compression force. No causal, single-berry or bounce-height prediction. [OpenStax University Physics](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/14-4-archimedes-principle-and-buoyancy) supports average density, the boat analogy, and how submerged fraction varies.
- [PNW cranberry extension guide](https://wpcdn.web.wsu.edu/wp-extension/uploads/sites/2056/2023/05/Cranberry-Production-in-PNW.pdf), printed pp. 55–57: wet and dry harvesting, shallow initial flooding and reel agitation, floating booms, stepped conveyor, pump and grate, and typical channels for wet- and dry-harvested fruit. No assumption about a given package. [OSU Extension](https://extension.oregonstate.edu/sites/extd8/files/documents/8836/fch1502oregoncranberriesleaderguide.pdf#page=3), p. 3: ripe-firm fruit rebound and extension activity. The purported inventor anecdote is not used.
- [EPA 1975 processing survey](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2025-03/canned-preserved-fruit-veg-category_fruits-veg-specialties-segment_dd_1975_0.pdf), cranberry processing subsection: three-step barrier sorter. [US5544761A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US5544761A/en), background and design: multiple berry grades may float; oddly shaped berries and localized soft spots can fool a bounce sorter; proposed repeated roller-pressure alternative. Not claimed as a current universal method, home ripeness rule or food-safety test.
- [2025 firmness experiment](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40145629/): six groups compared with micro-CT, gravimetric buoyancy and compression; postharvest freezing affected measured structure, and softer and firmer cultivars had similar compression force after freezing in the study. Density was not a useful proxy for firmness. No extrapolation to all varieties, storage histories, home ripeness tests or dessert consistency.
- Published home examples: [Massachusetts Cranberries sorbet](https://www.cranberries.org/Cranberry-Sorbet) uses fresh or frozen berries cooked and blended, then cooled, with lemon before churning. [Haider and Drakulich custard ice cream](https://www.cranberries.org/Cranberry-Ice-Cream) sieves cooked, spiced purée into the custard. [Nigella Lawson’s Feast syrup dessert](https://www.nigella.com/recipes/ice-cream-with-cranberry-syrup) serves cooked cranberry syrup over vanilla or white chocolate ice cream. The distinction is fruit throughout, strained fruit with custard, or fruit added at serving; no third-party directions reproduced and none of the recipes is represented as the shop’s.
