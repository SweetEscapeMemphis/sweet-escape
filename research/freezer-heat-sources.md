## Authoritative sources and boundaries

1. NASA My NASA Data, Heating Things Up:
   https://mynasadata.larc.nasa.gov/lesson-plans/heating-things
   Teacher background: temperature versus thermal energy transferred as heat, warmer-to-cooler flow. Original conceptual explanation, not copied classroom procedures or an exact molecular model for every substance. No experiment prescribed.
2. Danfoss, How a refrigerator works:
   https://www.danfoss.com/en/about-danfoss/our-businesses/cooling/the-fridge-how-it-works
   Manufacturer's own teaching text: evaporation absorbs heat; compression raises pressure/temperature; condensation releases heat; capillary expansion device. Primary search result supplied complete teaching text; direct open 403. Common vapor-compression cycle, not every freezer design or component-location map. No alcohol/aerosol demonstrations, hot-component touching or repair instructions.
3. OpenStax College Physics 14.3, Phase Change and Latent Heat:
   https://openstax.org/books/college-physics/pages/14-3-phase-change-and-latent-heat
   State changes exchange energy; pure-water melting can proceed without temperature rise under described conditions; freezing releases energy. Not extrapolated to a single ice-cream freezing point. No copied images, equations or textbook exercises.
4. OpenStax University Physics Volume 2, 4.3 Refrigerators and Heat Pumps:
   https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-2/pages/4-3-refrigerators-and-heat-pumps
   Cycle energy balance and cooling COP. Original hypothetical illustration: 300 J removed + 100 J work = 400 J warm-side release; COP = 3. Not measurements or claims about Sweet Escape/home appliance performance. Relevant section 150 words including heading, below 200-word source limit. No verbatim excerpt, textbook images or activities.
5. NIST Watt (W) glossary:
   https://www.nist.gov/glossary-term/34606
   Watt = joule/second. Independent illustrative calculation: 100 J / 10 s = 10 W average input power. Units example does not change cycle energy totals or establish an appliance rating.
6. University of Illinois Physics Van, Open Refrigerator:
   https://van.physics.illinois.edu/ask/listing/1790
   Appliance rejecting heat into the same closed room does not provide net room cooling with door open; local cold/hot spots versus average room effect. Thought experiment only, no open-door test or exact room-temperature/efficiency prediction.
7. U.S. DOE, May 30, 2023, Pump Up Your Savings with Heat Pumps:
   https://www.energy.gov/articles/pump-your-savings-heat-pumps
   Cooling-season heat movement outdoors versus heating-season movement indoors. Boundary comparison only; omit dated shares, prices, tax incentives, percent/dollar/carbon savings and upgrade recommendations. Old /energysaver/heat-pump-systems URL 404, so cited the live primary article instead.
8. ENERGY STAR refrigerator guidance:
   https://www.energystar.gov/products/refrigerators
   Air circulation, avoidance of heat sources, manual-guided safe coil cleaning and insulation context. Appliance-specific manual controls clearance/maintenance, not this blog. No numeric food temperatures, buying or savings claims.
9. Energy Rating government consumer guidance:
   https://www.energyrating.gov.au/consumer-information/tips/save-energy/tips-using-your-fridge-and-freezer-efficiently
   Ventilation, seals and thinking ahead before opening. Bowl/utensil/retrieval paragraph is an original practical application, not timed food-storage advice or safety guarantee.
