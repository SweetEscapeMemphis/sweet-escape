Its current figures on production and promotional superiority language are not used. Approximately 150 derived article words including the opening.

University of Maine Cooperative Extension bulletin 7036 (updated 2025), https://extension.umaine.edu/publications/7036e/ — ray-cell starch storage, conversion largely to sucrose, variation among trees, and Indigenous maple-product use before European colonization. No detailed tapping instructions or calendar generalized to Memphis. Under 200 derived words.

Oregon State University Extension, https://extension.oregonstate.edu/catalog/em-9163-making-bigleaf-maple-syrup-hobbyists-guide — freeze/thaw pressure and regional variability; raw sap can look clear while microbial growth occurs. No raw-sap consumption suggestion, commercial equipment recommendation, tapping method or food-preservation recipe. Under 200 derived words.
4. Utah State University Extension, https://extension.usu.edu/forestry/publications/utah-forest-facts/044-maple-sap-processing — bucket/bag versus tubing/gravity/vacuum, spiles, divided evaporators and refractometers. Its apparent typo about a gallon of finished sap and equipment purchasing advice are not used. Under 200 derived words.
5. University of Vermont Proctor Maple Research Center, https://www.uvm.edu/cals/proctor/proctor-maple-outreach — own reverse-osmosis operation, membrane water removal and subsequent evaporation; mineral niter/sugar sand filtering. No fuel-saving percentages, supplier claims or dietary claims. Under 200 derived words.
6. UNH Extension, Steven Roberge, https://extension.unh.edu/blog/2022/03/making-grade-color-flavor-maple-syrup — sap composition, acidity, sugar types, heating time and browning affect color/flavor; four descriptive color/flavor categories. No universal “darker is better,” nutrition inference, universal syrup density specification or product-specific sensory claim. Under 200 derived words.
7. NIST, January 4, 2017, https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2017/01/how-sweet-it-nist-metrology-and-maple-syrup-industry — primary calibration work on sap hydrometers, reference-temperature effects, illustrative approximately 43 gallons at 2% sucrose versus 22 gallons at 4% for one gallon of syrup. No outdated production/economic statistics or legal guidance. Under 200 derived words.
8. Nottawaseppi Huron Band of the Potawatomi, https://nhbp-nsn.gov/environmental/maple-syrup-program/ — first-party Pine Creek Indian Reservation sugar-bushing program and Blessing of the Maples ceremony; respect for trees and Mother Earth. Explicitly one community's account, not generalized to all nations. Under 200 derived words.
