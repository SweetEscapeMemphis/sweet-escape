## Authoritative sources and limits

1. Erica Jacobsen, Journal of Chemical Education, 1999, 76(5), 624A, DOI 10.1021/ed076p624: https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/ed076p624 — publisher abstract explains proteases, peptide-link cleavage, fresh/frozen pineapple warning and heat denaturation. Abstract retrieved via official indexed publisher result; direct page probe later returned 403. No paywall bypass and no claims from inaccessible full text. No universal exact setting time or minimum enzyme dose asserted.
2. UC biotechnology education, Adventure 4:1, Jell-O and Pineapple: https://r830.cnr.berkeley.edu/ucbiotech/resources/methods/whifbiotech.pdf — official indexed PDF passage describes paired fresh/canned fruit comparison, water-holding matrix and short gelatin chains. Article offers original comparison-design suggestions, not a claim to have performed the experiment. No PDF figure reproduced.
3. UC Santa Barbara Science Line: https://scienceline.ucsb.edu/getkey.php?key=23 — official indexed answer on cooled gelatin chain reassociation/semisolid texture; direct open timed out. Does not justify treating gelatin as a frozen block.
4. American Society of Baking: https://asbe.org/article/gelatin/?topic=ingredient — accessible ingredient section describes collagen-derived protein, hot-water dissolution and cooling gelation. Historical anecdotes on this page deliberately omitted.
5. OpenStax Biology 6.5: https://openstax.org/books/biology/pages/6-5-enzymes — biological catalyst, substrate/active site, activation energy, temperature and protein structure. General scientific facts only, independently re-expressed; no text, illustrations or exercises reproduced.
6. McGill Office for Science and Society: https://www.mcgill.ca/oss/article/general-science-you-asked/why-cant-i-use-fresh-pineapple-make-jell-o — proteolytic enzymes and heat-processed canned-fruit distinction. Chilli, digestion/health, tenderizing instructions and historical anecdotes omitted.
7. University of Missouri Extension freezing guide, hosted by K-State: https://www.ksre.k-state.edu/humannutrition/nutrition-topics/eatingwell-budget/makeaheadmeals-documents/freezingbasicsunivofmissouri.pdf — freezing slows, rather than destroys, fruit/vegetable enzymes. Official indexed PDF text checked; no preservation recipe or numerical enzyme threshold supplied.
8. Glider and Hargrove, ABLE, Tested Studies in Laboratory Teaching, 2002 volume 23: https://www.ableweb.org/volumes/abstract/?article_id=444 — primary teaching-investigation abstract: developed black/white film, gelatin binding black silver grains to plastic, clearing time and temperature/pH variables. Article explicitly distinguishes laboratory description from food preparation; no film handling procedure supplied.
9. H. Douglas Goff, University of Guelph, Structure from the Ice Crystals: https://books.lib.uoguelph.ca/icecreamtechnologyebook/chapter/structure-from-the-ice-crystals/ — partly frozen foam with ice crystals, air, fat and concentrated unfrozen solution. No reproduction of diagrams or exact product composition; generic contrast, not Sweet Escape manufacturing evidence.
10. Penn State Extension pectin guide: https://extension.psu.edu/pectins-role-in-making-jam-and-jelly — official indexed transcript identifies carbohydrate, traditional sugar/acid/cooking balance and non-interchangeability of gelatin/starch with pectin for canned preserves. Direct open 403; no home-canning recipe offered.
11. FDA: https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/7-tips-cleaning-fruits-vegetables — current public guidance on clean hands, rinsing before peeling, plain running water and refrigeration at/below 40°F. Food-handling point is separate from enzyme activity and not a dietary-safety guarantee.
