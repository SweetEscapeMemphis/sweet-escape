## Sources checked November 26, 2026

- Smithsonian National Museum of American History [1938 truck collection record](https://www.si.edu/object/good-humor-truck-1938%3Anmah_1287653) documents the refrigerated truck and announcing bells; public copy linked through the Smithsonian’s [object feature](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/innovation/ice-cream-truck-innovation-history-180975199/) because the main collections host returned 403 locally. [Wisconsin Historical Society, Good Humor Ice Cream Truck](https://legacy.wisconsinhistory.org/Records/Image/IM96875), June 1, 1936, International Harvester photograph and caption mentioning chimes and a scheduled route. The interpretation of repetition as an audible sign is an inference, not a documented meter, uniform route policy or experimental sales result.
- Paillard and Sueur [US526499A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US526499A/en), issued 1894: pin placement, vibrating teeth, longitudinal cylinder movement to change selection, tune repetition design. Mechanical principle only, not a claim that the specific patent was installed on a truck. [Nichols Electronics product overview](https://www.nicholselectronics.com/overview) and [tune list](https://www.nicholselectronics.com/our-songs): Mark IV single selection; Omni II 32 positions including mechanical-style arrangements and repeated titles; two versions of The Entertainer and the RZA title. No market-share, popularity, price, product recommendation or current commercial relationship inferred.
- Library of Congress [The Entertainer piano score](https://www.loc.gov/item/2023864238/), dated 1902, and [Joplin biography](https://www.loc.gov/item/n50039880/scott-joplin-1868-1917/), 1973 film use. [Good Humor’s RZA announcement](https://www.goodhumor.com/us/en/jingle.html) documents its 2020 release and cites racist minstrel-era uses of the “Turkey in the Straw” melody. No claim of one unambiguous origin, no lyrics, score or audio reproduced. Descriptive historical context, no brand comparison, endorsement or affiliation.
- FHWA [barrier physics](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/Environment/noise/resources/quantifying-benefits-of-abatement-part-1.pdf) on path obstruction and edge diffraction; OpenStax [sound intensity](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/17-3-sound-intensity) on inverse-square idealization and [Doppler effect](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/17-7-the-doppler-effect) for stationary listener and moving source. At 20 mph ≈ 8.94 m/s and with sound speed ≈ 343 m/s, 440×343/(343∓8.94) gives ≈452 Hz approaching and ≈429 Hz receding when motion is directly along line of sight in still air. Illustrative, not measured route coverage, loudness, or advice to approach moving traffic.
