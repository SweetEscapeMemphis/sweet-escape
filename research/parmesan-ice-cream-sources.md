## Authoritative sources and claim boundaries

- [Wellcome Collection catalog](https://wellcomecollection.org/works/eykc8dzs): London 1789 edition of The Complete Confectioner attributed to Frederick Nutt, provided by Glasgow University Library; public-domain scan. Catalog title identifies its intended family/table-presentation scope.
- [Nutt 1789, printed p. 125, image 175](https://wellcomecollection.org/works/eykc8dzs/items?canvas=175), continuing onto printed p. 126/image 176: recipe 150, normalized title “Parmasan Cheese Ice Cream.” Original scan and OCR inspected directly, not solely through a modern retelling. Eggs, syrup, cream and three ounces of rasped cheese; cheese is mixed before freezing. Contents image 24 confirms neighboring flavor names; images 25–28 show water ices and fruits preserved in brandy. No full recipe transcription or modern unit conversion. No first-inventor, mass-popularity, preserved-batch taste, specific cheese producer or historical-person consumption claim.
- [Mount Vernon adaptation](https://www.mountvernon.org/inn/recipes/article/parmesan-ice-cream/): explicitly credits Nutt's 1807 edition and names eggs/simple syrup/cream/Parmesan. Kept distinct from the original 1789 scan. Not proof that Washington ate this flavor. No tasting claims adopted and no cooking procedure reproduced as advice.
- [Library of Congress, Williams 1891 The Book of Ices](https://www.loc.gov/item/ltf89003634/): bibliography and original printed p. 50, PDF page 56, inspected by read-only text extraction and full-page rendered image. “Fromages Glacés” / “Iced-Cream-Cheeses” describes molded ice cream and colored forms; the title does not by itself establish a cheese ingredient. Direct [public-domain PDF](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/public/gdcmassbookdig/bookofices00will/bookofices00will.pdf) was too large for web extraction, so used local read-only PDF workflow. This is Williams's 1891 book, not Marshall's similarly titled book.
- [Museum of Royal Worcester historic-ices activity](https://www.museumofroyalworcester.org/app/uploads/2025/08/Design-Your-Own-Historic-Ices.pdf): Victorian molds, examples from A. B. Marshall 1885, and decorative meal-centerpiece context. Shape is not treated as an ingredient list.
- [Smithsonian metal mold](https://www.si.edu/object/ice-cream-mold%3Anmah_870279): quart-sized metal example, probably used 1900–1920. Preserves the museum's uncertainty; no unsupported precise manufacture date.
- [V&A historical serving objects](https://www.vam.ac.uk/blog/creating-new-europe-1600-1800-galleries/can-i-have-a-taste-of-your-ice-cream): handled cups and a 1778 Sèvres pail/inner-liner arrangement. No uncertain “first ice-cream shop” or universal origin claim reused.
- [Fitzwilliam Museum, Cambridge](https://feast-and-fast.fitzmuseum.cam.ac.uk/discover/pineapple-icecream): Nutt's apprenticeship to Negri and Witten, successors in the Berkeley Square confectionery business associated with The Pot and Pineapple. Supports the craft/training context without inventing his biography, claiming a first invention, or reproducing historical prices.
- [National Archives transcription tips](https://www.archives.gov/citizen-archivist/transcribe/tips): long-s resemblance to f, typeset/handwritten use into early nineteenth century, and contextual reading. Applied to the original recipe's letterforms and page break, not used to invent a new spelling or ingredient.
- [USDA homemade ice-cream safety](https://ask.fsis.usda.gov/article/Is-your-homemade-ice-cream-safe): raw/undercooked egg risk and pasteurized alternatives. Article explicitly is not a cooking tutorial; no historical sensory test is presented as modern food-safety guidance.
