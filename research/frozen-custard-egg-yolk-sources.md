# Why Does Frozen Custard Have Egg Yolks? — verification notes

- Canonical: https://www.sweetescapememphis.com/blog/why-frozen-custard-has-egg-yolks.html
- November 23, 2026 PM America/Chicago, second/final post for the local day. Reviewed the vanilla picker, vanilla aroma, butter/churning and ice cream category articles. Distinct focus: egg-yolk protein thickening versus emulsification, physical and sensory comparisons of yolk alternatives, pigment variation, and the U.S. naming standard.

## Sources checked November 23, 2026

- [Exploratorium custard comparison](https://annex.exploratorium.edu/cooking/eggs/gentle.html): gentle versus high heat, protein coagulation and liquid leakage. [Susan Reid’s frozen custard for King Arthur Baking](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/recipes/frozen-custard-recipe): warm yolk base, spoon coating as texture cue, strain into a chilled bowl, chill before churning. [FDA egg guidance](https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/what-you-need-to-know-about-egg-safety): egg safety, including pasteurized egg options when a recipe leaves eggs raw or undercooked. No recipe replication or process validation.
- [H. Douglas Goff, Ice Cream Technology e-Book](https://books.lib.uoguelph.ca/icecreamtechnologyebook/chapter/emulsifiers/): yolk as traditional emulsifier, oil/water interface, droplet coating, partial fat clustering and air structure. [Julia Reed’s side-by-side tests](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2018/08/24/ice-cream-two-ways): yolkless Philadelphia-style dairy mix, warmed to dissolve sugar; two custard versions differing in sugar and dairy blend. Firmness result does not isolate a single variable.
- [Herald, Aramouni & Abu-Ghoush, Journal of Texture Studies (2008)](https://www.ars.usda.gov/ARSUserFiles/30200510/2008%20-%20Comparison%20Study%20of%20Egg%20Yolks.pdf): methods specify liquid yolk control, three batches per treatment and fixed target overrun. Table 1: modified corn starch, whey protein concentrate and soy protein isolate alongside yolk control; some other amounts changed. Starch and whey had lower hour-end drip loss, while hardness and flavor ratings differed. No evidence all recipes behave the same or that drip loss directly measures ice left behind.
- [Orange corn and yolk pigmentation, Poultry Science (2021)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34102484/): white/yellow/orange corn diets, visual and instrument color evaluations and measured yolk pigments. Not an ice cream comparison or health claim.
- [21 CFR § 135.110, paragraphs (a)(2) and (f)(1)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-135/subpart-B/section-135.110), cross-checked against the [GPO print edition](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2025-title21-vol2/pdf/CFR-2025-title21-vol2-sec135-110.pdf) and [USDA’s 2026 companion standard](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2026-title7-vol3/xhtml/CFR-2026-title7-vol3-sec58-605.html): 1.4% yolk solids by weight for plain frozen custard, proportional adjustment for bulky flavors with 1.12% floor, permitted food names. Illustration 1.4% × 1,000 g = 14 g solids, not a recipe, local classification or allergen test.
