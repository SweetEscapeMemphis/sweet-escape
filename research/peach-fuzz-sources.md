# Peach fuzz — source and publication notes

Checked November 15, 2026. Canonical: https://www.sweetescapememphis.com/blog/why-peaches-have-fuzz.html

- [Fernández et al. 2011, Plant Physiology](https://academic.oup.com/plphys/article/156/4/2098/6108923): Calrico fruit microscopy, non-glandular single-cell hairs around 100–1,000 µm; removal of long hairs did not remove every hair. Table II: water contact angles 134.2° and 134.5°. Figure 7: reported water loss of 5%, 13% and 20%, respectively, after two days at 24°C and 40% relative humidity for intact, shaved and dewaxed peaches. No treatment procedure or extrapolation to home storage, handling or nectarines.
- [Vendramin et al. 2014, PLOS One](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0090574): Contender/Ambra cross, recessive smooth trait, insertion associated with PpeMYB25 and validation in a wider panel. [Yang et al. 2022, New Phytologist](https://nph.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/nph.17965): regulation of both wax and hair development; distinction between shaving grown fruit and fruit development.
- [UF/IFAS variety guide](https://ask.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/MG374): same species, flesh type, color and pit adhesion as separately noted variety traits.
- Williams Sonoma's [Peach Ice Cream](https://www.williams-sonoma.com/m/recipe/peach-ice-cream.amp.html) and [Fresh Peach Ice Cream](https://www.williams-sonoma.com/m/recipe/fresh-peach-ice-cream.html): first-party examples of peeled diced fruit, and of separately incorporated purée and chopped pieces. Examples only; no connection to Sweet Escape's formulation.
